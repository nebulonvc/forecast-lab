UK House of Commons Debate, Home and Overseas Trade (Hansard, 5 November 1946, HC Deb vol 428 cc1231-345): Labour supply is more difficult. After the war, we wisely abandoned the direction of labour, and that meant that we could not, except by persuasion, get people into the industries where they were most urgently needed. The Ministry of Labour and Service Departments effected demobilisation with precision, smoothness and fairness, and, similarly, the Supply Departments reduced the strength of the munitions industries with promptitude. As demobilisation progressed, more and more people entered civilian industries, but very seldom in the balanced numbers required. There has been, and still is, an acute shortage of women workers, although many more women are now employed. But that did not mean that those sections of industry most hardly pressed for production got their fair share.
