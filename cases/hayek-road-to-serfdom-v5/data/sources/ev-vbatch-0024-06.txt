Post World War II: 1946-1970 (Striking Women, University of Lincoln): The gains made during the Second World War proved transitory as women were demobilised from ‘men’s work’ to make way for the returning servicemen, as had happened following the First World War. However, unlike the 1920s, the late 1940s and 50s were periods of sustained economic growth. The post-war reconstruction effort made the need for an expanded labour force urgent. The welfare state created many job opportunities in what was seen as ‘women’s work’. Jobs were available in the newly created National Health Service for nurses, midwives, cleaners and clerical staff. Banking, textile and light industries such as electronics also expanded during this period and provided women with opportunities in clerical, secretarial and assembly work. Jobs were still strictly segregated by gender and routine repetitive work was paid less. Despite this increase in the rate of women’s employment, women were still considered to be 'secondary workers' and continued to be paid less than men even if they did the same work.
