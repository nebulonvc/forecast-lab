Office of Price Administration, Twentieth Quarterly Report for the Period Ended December 31, 1946 (U.S. Government Printing Office, Washington, transmitted April 15, 1947): Average weekly earnings in manufacturing had reached a peak in the first quarter of 1945. Except in nondurable goods, where reconversion was barely felt, the wage increases of 1945 and 1946 fell short of restoring dollar earnings to that peak. The lower level of income reflected the shorter hours and loss of premium overtime, the shift to lower paid occupations, and other reconversion factors. In durable goods, in fact, dollar earnings in the last quarter were back to the average of the first quarter of 1943. For manufacturing as a whole, weekly earnings were restored to the level of first quarter 1944, but this was true only in money terms. In terms of purchasing power, the rise of consumer prices had cut factory workers’ earnings nearly 20 percent below first quarter 1944.
