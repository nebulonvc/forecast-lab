Council on Foreign Relations — Global Inflation Tracker (based on IMF consumer price data for almost two hundred countries going back to 1990): Median global inflation fell from 2.2 percent to 1.9 percent that year. In 2021 and 2022, consumer demand recovered far more rapidly than supply, which was limited by labor shortages, supply-chain interruptions, and the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Between the third quarters of 2020 and 2022, median global inflation soared from 1.9 percent to 8.7 percent. This contrasts with the experience of the Great Recession, when median global inflation fell from 9.9 percent in the third quarter of 2008 to 1.7 percent in the third quarter of 2009. Median global inflation declined steadily following the pandemic, dropping to 2.6% in the second quarter of 2025. Over the last three decades, median food inflation has ranged from 1 percent to more than 13 percent.
