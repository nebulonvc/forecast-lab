Government finance statistics contain crucial indicators for determining the health of the economies of the EU countries. Under the terms of the EU's Stability and Growth Pact (SGP), EU countries pledged to keep their deficits and debt below certain limits: a Member State's government deficit may not exceed 3% of its gross domestic product (GDP), while its debt may not exceed 60% of GDP. If a Member State does not respect these limits, the so-called excessive deficit procedure (EDP) is triggered. The importance of the general government sector in the economy may be measured in terms of general government total revenue and total expenditure as a percentage of GDP. In 2025, government expenditure in the euro area was equivalent to 49.8% of GDP and government revenue to 46.8% of GDP. The figures for the EU were 49.4% and 46.3%, respectively. The government expenditure and revenue ratios increased in the EU and the euro area compared with 2024. In 2025, the EU countries with the highest levels of combined government expenditure and revenue as a proportion of GDP (in excess of 100%) were Finland, France, Austria and Belgium.
