Statement by the President Upon Terminating Price and Wage Controls (November 9, 1946, Harry S. Truman Library Public Papers): Accordingly, I have directed the immediate abandonment of all control over wages and salaries and all control over prices except that necessary to implement the rationing and allocation programs of sugar and rice. Rent control, however, must and will be continued. The major problem with which we have had to contend is the withholding of goods from the market. As price controls are dropped, one by one, many sellers naturally hold on to their goods in the hope that their turn will come next and that they can obtain a higher price. The plain truth is that, under this inadequate law, price control has lost the popular support needed to make it work. In the fifteen months since V-J Day the stabilization program has preserved a large measure of general economic stability during a period in which explosive forces would otherwise have produced economic disaster. Nevertheless, some shortages remain and some prices will advance sharply when controls are removed. The lifting of price controls and wage controls results in the return to a free market with free collective bargaining.
