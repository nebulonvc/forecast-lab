Supreme Soviet of the USSR, On the Annulment of Criminal Responsibility of Workers and Employees for Unauthorized Departure from Enterprises and Institutions and for Absence Without Valid Reasons. April 25, 1956 *A landmark labor decree that rolled back the Stalin-era system of binding workers to their enterprises under threat of criminal prosecution. The edict abolished criminal responsibility for “unauthorized departure” and for absenteeism, released and rehabilitated people convicted under those rules, and stopped pending cases. It replaced prison with workplace discipline: a two-week notice requirement for quitting, temporary loss of certain benefits, and penalties ranging from fines to dismissal, with the option to send cases to comradely courts."

Original Source: D. S. Karev (ed.), Sbornik zakonodatel'nykh aktov o trude (Moscow, 1956), p. 74.

As a result of the growth in the consciousness of the toilers and the rise in their material well-being and cultural level, there has been an improvement of discipline at enterprises and institutions.

In these circumstances the existing criminal responsibility of workers and employees for unauthorized departure from enterprises and institutions and for repeated or long-term absence without valid reason is not necessary, and can be replaced by measures of a disciplinary and social character.

The Presidium of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR decrees:

To annul the criminal responsibility of workers and employees for unauthorized departure from enterprises and institutions and for repeated or extended absence without valid reason.

To release from their sentences persons found guilty of unauthorized departure from enterprises and institutions and of absence without valid reason.

To halt proceedings in cases of unauthorized departure from enterprises and institutions and cases of absenteeism which have not been examined by courts up to the publication of this Edict.
