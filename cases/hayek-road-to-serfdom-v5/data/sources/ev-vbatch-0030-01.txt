Colonial Profits, Unequal Exchange and the Segmentation of the World Working Class (published 2026-03-05, DOI: 10.1163/24714607-bja10209). Abstract: To what extent did and do wage earners in the advanced capitalist countries (“the North”) benefit from the poor living and working conditions of producers and service providers in the poorer parts of the world (“the South”)? The article distinguishes three historical periods: the mercantilist period until the 1830s; the first international division of labor, 1830s–1940s; and the period of Fordism and post-Fordism, with a second international division of labor, from the 1940s. The analysis makes plausible that colonial pillage and unequal exchange have persisted over the years, and that the importance of unequal exchange has increased strongly after World War II. The next steps are a more thorough empirical reconstruction of this development; resulting from this an explanation of the prosperity differentials between different segments of the world’s working class; and an analysis of the ecological aspects of this issue.
