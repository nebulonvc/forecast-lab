The UNEP Regional Seas Programme is a global flagship programme implemented through regional frameworks for cooperation, management and protection of shared marine and coastal environments.

The ocean sustains life, supports livelihoods and economies, and connects people and places, today and for future generations. Securing these benefits depends on effective ocean governance, sound science and cooperation between countries. This means conserving marine and coastal ecosystems, using ocean resources sustainably and strengthening the health and resilience of the ocean. Because marine ecosystems and the processes that sustain them extend beyond national borders, countries need to work together, sharing knowledge, setting common priorities and taking coordinated action.

For more than 50 years, Regional Seas Programme has been an action-oriented programme that implements region-specific activities in support of this cooperation. It brings governments, scientific communities, civil society, Indigenous Peoples, local communities and young people together through a “shared seas” approach to the management and protection of marine and coastal environments.

The Programme covers 18 regions covering more than 146 countries. Most regions have a regional action plan backed by a legal framework: a regional convention, with protocols on specific issues. Together they are the Regional Seas Conventions and Action Plans (Regional Seas), which have developed more than 50 thematic protocols.
