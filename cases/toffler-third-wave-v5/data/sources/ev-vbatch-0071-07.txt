USDA Economic Research Service (ERS) — Ag and Food Statistics: Charting the Essentials - Food Prices and Spending (Updated: 11/17/2026). U.S. food spending by U.S. consumers, businesses, and Government entities reached $2.51 trillion in 2025 from an inflation-adjusted $1.56 trillion in 1997. This increase reflected higher inflation-adjusted spending on both food at home and food away from home. Food-away-from-home spending grew more than food-at-home spending, increasing to $1.41 trillion in 2025 from an inflation-adjusted $818 billion in 1997. Food-at-home expenditures increased to $1.10 trillion in 2025 from $738 billion over the same period. In 2025, U.S. consumers spent an average of 9.7 percent of disposable personal incomes on food, down from 10.4 percent in 1997. The decline was driven by a lower share spent on food at home, which was 4.8 percent in 2025, down from 6.1 percent in 1997. The share spent on food away from home was 4.9 percent in 2025, up from 4.3 percent in 1997.
