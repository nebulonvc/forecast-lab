Key milestones for young adults today versus 30 years ago, Pew Research Center, January 25, 2024. Among young adults ages 25 to 29 today, 40% have completed at least a bachelor’s degree. This share is slightly higher among adults ages 30 to 34 (44%). By comparison, only 24% of young adults ages 25 to 29 and 30 to 34 had a four-year college degree or more education in 1993. While there hasn’t been a significant change among young adults ages 18 to 24, the share employed full time among young adults ages 25 to 29 increased from 60% in 1993 to 65% today. Today’s young adults are getting married and forming families much later in life than young adults in previous generations. Among adults ages 18 to 24, only 7% were married in 2023, compared with 18% three decades earlier. The falloff has been especially sharp among young adults ages 25 to 29: 29% were married in 2023, compared with 50% in 1993. Young adults today are also less likely to have a child living in their household than their counterparts in 1993. These declines can be seen across all age groups (13 percentage points among those ages 30 to 34, 17 points for those ages 25 to 29, and 9 points among adults younger than 25).
