The food-service ecosystem is a core segment of China’s platform economy. © Crozet M. / ILO International Labour Review International Labour Review Volume 164 • Issue 4 • 2025 Download Content also available in: español français Xueyu Wang Center for Industrial Relations and Human Resources, University of Toronto The rise of platform-based work in China, engaging an estimated 84 million individuals, mirrors a global shift in how labour is organized and experienced. This digital transformation has sparked critical academic and policy dialogue regarding the adequacy of traditional labour law categories. Historically, legal frameworks have relied on a binary distinction between employees and independent contractors. However, the diverse reality of platform work—characterized by varying levels of algorithmic management and economic dependency—frequently blurs these boundaries, creating a "gray zone" that challenges conventional governance models. In recent years, the discourse in China has evolved toward addressing this complexity by focusing on the functional nature of the work relationship. Rather than strictly adhering to a binary threshold, there is an increasing move toward recognizing the nuances of workers who exhibit certain characteristics of subordination without necessarily fitting the classical mold of a full-time employee. The focus is shifting toward ensuring a baseline of essential protections that follows the worker, regardless of their specific classification, particularly for those subject to significant enterprise-level control during the labour process. Our research contributes an empirical foundation to this global dialogue by moving beyond conceptual debates over legal "tiers" to examine the actual working scenarios of platform participants. Utilizing a robust dataset of over 7,000 food-delivery riders across five diverse Chinese cities, we capture a broad spectrum of the platform economy, from first-tier digital hubs to industrial centers in transition.
