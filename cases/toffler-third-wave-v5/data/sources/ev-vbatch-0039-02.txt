Computer Use in the United States: 1984 (Current Population Reports, Special Studies Series P-23, No. 155, Issued March 1988). HIGHLIGHTS: In October 1984, 6,980,000, or 8.2 percent (±.6), of all U.S. households reported that they had a computer. Fifteen million adults (9.1 percent ± .2) had a computer at home, and of those about half (53.3 percent ± 1.0) used it. Of the over 100 million employed adults, 24,172,000, or 24.6 percent (±.3) used a computer at work. Within the occupational category of technical, sales, and administrative support (which includes secretaries and administrative clerical workers), computer use at work was reported by 32.7 percent of males, and 39.1 percent of females. In addition to the basic rates of use, we can also examine some of the purposes for which adults use their home computers (table 5). The most popular choice was "learning to use" (59 percent), an obvious choice given the newness of the technology to most individuals. More specific activities, such as household recordkeeping (40.2 percent), job-related activities (36.9), and word processing (32.9), were also chosen by substantial proportions of individuals.
