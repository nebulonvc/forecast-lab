In 2024, the total expenditure on social protection benefits in the EU amounted to €4 939 billion, or 27.4% of GDP.
In 2024, some 47% of total expenditure on social protection benefits in the EU was for the old age and survivors function, followed by the sickness/health care function with 30%.
During the period 2014-2024, in constant prices, EU expenditure on social protection benefits rose by 1.8% per year. The highest expansion was in the family/children function, followed by the sickness/health care function.
This article presents statistics relating to expenditure on social protection benefits in the European Union (EU) , the EFTA countries, as well as the enlargement countries. These statistics are collected through the European system of integrated social protection statistics (ESSPROS) .
In 2024, the total expenditure on social protection benefits in the EU amounted to €4 939 billion , which was equivalent to 27.4% of gross domestic product (GDP) . An analysis by function reveals that the highest level of expenditure in the EU was recorded for the old age and survivors function (largely composed of pensions), which accounted for close to half (47.0%) of the total expenditure on social protection benefits in 2024. The next highest share, accounting for slightly under a third (29.7%) of the EU's expenditure on social protection benefits, was for the sickness/health care function , while each of the remaining functions (shown in Figure 1) accounted for single-digit shares. Among these, the highest proportion was recorded for the family/children function (8.8% of total expenditure on social protection benefits), followed by disability (7.0%), unemployment (4.0%) and housing and social exclusion (3.6%).
Across the whole of the EU, the highest growth rate for expenditure on social protection benefits during the period 2014-2024 was recorded for family/children, where expenditure rose, on average, by 2.7% per year. The next highest rates of change were recorded for the sickness/healthcare function (2.3% per year) and for the housing and social exclusion function (1.9% per year). By contrast, the unemployment function showed a negative average growth rate (-1.9% per year). The largest increase in expenditure for family/children was registered by Poland (14.3% per year), while Cyprus recorded the highest growth for the sickness/health care function (13.5% per year) and Romania for the housing and social exclusion function (15.7% per year). Belgium, Romania and Cyprus showed the largest decrease for the unemployment function (-10.7%, -8.2% and -8.1% per year respectively).
