Datapoint Corporation History. Harold O'Kelley had positioned Datapoint for years of astonishing growth. Between 1973 and 1981, revenues grew at close to 40 percent a year, and sometimes more. Sales surpassed the $100 million mark in 1977, and by 1981 were almost $450 million. The poor business climate led Datapoint to lay off workers, further spooking Wall Street. Flagging customer confidence, shaken by Datapoint's drastic plunge in earnings, added to the company's difficulty in recovering from the crisis of 1982. Harold O'Kelley vowed to concentrate on selling Datapoint's minicomputers and to let the office of the future go.
