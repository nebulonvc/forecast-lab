When both partners work from home: the hidden cost of always-on technology. UNSW Newsroom, January 29, 2026. Work-from-home couples experience heightened frustration and relationship conflict when technology allows the intrusion of work into family time, according to new research from UNSW Business School. Furthermore, women bear a disproportionate psychological burden from these digital interruptions, though simple planning strategies can help reduce the negative impacts of work technology on home life for couples. Published in the Journal of the Association for Information Systems, the study is based on a 10-day diary study with 117 participants who lived with their partners while both worked from home full-time through the COVID-19 pandemic. When both partners worked from home simultaneously, neither could provide the buffering support typically available when only one partner worked remotely. Each partner faced their own technology intrusions while also managing their partner’s work demands, creating compound stress that intensified frustration levels. The research identified daily planning as a strategy associated with reduced frustration from technology-enabled work intrusions.
