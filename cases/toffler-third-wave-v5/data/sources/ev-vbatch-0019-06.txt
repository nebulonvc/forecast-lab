Inorganic fertilizers. 2015–2024 Share Close Highlights Inorganic fertilizers. 2015–2024 New FAOSTAT data release 30/07/2026 FAOSTAT has released updated statistics on the production, trade and agricultural use of inorganic fertilizers at the country, regional and global levels. This analytical brief focuses on the most recent decade (2015–2024) both globally and by region. At the country level it presents the leading users and producers and their position across nutrients, and it discusses fertilizer use normalized by cropland area and by the value of agricultural production. A dedicated section examines the international trade of fertilizers, including the bilateral corridors that link major exporters and importers. Main findings: Global agricultural use of inorganic fertilizers reached 197 million tonnes (Mt) of nutrients in 2024. After three decades of sustained growth, use has broadly plateaued over the past decade, rising just 7 percent from 185 Mt in 2015. The 2021–2022 energy and fertilizer price crisis interrupted the trend: world use fell 5.7 percent between 2021 and 2022 to 187 Mt, close to its 2015 level, before recovering to 197 Mt in 2024, still 2.1 percent below the 2020 peak of 202 Mt. Nutrient trends diverged over 2015–2024. Nitrogen use rose 8 percent to 115 Mt and potassium 12 percent to 38 Mt, while phosphorus use held broadly steady at 44 Mt. World production of inorganic fertilizers reached 216 Mt in 2024, up 3 percent since 2015. Nitrogen accounted for 57.7 percent of output, phosphorus 21.8 percent and potassium 20.5 percent. Regional paths diverged sharply. Use in Asia fell by 2 percent and in Europe by 4 percent over the decade coinciding with China's policies to rationalize fertilizer use while Africa (+40 percent), the Americas (+25 percent) and Oceania (+63 percent) expanded. Fertilizer use per hectare of cropland was broadly stable at 122 kilograms per hectare in 2024 (118 kg/ha in 2015), while use per value of agricultural production fell by 8 percent to 46 grams per international dollar, pointing to continued gains in the economic efficiency of fertilizer use.
