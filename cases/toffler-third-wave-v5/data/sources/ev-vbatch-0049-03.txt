This article presents data on how the number and composition of households have changed in the European Union (EU) and in the EU countries since 2016. Special attention is given to the presence of children in households and the working status of household members.
For the rest of households in the EU, the number remained almost unchanged or decreased between 2016 and 2025: couples with children decreased by 6.3% whereas households with 2 adults (not a couple) or more, also referred to as 'other type' of households in this article, fell by 0.8%. In this type of household, the decrease was higher in households with children (-3.5%) whereas households without children increased by 0.3%.
22.5% of the adult population live alone or are single parents. The proportion of single adults increases with age. 12.5% of young adults aged 18-24 live alone, but 32.2% of those aged 65 years or older were also single. For all age groups the proportion of both men and women living alone (with or without children) was higher in 2025 than in 2016. Also in 2025, the number of single adults aged 18-64 increased by 14.6%, while for people older than 64 the increase was 22.1%.
The household breakdown shows that the number of children varied considerably between countries (see Map 1). In the EU as a whole in 2025, 23.4% of households included children, whereas 76.6% did not, and around one third of households in Slovakia (35.4%) and Ireland (30.8%) had children. By contrast, less than one fifth of households in Finland (18.2%), Lithuania (18.4%) and Germany (19.9%) included children.
Couples accounted for 63.1% of households with children in the EU, making it the most common type of household with children. Sweden, Finland, the Netherlands and Slovenia had the highest shares of couples (all above 70%), among households with children. By contrast, Latvia, Estonia and Denmark had the lowest shares of couples (45.5%, 46.0% and 47.3% respectively) among households with children.
Other types of households accounted for less than 1 quarter (24.0%) of households with children in the EU. This share was more than 35% in Slovakia, Romania, Croatia and Spain, but less than 14% in Finland, Lithuania and Estonia.
Single parents accounted for 12.9% of households with children in the EU as a whole. Among households with children in the EU, the highest proportions of single parents were recorded in Estonia (40.6%), Lithuania (32.7%) and Latvia (28.5%). By contrast, Slovakia, Greece and Slovenia had the lowest shares (all below 5%).
