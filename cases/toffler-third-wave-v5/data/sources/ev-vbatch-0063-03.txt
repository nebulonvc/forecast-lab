Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, FEDS Notes (February 23, 2024), Fang Cai and Sharjil Haque, 'Private Credit: Characteristics and Risks'. Private credit or private debt investments are debt-like, non-publicly traded instruments provided by non-bank entities, such as private credit funds or business development companies (BDCs), to fund private businesses.2 Private credit is typically extended to middle-market firms with annual revenues between $10 million and $1 billion, but has grown rapidly in recent years to fund larger companies that were traditionally funded by leveraged loans. Figure 1 reports the growth of private credit since 2000, including all private credit strategies (left panel) and direct lending only (right panel). The left panel shows that total private credit has grown exponentially in recent years, reaching nearly $1.7 trillion, comparable to those of leveraged loans (roughly $1.4 trillion) and high-yield (HY) bond markets (about $1.3 trillion). The right panel shows that the growth of private credit is most pronounced for direct lending, which amounts to $800 billion, or about one half of the total.6 There is also growing amount of committed but uninvested capital (or 'dry powder') in the industry, suggesting supply of private credit funding is outstripping demand for private loans.
