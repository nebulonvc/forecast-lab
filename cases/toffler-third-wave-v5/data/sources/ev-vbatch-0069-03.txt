Are we working more than ever? By Charlie Giattino and Esteban Ortiz-Ospina, December 16, 2020. Summary: Working hours for the average worker have decreased dramatically over the last 150 years. This has come from working fewer hours each day, fewer days each week, and fewer weeks each year. While working hours have declined in many countries, particularly today’s richest ones, there are still large differences across and within countries. In recent decades working hours have continued to decline in many countries, but there are large differences between countries. For some countries, such as Germany, working hours have continued their steep historical decline; while for other countries, such as the US, the decline has leveled off in recent decades. In the companion Our World in Data annual-working-hours-per-worker dataset (1980-2023): Germany declined from 1756.09 hours in 1980 to 1335.27 in 2023; France from 1775.7 to 1487.1; United Kingdom from 1653.51 to 1522.8; Japan from 2120.53 to 1653.9; South Korea from 2860.94 to 1910.23; whereas United States only edged down from 1840.47 in 1980 to 1788.88 in 2023, and Sweden edged up from 1369.66 in 1980 to 1431.5 in 2023.
