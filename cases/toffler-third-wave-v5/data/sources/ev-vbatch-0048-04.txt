In almost all OECD countries, there has been a trend-decline in marriage rates over the past decades (Chart SF3.1.A). In 1970, most OECD countries had CMRs of somewhere between 7 and 10 marriages per 1000 people. By 1995, in most OECD countries, CMRs had fallen to around 5 to 7 marriages per 1000 people, and in many countries, they have continued to fall since. In most OECD countries, the CMR is somewhere between 3 and 5 marriages per 1000, with the OECD average standing at 4.3. In the past decades, declining rates of marriage have been accompanied by increases in rates of divorce (Chart SF3.1.C). In comparison to 1970 for example, current CDRs in most OECD countries are generally high. All except seven OECD countries with available data (Canada, Czechia, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Slovenia and the United States) have CDRs that are higher now than they were in 1970. Despite long-run increases in rates of divorce, most of the people getting married are still getting married for the first time. Chart SF1.3.D shows that for 2021 and on average across OECD countries, four in every five people getting married had not been married before. In all OECD countries it is possible to divorce, but there is considerable cross-country variation in the length and nature of the divorce process.
