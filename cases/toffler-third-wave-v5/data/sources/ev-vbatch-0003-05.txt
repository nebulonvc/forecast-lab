This fact sheet describes how the EU’s digital agenda has evolved and how, since 2024, the EU has shifted the focus from agenda-setting to implementation by introducing legal frameworks such as the AI Act. It also explains how the EU’s new rules on platforms, data, digital identity, AI and cybersecurity are driving secure digital spaces, fair competition and digital sovereignty in line with the green transition. Legal basis While the Treaties do not contain specific provisions on information and communication technologies (ICTs), on the basis of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), the EU can act within policy areas such as: industry, competition, trade, trans-European networks, research, energy, the establishment of the single market, the free movement of goods, the movement of people and services, education and culture. Objectives Through its digital agenda, the EU aims to enable a human-centred, secure and sustainable digital transformation. Building on the digital single market strategy and the 2030 Digital Compass , the digital agenda promotes trusted data flows, interoperable digital identity, fair online platform markets, responsible AI and strong cybersecurity. The Digital Decade Policy Programme turns these objectives into targets for skills, infrastructure, business digitalisation and public services. Achievements A. The first digital agenda for Europe: 2010-2020 The first digital agenda focused on improving connectivity, reducing costs and strengthening consumer protection. Its main achievements included: Eliminating roaming charges (‘ roam like at home’ ); Expanding broadband access; Introducing key data protection rules, notably the General Data Protection Regulation . --> It also promoted digital growth through support for enhancing skills, industry digitisation and public services, as well as introducing rules on geo-blocking and cross-border portability of online content services . To support a data-driven economy, the EU adopted measures on the free flow of non-personal data, cybersecurity and open data, and strengthened regulatory coordination through the Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications.
