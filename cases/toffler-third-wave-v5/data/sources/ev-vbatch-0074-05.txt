Updating the 2008 SNA • Macroeconomic Accounting Standards are regularly updated to reflect changes in the economy. • The “updates” can be categorized into three main groups: • Conceptual Updates (i.e., change in what is measured) • Methodological Updates (i.e., change in how activities are measured) • Presentational Updates (i.e., change in how the statistics are presented) • A lot has changed since the latest version: • Increasing globalisation which causes measurement issues that need to be addressed • Increasing digitalisation which may not be visible and fully captured in the accounts • Increasing demand for data on well-being and sustainability in addition to traditional economic measures such as GDP and Household Disposable Income • … • For this reason, an update process was launched in 2020 1953 SNA 1968 SNA 1993 SNA 2008 SNA

Key features • Adopted definitions • 3rd party criterion from the UNECE 2017 guide to valuing unpaid work • Volunteering vs Own use unpaid household services – ILO and the 19th ICLS Conference • Build on sub-categories of unpaid household work to meet current and future user needs • Traditional activities • Breaking down volunteering • Digital DIY • New guidance on secondary/simultaneous activities captured in time-use data • Recommendations related to the use of specialist wage rates and valuation processes

In summary… • The ISWGNA guidance includes detailed recommendations on the inclusion of unpaid household service work in macroeconomic statistics • The main recommendations will feed into the new SNA, although in a condensed form, focusing on the main conceptual issues • Countries will be encouraged to compile the results in the form of extended accounts • However, neither the SNA or the guidance note have any mandated requirements, they simply set out a conceptual framework (SNA manual) and make some recommendations (ISWGNA note) of how to create these extended accounts • International organisations may see if they will encourage regular compilation of these results and/or make it part of their regular data collection • As a guiding principle, the addition of unpaid household service work should enable: • The SNA GVA estimates to be comprehensive (showing both core and general production in separate estimates) • The SNA 2025 to capture unpaid household service work as completely as possible • Flows of economic activity to be readily tracked moving across the core production boundary

State of play • Global consultations have been conducted for all guidance notes, including on this one, obtaining feedback from compilers and users all around the world • Testing has been conducted for some topics, including this one, to assess feasibility of the proposals • Implementation Task Teams have been established for some specific topics to develop more practical guidance, i.e., data, marketing assets and natural capital • In the coming years, expert groups may be foreseen to assist countries in compilation of results in specific areas • Editors have started to draft the updated version of the SNA to reflect the recommendations • Rounds of global consultation on the new chapters 2 and 34 have been completed • The new SNA should become available in 2025 • Subsequently, countries will start to adopt this in their statistics, probably around 2028-2030
