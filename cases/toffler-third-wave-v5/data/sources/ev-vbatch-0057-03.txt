Over a quarter (27.6%) of all U.S. occupied households were one-person households in 2020, up from just 7.7% in 1940, according to recently released 2020 Census data. The share of people living alone increased every decade from 1940 to 2020 (Figure 1). The largest increase happened between 1970 and 1980, when the share increased from 17.6% to 22.7%. The overall share of people living alone inched up (from 26.7% to 27.6%) between 2010 and 2020. So did the share (from 9.4% to 11.1%) of one-person households headed by adults ages 65 and over. But the share of one-person households among people ages 15 to 64 declined from 17.3% to 16.5% during the same period.
