COVID-19 leads to changes in paper and paperboard production (Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations). With schools and offices shut and with work and educational activities moving online, there was a sharp decrease in the manufacture of graphic papers during 2020, the data show. Although the production of printing paper, writing paper and newsprint have been declining every year by 2-3 percent since 2007, last year when the pandemic hit, production plummeted by 11.4 percent. This brought production down from its peak of 153.7 million tonnes thirteen years ago to under 100 million tonnes today. "The likely cause for this decrease in printing and writing paper manufacturing is an increase in people meeting virtually and consuming news digitally," said Sven Walter, FAO Senior Forestry Officer. Conversely, 2020 saw a surge in the production of packaging paper and paperboard and of household and sanitary papers.
