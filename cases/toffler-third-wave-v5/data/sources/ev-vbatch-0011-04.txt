The Death of Synfuels. Hans H. Landsberg. Date Jan. 1, 1986. Published by Resources for the Future. Reeling from the second oil price shock in half a dozen years, Congress bought national energy insurance by establishing the Synthetic Fuels Corporation (SFC) via the Energy Security Act of 1980. The program was to terminate on September 30, 1997, or earlier by executive order of the president, but "in no event prior to September 1992." "In no event" notwithstanding, the corporation's short and unhappy childhood will end before it sees its sixth birthday. President Reagan in December signed a bill that kills the corporation as of mid-April 1986. By 1987 production was to hit no less than 500,000 barrels per day (b/d) in equivalent, and by 1992 the target was to climb to 2 million b/d. Moreover, if there was to be a production target, half a million barrels within seven years was wholly unrealistic. Consider that a single 50,000 b/d plant would cost $3.5 billion to $4.5 billion (not one has been built).
