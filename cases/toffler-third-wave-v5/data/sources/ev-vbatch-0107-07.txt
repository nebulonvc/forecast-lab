Since 1980, the growth of government spending has been slowing down in early-industrialized countries — and in some cases, it has gone down in relative terms. However, in spite of differences in levels, in all these countries public spending as a share of GDP is higher today than before the Second World War. At the end of the 19th century, governments in European countries spent around 10% of their GDP. In the 21st century, this figure is closer to 50% in many European countries. While the relative ranking of these priorities has remained largely unchanged since 1980, most countries now allocate a larger share of their GDP to each.
