Pew Research Center Report (January 25, 2024; updated April 1, 2025): Parents, Young Adult Children and the Transition to Adulthood - 1. Key milestones for young adults today versus 30 years ago. Using 1993 and 2023 Current Population Survey (CPS) data: Young adults today are much more likely than their counterparts 30 years ago to be college graduates. Among young adults ages 25 to 29 today, 40% have completed at least a bachelor’s degree. This share is slightly higher among adults ages 30 to 34 (44%). By comparison, only 24% of young adults ages 25 to 29 and 30 to 34 had a four-year college degree or more education in 1993. Compared with their parent’s generation, young adults today are more likely to work full time. While there hasn’t been a significant change among young adults ages 18 to 24, the share employed full time among young adults ages 25 to 29 increased from 60% in 1993 to 65% today. Similarly, among those ages 30 to 34, it rose from 62% to 67%. Today’s young adults are getting married and forming families much later in life than young adults in previous generations. Among adults ages 18 to 24, only 7% were married in 2023, compared with 18% three decades earlier. The falloff has been especially sharp among young adults ages 25 to 29: 29% were married in 2023, compared with 50% in 1993. Meanwhile, living with parents has become somewhat more common for young adults. Among those ages 18 to 24, 57% are living with a parent today, compared with 53% in 1993.
