U.S.-South Korea Alliance: Background and Issues for Congress, Updated March 16, 2026 (R48877). Under the Mutual Defense Treaty that took effect in 1954, the United States and South Korea committed to defend each other against armed attack. Today, about 28,500 U.S. troops are stationed in South Korea. The alliance has traditionally focused on perceived threats from North Korea (officially the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, or DPRK), which have changed as North Korea has developed weapons of mass destruction (WMD) and missile capabilities. South Korea is included under the U.S. "nuclear umbrella." U.S. Forces Korea (USFK) comprises predominately Army personnel operating some of the U.S. military's most advanced combat capabilities. Most U.S. troops in South Korea are assigned to Camp Humphreys, which underwent a major expansion in the 2010s and is the largest U.S. overseas military base in terms of land area. To prepare their forces to, if necessary, "fight tonight"—a USFK motto—the United States and South Korea have conducted combined and joint training and exercises, such as Freedom Shield.
