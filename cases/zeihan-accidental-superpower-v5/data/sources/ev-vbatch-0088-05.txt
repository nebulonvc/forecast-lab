2025 Overview Foreign direct investment in the United States, known as FDIUS, reached $5.7 trillion cumulatively at the end of 2024 on a historical-cost basis. Every year, international firms make new investments, which benefit the American economy in numerous ways. They build new factories, fund U.S.based research and development (R&D), and grow their well-established U.S. operations International companies employ 8.4 million Americans in well-paying jobs. Investment flows to the United States from overseas companies totaled $292 billion in 2024, down slightly from 2023. Overall, 2024 FDIUS flows were a little less than average for the past decade. During the COVID-19 pandemic, FDIUS flows dropped to $112 billion in 2020, the lowest level of the past decade. Inflows then rebounded to more than $400 billion in 2021. International companies invested record highs of $484 billion and $480 billion in 2015 and 2016, respectively. Japan, Canada, and Germany held the top three investment positions in the United States in 2024. But until 2018, the United Kingdom had been the largest cumulative overseas investor in the United States. With divestments, Britain dropped to fourth. Still, these four countries provided more than half of all FDIUS through 2024. Manufacturing accounted for 42 percent of total FDIUS through 2024. Chemicals made up more than a third of this investment. Finance and insurance, wholesale trade, and information rounded out the top four industries by FDIUS at the end of last year. After accounting for European ‘conduit’ countries (Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom, which often act as intermediaries for FDI destined for other countries), FDI flows fell 11 percent in 2024, according to the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD) World Investment Report 2025 (WIR25). On future FDI growth, UNCTAD notes, “trade tensions have led to downward revisions of most indicators of FDI prospects.” The United States was the world’s top destination for FDI flows in 2024; and cumulatively, it remains the world’s prime location for international investment. The United States’ share of global investment has rebounded recently to almost 31 percent in 2024, compared with 21 percent in 2014. Whether the United States will retain its status as the world’s most attractive investment location hinges on macroeconomic policy decisions, both in the United States and abroad. But for the 13th consecutive year, A.T. Kearney ranked the United States as number one in its 2025 Foreign Direct Investment Confidence Index, followed by Canada and the United Kingdom. About the Data This report provides the most recent data on foreign direct investment in the United States at the time of publication in August 2025. FDIUS (inward investment) is defined as the ownership or control, directly or indirectly, by one foreign entity of a 10 percent or more ownership share of an incorporated or unincorporated U.S. business enterprise. The foreign direct investment figures are shown on a historical-cost basis.
