Centre for Applied Turkey Studies (CATS). Visualising the Dynamics and Potential of Turkey’s Economic Relations with Germany and the EU (Update), by Yaşar Aydın and Marcela Müggler. It incorporates new data and developments up to November 2026, reflecting recent changes in bilateral economic relations.
Over the past two decades, the regional composition of Turkey’s foreign trade has shifted significantly: the EU’s share of Turkey’s total trade declined from 46 per cent in 2002 to 36 per cent in 2025, while the share of the BRICS countries increased from 9 per cent to 18 per cent over the same period (see Fig. 7).
In 2025, Turkey's goods trade with the EU 27 totalled US$ 232.5 billion, accounting for approximately 36 per cent of Turkey's overall goods trade of US$ 638.6 billion. The EU is Turkey’s single-largest trade partner in goods: in 2025, approximately 43 per cent of Turkish exports went to the EU, while around 35 per cent of Turkish imports originated from the EU.
As of 2025, Germany accounts for 8.1 per cent of Turkey’s total exports (Fig. 13a) and is the country’s largest export partner. The United Kingdom and the US follow in second and third place, with 6.1 per cent and 6.0 per cent, respectively.
