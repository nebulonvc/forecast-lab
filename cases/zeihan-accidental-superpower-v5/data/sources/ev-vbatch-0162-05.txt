A new study from the Fraser Institute says Albertans' net contributions to federal finances have helped offset federal spending costs for taxpayers across Canada, saving residents in other provinces an average of about $1,000 annually.
The report
, released by the independent public policy think tank, examines the difference between the amount of federal revenue generated in each province and the amount returned through federal spending and transfers.
According to the study, Alberta's net contribution to federal finances between 2007-08 and the projected 2026-27 fiscal year is expected to total $321.9 billion.
That figure is nearly four times the net contribution attributed to British Columbia at $87.8 billion and more than five times Ontario's net contribution of $59.6 billion over the same period.

The study found Alberta consistently contributes more money to Ottawa than it receives back in federal spending and transfers.
By contrast,
the report
says the other seven provinces were net recipients during the period studied, meaning they received more in federal expenditures and transfers than they contributed in federal revenues.
Fraser Institute researchers argue Alberta's contribution plays a significant role in helping Ottawa maintain current spending levels across the country.
"Many Canadians don't realize what a sizeable contribution Albertans make to federal finances and how taxpayers in other provinces benefit from it," said Tegan Hill, director of Alberta policy at the Fraser Institute and co-author of the report.
Study estimates impact on taxpayers
Researchers estimate that without Alberta's net fiscal contribution, maintaining existing federal spending levels would require taxpayers in other provinces to contribute an additional $1,007 per year on average through higher income taxes.
