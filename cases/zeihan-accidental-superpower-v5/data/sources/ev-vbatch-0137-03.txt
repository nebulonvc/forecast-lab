IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) collected data on Ukrainian nationals crossing back into Ukraine from Poland for temporary stays, permanent returns, or as part of cyclical mobility. The survey focuses on respondents' return intentions, duration of displacement, destinations, assistance needs, and experiences of discrimination. A total of 566 surveys were conducted between 1 April and 30 June 2026, all completed by Ukrainian nationals. Consequently, the analysis presented in this report is based solely on their responses. Key findings: Crossing Patterns: Cross-border movements into Ukraine remained predominantly temporary, with 95% of respondents undertaking pendular movements or short-term visits rather than permanent returns. Current Ukrainian residents accounted for 60% of all respondents, while 35% were short-term visitors. Only 3% intended to remain in Ukraine, and 2% were undecided. Countries of Stay (excluding current Ukrainian residents): More than half of respondents returning to Ukraine had been displaced in Poland (55%). Other main countries of stay included Germany (14%), the United Kingdom (7%), Czechia (6%), and Austria (3%). Destinations and Return Intentions: Most respondents maintained strong ties to their places of origin. Among Ukrainians returning from abroad, 69% intended to stay in their own homes, while 85% planned to return to their oblast of origin. Dnipropetrovska oblast was the most frequently reported destination (13%), followed by Lvivska, Kyiv, and Odeska oblasts (12% each). Reasons for Returning: Family reunification remained the primary reason for crossing back to Ukraine, cited by 69% of respondents returning from abroad. Other common reasons included access to health care (40%) and feelings of homesickness (35%). Needs: Health services were the most commonly reported immediate need among respondents returning from abroad (31%), while 30 per cent reported no immediate needs. Health-related needs increased compared to the previous reporting period.
