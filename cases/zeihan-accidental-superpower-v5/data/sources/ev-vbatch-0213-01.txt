FAO GIEWS Country Brief on Bolivia (Reference Date: 09-September-2026). A persistent shortage of diesel is hampering the ongoing harvesting operations of the 2026 winter crops by constraining the use of tractors and other agricultural machinery, thus reducing yield prospects. The fuel shortage largely reflects the country’s limited foreign exchange reserves, which continue to constrain import capacity and drive up agricultural production costs. Lack of fuel and rising costs of agricultural inputs at the beginning of the season in May 2026 limited planting intentions, particularly among smallholder farmers, and output is expected to be below average. Nevertheless, aggregate maize production in 2026 is anticipated at an above-average level, reflecting an expansion of the area planted to the main season maize crop in response to higher year-on-year prices at the end of 2025. The area sown with sorghum, mostly concentrated in eastern Santa Cruz department, is estimated at an above-average level, supported by robust demand from the domestic feed industry. Imports of cereals in 2025/26 marketing year (July/June) are estimated around 441 000 tonnes, almost 65 percent above the five-year average level. The devaluation of the Boliviano following the adoption of a more flexible exchange-rate regime in June 2026 increased the cost of imported agricultural inputs, limiting domestic production prospects and further boosting import demand. Market availability was further constrained by roadblocks along major roads and the declaration of the state of exception which limited producers’ access to markets. Prices of wheat flour remained stable in May 2026, ahead of the start of the main season harvest in July and were up to 30 percent below their level of June 2025, mainly driven by large import amounts.
