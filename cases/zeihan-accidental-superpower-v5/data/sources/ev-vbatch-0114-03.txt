Kyrgyzstan’s state weather agency, Kyrgyzhydromet, has warned that last month’s record-breaking heatwave is the latest sign of climate change accelerating the retreat of the country’s glaciers, threatening water supplies across Central Asia. According to Kyrgyzhydromet, Kyrgyzstan’s glaciers have lost around 16% of their total area over the past 70 years. Recent monitoring places the decline in the Talas Valley at 22%. Smaller, lower-altitude glaciers are particularly vulnerable, and scientists expect their number and area to decline substantially by 2050. Separately, Kyrgyzhydromet reported on July 30 that glacier area in the Issyk-Kul basin had declined by 33.7% over 70 to 90 years and by 23.1% over the most recent seven to ten years. The consequences extend beyond Kyrgyzstan. Tien Shan glaciers feed the Syr Darya, Chu, Talas, and Ili river systems, supplying water for millions of people, irrigation, and hydropower. Continued glacier retreat could alter seasonal flows and increase the risk of glacial lake outburst floods threatening mountain communities.
