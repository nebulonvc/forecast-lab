Statement by the UNFPA Representative in Ukraine on World Population Day Today, on World Population Day , we are reminded that behind every statistic lies a human story — of aspiration, resilience, and, too often, dreams deferred or unrealized. A new global report from UNFPA, The State of World Population 2025 , reveals that people across diverse contexts still desire families — often two or more children — but are blocked by economic insecurity, housing instability, gender inequality, lack of child care, and uncertainty about the future. Ukraine faces a stark version of this global challenge, magnified by over a decade of war and three years of the full-scale invasion. In 2024, just 176,700 children were born — a 35% drop compared to 2021, according to the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine. The fertility rate is projected to fall to just 0.9 children per woman, far below the replacement level of 2.1, and life expectancy has plummeted — particularly for men, who now live on average only 57.3 years.
