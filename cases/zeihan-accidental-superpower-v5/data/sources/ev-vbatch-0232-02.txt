For decades, some foreign students who graduate from an American university have been able to remain in the United States for at least one year to pursue employment through Optional Practical Training (OPT) in their field of study. Graduates of certain STEM programs are permitted to remain for an additional 24 months, allowing them to work for up to three years post-graduation. This program has fueled a rise in foreign student admissions and become a vital pipeline for top students seeking to eventually immigrate to the United States permanently. But now the fate of the program is in limbo, as the Trump administration is proposing to levy exorbitant new fees on universities for each student seeking OPT; $70,000 for the first year of a student’s participation and $30,000 for the additional 24-month extension for STEM OPT. The fees would only apply to students who have not received an OPT recommendation from their Designated School Official when the rule goes into effect; any students currently utilizing OPT or who have applications pending would not be impacted. The Notice of Proposed Rulemaking published in the Federal Register on October 8 claims that imposing these high fees is necessary to “combat fraud and abuse” in the program, although the government admits the fees would not be used to increase fraud detection and would instead be deposited as general funds in the U.S. Treasury. How the Proposed Fees Could Affect Universities and Tuition In addition, the Trump administration says the fees are necessary because STEM OPT applicants are competing with American tech workers, framing their argument as a fight against “large-scale replacement of American workers.” DHS is clear that one main purpose of the fees is to “discourage aliens from using the OPT program.”
