South Africa is the second-largest economy in Africa, with one of the continent’s most diversified production bases, spanning mining, agriculture, manufacturing, and services, including a well-developed and deep financial sector and world class tourism. It is the region’s leading transport and logistics hub, a major outward investor, and home to some of Africa’s largest firms. Economic growth increased from 0.5% in 2024 to 1.1% in 2025, as structural reforms began to yield results. Performance at key state-owned enterprises is improving, with a substantial reduction in load-shedding, increased rail freight, and more efficient port operations. After expanding by 0.5% in Q1 of 2026, the economy contracted by 0.2% in Q2 as higher oil prices and weaker demand weighed on activity. Economic growth has been insufficient to create enough jobs or significantly reduce poverty. Close to 60% of South Africans live below the upper-middle-income poverty line. The unemployment rate rose to 33.6% in Q2 of 2026, leaving 8.5 million people unemployed. The expanded unemployment rate, which includes discouraged job seekers, reached 44%.
