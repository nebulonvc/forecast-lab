Georgia has made strong progress in reducing poverty over the past decade, supported by sustained growth and rising wages. The share of the population living below the upper-middle-income poverty line ($8.30 a day, 2021 PPP) fell to an estimated 43.3% in 2025, from 45.6% in 2024. It is projected to decline further, to 34.3% by 2028.

Since 1992, the World Bank Group has been a pillar of support for Georgia, contributing to the development, growth, and modernization of the country, leading to notable enhancements in citizens’ quality of life.

The institution’sactive portfolio in Georgia comprises ten projects totaling $1.3 billion, a significant expansion that reflects growing ambition and client demand. The World Bank Group's engagement is organized around four pillars: (1) regional connectivity, (2) human capital, (3) jobs and productivity, and (4) resilience, with digital development as a cross-cutting theme. Human capital remains the largest share, focused on improving education quality and strengthening health and social protection systems.

Transport and connectivity have grown substantially following the June 2026 Board approval of the $752 million Trans-Caspian Transport Corridor – Georgia Accessibility and Transport Enhancement Project (TC-GATE) , co-financed by the Asian Development Bank ($175 million) and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank ($182 million), reinforcing Georgia's role as a Middle Corridor transit hub linking Europe and Central Asia. TC-GATE anchors the connectivity pillar through a One World Bank Group approach that sequences public investment and institutional reform to mobilize private capital, including a potential IFC engagement through capital markets instruments and MIGA political risk coverage.

The Georgia Bank Resolution Readiness Project approved in July 2026, anchors the resilience pillar by building the institutional framework for an effective bank resolution regime, strengthening the country’s financial sector's capacity to manage and resolve bank failures, a critical foundation for macroeconomic stability and long-term growth. The Bank’s portfolio also includes energy investments in transmission, generation capacity, and the Black Sea Submarine Cable; private sector development through MSME support; agriculture and rural resilience; and digital infrastructure.
