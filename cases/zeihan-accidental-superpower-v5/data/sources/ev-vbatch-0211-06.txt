Trade policy uncertainty reached historic highs in 2025 and remained elevated through the first half of 2026, with media coverage increasingly focused on tariffs, shipping disruptions, and changing trade rules. Yet global trade continued to expand during this period, with little evidence of a widespread deterioration in trade flows. 2 This apparent disconnect motivates three questions: Why has global trade remained resilient despite historically high trade policy uncertainty? When does trade policy uncertainty reduce trade? What adjustments have helped some countries limit large trade disruptions despite elevated trade policy uncertainty? 
A key part of answering these questions is distinguishing between two ways of measuring trade uncertainty. We consider a news-based measure of trade policy uncertainty (TNU), which counts trade-related terms in country reports, and a data-based measure of trade flow uncertainty (TFU), which captures how difficult underlying trade flows are to predict. Our main finding is that TNU is not redundant once TFU is accounted for: higher TNU is associated with weaker trade flows primarily when TFU is already elevated. We argue that TFU is useful as a state variable because it indicates how predictable underlying trade outcomes are, regardless of how extensively trade uncertainty is covered in the news. While elevated TNU became widespread in 2025, elevated TFU remained confined to relatively few countries. We show that countries with relatively low TFU appear to have adapted to rising trade policy uncertainty by shifting trade routes or front-loading shipments before trade disruptions occurred.
