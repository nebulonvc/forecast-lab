Download PDF Share on LinkedIn Share on X Share on Facebook Email In response to the global financial crisis that began in 2008, China unleashed a debt-fueled stimulus to maintain growth and employment. Although the global financial crisis has come and gone, debt-fueled growth has not. One measure of credit registered China’s debt-to-gross domestic product (GDP) ratio at 315 percent in June 2025. Much of this debt is tied to local governments, which manage about 85 percent of government spending but face a persistent mismatch between their spending responsibilities and revenue. That mismatch has worsened as land sales—one of their largest locally generated revenue sources—have sharply declined because of a downturn in the property sector. The authors of this report examine the profile, scale, and consequences of China’s local government debt. They estimate that local government debt reached at least $12 trillion in 2024, or 64 percent of China’s GDP, including more than $5 trillion in off-balance-sheet borrowing. They also investigate recent debt-resolution measures and argue that these steps can buy time but do not resolve the underlying fiscal imbalances. The authors assess the implications of this debt for China’s economy, public services, and political stability, and consider what it means for competition between the United States and China. Key Takeaways China’s local government debt problem is a central concern among the country’s ongoing fiscal challenges and reflects a structural imbalance. Local governments manage about 85 percent of government spending, but one sizable revenue source—land sales—has sharply declined because of a downturn in the property sector. Using data from the Wind Financial Terminal, the authors estimate that China’s total local government debt was at least $12 trillion in 2024—64 percent of China’s GDP. This total includes off-balance-sheet debts, which were valued at more than $5 trillion—if not more—as of December 2024, that had been accumulated through local government financing vehicles (LGFVs).
