WASHINGTON — The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) today announced preliminary Fiscal Year (FY) 2026 data showcasing a 95% drop in southwest border apprehensions compared to the Biden Administration, alongside the lowest annual encounters in American history and the first-ever fiscal year with zero Border Patrol releases . Driven by President Donald J. Trump’s policies and the leadership of DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, FY 2026 combined historic low crossings with strengthened border infrastructure, disrupted cartels, and protected American trade . September marked the 17th consecutive month with ZERO releases. CBP’s enforcement posture drove U.S. Border Patrol apprehensions to their lowest level in agency history. The Border Patrol, which completed its first-ever fiscal year with no releases, recorded less than 99,000 southwest border apprehensions in FY 2026—a 95% decline compared with more than 1.8 million per year under President Biden. Additionally, preliminary data shows total nationwide encounters in FY 2026 were the lowest in agency history at 379,243, compared with 2.7 million per year under Biden (seven times higher). FY 2026 encounters were 88% lower than FY 2023 under Biden, which was the highest fiscal year ever.
