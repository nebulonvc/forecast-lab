On October 27, the Constitutional Council officially declared President Paul Biya the winner of the October 12, 2025, presidential election, securing 53.66% of the vote. This is Paul Biya’s eighth term.
Legislative and municipal elections are planned for 2026. A Presidential decree had extended the mandate for current municipal councilors for a year from its expiry date of Feb. 25, 2025, to May 31, 2026. And the mandate of parliamentarians had been extended for a year from 10 March 2025 to 30 March 2026.
Having enjoyed several decades of stability, Cameroon has in recent years been grappling with attacks by Boko Haram in the Far North and a secessionist insurgency in the Anglophone regions. Since September 2017, this situation has displaced more than one million people internally and around 431,530 refugees have sought shelter in Cameroon. Following the resurgence of the crisis in the Central African Republic since January 2021, over 6,000 Central Africans refugees fled to Cameroon’s East region which was already hosting over 67 % of Central African refugees. According to the UNHCR, as of August 31, 2026, Cameroon is hosting over 414,573 refugees, primarily from the Central African Republic (67%) and Nigeria (29%) and other nationalities (4%).
Poverty reduction in Cameroon has stagnated over the past 20 years, with approximately 4 in 10 Cameroonians living below the national poverty line. The household survey data from 2021-2022 suggests that 23.0% of the population lives below the extreme international poverty line.

Services and manufacturing supported activity, while declining hydrocarbon production, weak exports, infrastructure gaps, poor governance, low investment, and internal conflicts constrained growth. Labor has continued shifting from agriculture to low-productivity urban services, making services the main growth driver since the 1993 devaluation. Growth is projected to average 3.7% in 2026–28, supported by improved energy supply and private investment. The current account deficit widened from 3.4% of GDP in 2024 to 4.2% in 2025, due to Lower oil production and prices, weaker log exports, and higher imports despite strong cocoa exports. The current account deficit is projected to remain near 4.5% of GDP as non-hydrocarbon exports weaken and imports remain high.
The fiscal deficit rose from 1.4% of GDP in 2024 to 2.2% in 2025 as oil revenues declined and election-related recurrent spending increased. The deficit should average about 1.8% of GDP over the medium term, although tight regional liquidity and costly external borrowing pose risks.
Cameroon’s public and publicly guaranteed debt declined to an estimated 42.9% of GDP in March 2026 from 43.3% a year earlier. Cameroon remains at high risk of debt distress, although debt is considered sustainable and debt-carrying capacity has been upgraded to medium. Prudent fiscal policy and resilient growth support a declining debt ratio, but high debt-service costs, weaker exports, and a narrow export base create significant liquidity risks.
