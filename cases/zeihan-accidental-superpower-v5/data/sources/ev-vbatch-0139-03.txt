NextGenerationEU is a groundbreaking temporary instrument to support Europe’s economic recovery from the coronavirus pandemic and build a greener, more digital and more resilient future. Based on Member State requests for funding under the Recovery and Resilience Facility and the funding needs of other EU programmes supported by NextGenerationEU, the EU expects to raise up to €634 billion by end 2026 (out of a maximum programme envelope of €806.9 billion, as set in 2021). Link to policy brief Use of NextGenerationEU proceeds The centrepiece of the NextGenerationEU is the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) - an instrument that offers grants and loans to support reforms and investments in the EU Member States. RRF funds are being provided to Member States in line with their national Recovery and Resilience plans – the roadmaps to reforms and investments aimed to make EU economies greener, digital and more resilient. The financial envelope under the RRF at the end of August 2026, consist of: up to €360 billion of grants, out of which €338 billion will be financed through borrowing operations (Member States will also receive additional RRF grants of €20.0 billion financed under the Emissions Trading System (ETS) and €2 billion under the Brexit Adjustment Reserve (BAR); up to €213 billion of loans (out of the initial total available RRF loan envelope of up to of (€385 billion).
