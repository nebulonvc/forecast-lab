CRSS Security Report | Q3 2026 Q3, 2026 Security Landscape Pakistan recorded a sharp increase in violence-linked fatalities in Q3 2026, reversing the declining trend reported in Q1 and Q2, with terrorism and counter-terrorism linked deaths surging by 83% compared with Q2. Driving this reversal, with its death toll more than tripled, Balochistan accounted for nearly 88% of the national increase and overtook Khyber Pakhtunkhwa as the worst-hit province in both fatalities and recorded incidents. Outlaws’ fatalities more than doubled and accounted for nearly three-quarters of the additional deaths nationwide. However, the combined death toll among civilians and security personnel also rose by nearly 54%, indicating that the increased losses extended across all three groups, tallied the Center for Research and Security Studies (CRSS).
During the third quarter of 2026, the Center for Research and Security Studies (CRSS) recorded at least 1415 violence-linked fatalities and 558 injuries among civilians, security personnel, and outlaws across at least 385 incidents of violence involving terrorist attacks and counter-terrorism operations in Pakistan.
In terms of regional impact, southwestern Balochistan and northwestern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) provinces were the epicentres of violence, with the former recorded as the worst-hit region during the period under review. Jointly, they accounted for 98% of all violence-linked fatalities, with almost 59% (829) recorded in the former and over 39% (556) in the latter province.
Balochistan also suffered the highest number of violent incidents in Q3, 2026, i.e. 186 (over 48%), followed by KP with 173 (nearly 45% of all incidents of violence). Moreover, KP suffered the majority of violence-linked injuries, i.e. 376 (over 67%), followed by Balochistan with 163 (over 29%).
In terms of comparative impact, the country recorded a whopping 83% quarter-on-quarter surge in violence related to terrorism and counter-terrorism, marking the highest escalation since 2021 as well as discontinuing the trend of declining quarter-on-quarter violence reported by CRSS for the first and second quarters of the year.
The reported surge in violence between Q2 to Q3, 2026 (from 774 to 1415 deaths) was largely driven by the sharp escalation in Balochistan province, which recorded almost 213% more fatalities compared to the last quarter, i.e. 829 vs 265. On the contrary, in Q2, 2026, the province recorded over 40% less fatalities compared to the first quarter of the year, i.e. 265 vs 443 – Q2 vs Q1. In Q3, 2026, KP also suffered over 17% percent surge in violence – with fatalities jumping from 475 to 556.
Slight reductions were recorded in Punjab, Sindh and Gilgit Baltistan regions, with fatalities declining from 12 to 8, 14 to 12, and 3 to 0, marking 33%, 14% and 100% less violence, respectively.
In, AJK, the fatal toll rose from 5 to 10 in the reporting quarter, marking 100% surge in violence.
