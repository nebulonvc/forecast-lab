Share on Bluesky Share on Facebook Share on X Share on LinkedIn Print/Save as PDF Print/Save as PDF Last updated May 4, 2026 U.S. Treasurys have long been viewed as a safe, highly liquid investment to lenders worldwide, fueling the low borrowing costs and vast financial capacity of the U.S. federal government. The share of the federal debt held by foreign investors (“foreign debt” throughout this blog), whether governments or individuals, is on the decline. Foreign bank accounts hold more than $9.5 trillion in U.S. Treasury securities, which represents a substantial chunk of the publicly held federal debt—about 30%. This is down, however, from almost 50% in the early 2010s.

Debt held by the public has more than quadrupled since 2008, from $6.4 trillion to $31.4 trillion as of April 2026. Foreign debt has also grown, but only from $3.1 trillion to 9.5 trillion.

In June 2024, the amount of U.S. debt held by foreign private investors surpassed the amount held by foreign governments and central banks. The falling foreign share of Treasurys may also reflect a shift toward other U.S. assets with more attractive returns. U.S. stocks have surged over the last decade, consistently outperforming foreign indexes and comprising an ever-larger share of the global market capitalization.  Since 2015, the share of foreign-held U.S. securities that were Treasury debts declined from 35% to 22%.
