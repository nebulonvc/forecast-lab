Effective Tariff Rates and Revenues (Updated October 14, 2026) | Penn Wharton Budget Model
The USITC recently released updated trade and tariff data. As of August 2026, the average effective tariff rate stood at 7.1 percent. The decline in recent months follows the Supreme Court’s February 20, 2026 declaration that the IEEPA tariffs were unconstitutional.
Effective tariff rates vary dramatically by trade partner and product. Among major trading partners, China faces the highest effective tariff rate of 24.3 percent, although this remains well below its 2025 levels. The share of imports from Canada and Mexico claiming an exemption under USMCA stood at 81.4 percent in August. Steel and aluminum products remain the most heavily tariffed product category at 39.8 percent, followed by automotive vehicles at 13.8 percent.
The Trump administration’s tariff policies have resulted in substantial increases in effective tariff rates. In January 2025, the average effective tariff rate was 2.3 percent. As of August 2026, it stood at 7.1 percent.
The Section 122 tariff expired on July 24, 2026, and was replaced by new Section 301 tariffs of 10 or 12.5 percent on imports from 60 economies, with exemptions for certain products. August is the first full month of data under the new tariffs: the average effective tariff rate rose from 6.7 percent in July to 7.1 percent in August, and the pre-substitution rate rose from 9.6 percent to 10.1 percent.
Source: Penn Wharton Budget Model calculations based on data from U.S. International Trade Commission (USITC) DataWeb.
