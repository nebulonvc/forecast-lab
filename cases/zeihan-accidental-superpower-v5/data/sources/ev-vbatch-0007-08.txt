Understanding these trends is essential for navigating a more complex and less predictable global economy. Global trade reaches a record, driven mainly by goods Global trade in goods and services surpassed $35 trillion in 2025 – a new all-time high . Goods made up roughly three quarters of the total. Services accounted for the remainder. Growth in 2025 was strong across both: Goods trade grew by about 6.5% and drove most of the increase. Services grew faster, at almost 9% , continuing a long-term trend. Higher volumes rather than higher prices were behind most of the growth. Services have more than doubled since 2010 . This reflects the rise of digital services, e-commerce and the recovery of travel and tourism. Globalization rebounds, but fragmentation is rising After two years of decline, globalization strengthened in 2025 , with the trade-to-GDP ratio rising again. At the same time, trade patterns are shifting: Trade concentration increased , with faster growth in large economies. Long-distance trade trends stabilized, while closer trade links grew slightly faster . Policy changes and geopolitical tensions continue to reshape trade flows . These shifts point to a system that is less uniform and more fragmented, with a growing focus on resilience.
