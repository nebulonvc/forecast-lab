The Trump Administration on Wednesday proposed sweeping new tariffs on 14 major trading partners, including China and the European Union , threatening to reignite global trade tensions.

China is set to be hit with the highest rate, facing tariffs of up to 20% on most covered goods. The rate could potentially rise to 40% if Beijing fails to honor the trade truce agreed upon between Beijing and Washington last year.

The European Union also faces a proposed tariff rate of 15% , accounting for certain existing duties, which could rise to 20% if existing trade agreements are not honored.

The European Commission will continue engaging with the U.S. Administration on the implementation of the joint trade agreement signed August 2025, the spokesperson said. “Under the Joint Statement, the 15% all-inclusive ceiling applies to E.U. goods. We expect any measure from this investigation to respect it in full, including when combined with existing duties,” they added.

The U.S. tariffs also target other major economies, including key allies Japan and South Korea, which face rates of up to 15%. India, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, Vietnam, Taiwan, and Bangladesh are also included in the sweeping tariffs unveiled on Thanksgiving eve. The levies for these countries remain between 15% to upwards of 25%, with the notable exception of Vietnam, which may face tariffs of up to 35%. Further determinations concerning Mexico and Norway are still being considered.

The Trump Administration said it would impose the tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 , which permits the government to investigate unfair trade practices abroad and impose tariffs via the executive branch through its findings. The Administration previously announced two investigations in March as it sought ways to revive the tariffs struck down by the Supreme Court in February.
