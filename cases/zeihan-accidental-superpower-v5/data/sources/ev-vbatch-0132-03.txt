Forward Seamen’s Union of India image NEW YORK — October has had the highest number of ship attacks since the war between Iran and the U.S. began in late February. There have been 27 attacks on commercial ships so far in October, according to the International Maritime Organization. There were 22 in March, as well as attacks on an offshore rig and two tugs, according to a list of attacks compiled by USNI News. Despite the number of attacks, ships are continuing to sail through the strait. There were 104 non-Iranian-linked vessels that sailed through between Oct. 5-11, during which there were 12 attacks, according to data from London-based maritime intelligence firm Lloyd’s List . Of those, six occurred on Oct. 11, according to the IMO. Transits did fall, going from 126 the week prior. However, transits between Oct. 5-11 were still 31 percent higher than August’s weekly average, according to Lloyd’s List . While Iran is at fault for the majority of attacks, the U.S. has disabled a number of ships as part of its two-part blockade. Iran is not going after specific ships, Noam Raydan, a senior fellow at The Washington Institute for Near East Policy, told an audience at Lloyd’s List Outlook Forum on Wednesday in New York. But Iran does not have to hit every ship or a specific company to keep the risk high, Raydan said. “The aim is to hit as much as they can and make sure that they keep the southern route appear very unsafe and keep the risks very high,” Raydan said.
