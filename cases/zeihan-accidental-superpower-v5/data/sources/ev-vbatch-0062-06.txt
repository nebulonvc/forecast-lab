WASHINGTON (AP) — A third American aircraft carrier has arrived in the Middle East, a U.S. official said Monday, in a dramatic show of military might as President Donald Trump keeps his options open for next steps in the Iran war.
The U.S. official, who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military operations, confirmed the USS Theodore Roosevelt's arrival in the region.
It brings thousands of additional troops to the Middle East as Trump has oscillated between threatening additional large-scale strikes on Iran and insisting that the more than 8-month-old war "militarily is effectively over."
The Roosevelt, along with its accompanying strike force, left San Diego in September. It joins the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington aircraft carriers, as well as smaller Navy ships, which are in the region to maintain a naval blockade of Iranian shipping.
The H.W. Bush arrived in the region in April, while the George Washington arrived in August. The Navy previously had three carrier strike groups in the Middle East in April, for the first time since 2003.
In addition to the three aircraft carriers, the flotilla in the Arabian Sea now includes 13 destroyers, two cruisers and a littoral combat ship, according to the Navy. Two more destroyers are in the Red Sea.
The Roosevelt had deployed along with the USS Makin Island amphibious readiness group, which includes the USS John P. Murtha and USS Anchorage amphibious landing ships and around 2,000 Marines. Those ships have yet to make it to the region.
The third carrier arrived nearly two weeks after midterm elections that saw voters deliver major losses to Trump's Republican Party, driven in part by the unpopularity of the Iran war and its economic effects. The results led Vice President JD Vance to publicly break with Trump over the conflict, posting on social media that "the reason we lost is the war."
Trump has insisted it was the right thing to do and has not ruled out restarting the conflict, which has been locked in a stalemate for months.
Last week, Trump warned Iran that the U.S. military is "locked and loaded" and that the U.S. was keeping an eye on Pickaxe Mountain, a facility linked to Iran's nuclear program, for signs of activity.
"If something does not work out, I would not hesitate" to act, Trump told conservative broadcaster Hugh Hewitt on Thursday.
Yet, Trump also told reporters last week that he believed it was highly unlikely he would need to order new major strikes on Iran, declaring that the war "militarily is effectively over" after meeting with Qatar's prime minister.
