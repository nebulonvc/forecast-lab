Last Updated: July 2020 Overview Poland is the second-largest coal producer in Europe behind Germany . Poland produces small quantities of crude oil and natural gas, and it is a net crude oil and natural gas importer. The country contains shale resources, but companies exploring for economically recoverable volumes have had underwhelming results. As a result, exploration work has been discontinued. In 2019, Poland’s total primary energy consumption was 4.06 quadrillion British thermal unit (Btu). Consumption of coal accounted for 45%. Petroleum and other liquids (31%), natural gas (17%), and renewable energy sources including hydropower (7%) accounted for the remaining shares of consumption. Poland is working to reduce its dependence on Russian energy imports and better align its energy policy with European Union regulations. Petroleum and other liquids In 2019, Poland consumed about 670,000 barrels per day (b/d) of petroleum and other liquids. Poland imported more than 97% of the crude oil it processed in 2019. In 2019, 61% of Poland’s crude oil imports came from Russia , which is less than both the 10-year average (86%) and 5-year average (80%). Contamination of the Druzhba oil pipeline, which supplies crude oil to Ukraine , Poland, Germany, Hungary , and Slovakia , disrupted Poland’s oil supply between April 24 and June 9, 2019, and is part of the reason why crude oil imports from Russia decreased. Poland’s oil transfer system did not recover until early 2020. Poland’s total crude oil distillation capacity is 581,000 b/d as of January 1, 2020. Poland has two operational refineries. Poland was a net exporter of heavy fuel oil and petroleum coke and a net importer of motor gasoline, liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), and diesel oil in 2018. In 2018, 98% of Poland’s LPG supply was from imports, according to the International Energy Agency. Most LPG imports come from Russia by rail. Poland is also the transit country for shipments of LPG by rail from Russia, Khazakhstan , and Belarus to Germany and the Czech Republic . Most of the LPG consumed in Poland is used as fuel for road vehicles. LPG-powered vehicles represent 14% of all registered cars in Poland. As of 2018, Poland is one of the largest consumers of LPG as a road fuel, second in the world in terms of the number of LPG-fueled vehicles, and third in terms of LPG fueling stations.
