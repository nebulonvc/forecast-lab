Guanajuato, the State of Mexico, and Coahuila combine the highest labor availability with moderate formal wages among Mexico’s 13 main industrial states, while Queretaro, Nuevo Leon, and Baja California pair the highest wages with limited labor slack, according to ENOE and IMSS data. The data reflects nearshoring’s concentration in established hubs, where manufacturing wages already exceed MX$780 per day, while state averages mask wide municipal gaps and border hubs face a higher minimum wage floor.

The relocation of supply chains raised employment in high-value manufacturing but did not spread it geographically. The northern border and the Bajio increased their share of employment in nearshoring-linked sectors from 85.6% in 2019 to 86.6% in 2025. “Nearshoring has not redistributed productive activity; it has concentrated it even further in existing hubs,” says Banamex. The bank attributes this persistence to skilled labor, supplier networks, infrastructure, and proximity to US border crossings.

That concentration now shows in hiring conditions. Seventy-one percent of manufacturing employers in Mexico report difficulty filling vacancies, according to ManpowerGroup’s 2026 Talent Shortage Survey.

Guanajuato, the State of Mexico, and Coahuila offer the most favorable combination of available workers and moderate formal wages among Mexico’s 13 main industrial states, while Queretaro, Nuevo Leon, and Baja California close the ranking as the highest-paying states in the group, according to INEGI and IMSS data. Queretaro and Nuevo Leon also post unemployment rates below the national average, leaving the country’s most established hubs with the least labor slack.

MBN ranked 13 states that host Mexico’s main industrial corridors on two indicators. Labor availability is measured by the unemployment rate in INEGI’s National Occupation and Employment Survey (ENOE) for 2Q26, the most recent quarterly data available by state. Wage level is measured by the average daily contribution wage of IMSS-registered jobs in July 2026 from IMSS open data. Each state received a position on both indicators, with higher unemployment and lower wages ranking first. The two positions were added, and ties were broken by the number of unemployed people. State figures serve as a proxy for each hub, and the wage measure covers all sectors, not manufacturing alone.

San Luis Potosi ranks fourth, at 3.2% unemployment and MX$687.48, followed by Sonora, at 2.8% and MX$652.00, and Tamaulipas, at 3.2% and MX$705.86. Puebla ranks seventh. It has the lowest average wage in the group, at MX$592.96, but also its lowest unemployment rate, at 1.8%, and a labor informality rate of 70.3%. Aguascalientes ranks eighth, with 2.6% unemployment and the smallest pool of unemployed people in the group, at 19,152.

Jalisco ranks ninth, at 2.4% and MX$658.55. Chihuahua ranks 10th, at 2.7% and MX$699.06, with wages in Ciudad Juarez averaging MX$774.82. Juarez lost about 57,500 maquiladora jobs between mid-2023 and mid-2025, an 18% decline, while maquiladora wages rose 87% between August 2018 and August 2025, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas.

Baja California ranks 11th, with the highest average wage in the group, at MX$745.78, and the highest manufacturing wage among the states with available data, at MX$806.63. Nuevo Leon ranks 12th, at 2.5% unemployment and MX$739.87. The state added 31,585 manufacturing jobs through August, according to MBN. Queretaro ranks last, with the lowest unemployment rate among the 13 states outside Puebla, at 2.3%, and an average wage of MX$733.53 that rises to MX$814.33 in El Marques.

Minimum wages set different starting points across hubs. The general minimum wage in 2026 is MX$315.04 per day, while the Northern Border Free Zone, which includes Tijuana, Ciudad Juarez, and Reynosa, sets it at MX$440.87. Calder & Vale estimates first-year employer costs for an auto parts operator at US$3.53 per paid hour in Saltillo at the general minimum,US$4.37 in Queretaro at MX$400 per day, and US$4.94 in Tijuana at the border minimum.

Unemployment alone does not capture the full labor supply. The general pressure rate, which includes unemployed workers and employed workers seeking another job, reaches 6.9% in Guanajuato and Sonora and 6.8% in Coahuila and San Luis Potosi, compared with 3.1% in Jalisco and 3.2% in Queretaro, according to the ENOE. Informality also varies widely, from 34.8% in Nuevo Leon to 54.8% in Guanajuato and 55.3% in the State of Mexico, against a national rate of 55.1% that rose in 2Q26 as informal work absorbed 98% of job growth, reports MBN.
