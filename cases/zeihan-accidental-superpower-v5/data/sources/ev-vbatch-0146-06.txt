Download PDF Share on LinkedIn Share on X Share on Facebook Email Key Findings How the United States retrenches, not just whether it does, will shape key allies' responses. A phased and coordinated approach to retrenchment is likely to increase and/or focus allies' defense spending and deepen regional cooperation. And allies would likely seek to maintain remaining ties with Washington. However, an uncoordinated, rapid withdrawal—especially combined with normalizing ties with Russia while the war with Ukraine is ongoing—risks allies further loosening economic and security ties with the United States. Using posture and commitments to reward and punish individual allies could undermine regional cooperation. Since coming into office, the second Trump administration has been reshaping U.S. policy toward Europe. In addition to imposing tariffs, raising doubts about the U.S. commitment to NATO, seeking control of Greenland, and reducing support for Ukraine, the administration has called for a reduced U.S. security role in Europe. The administration's concept for Europe—NATO 3.0—envisions allies taking primary responsibility for their own conventional defense. In line with this vision, the administration has reduced planned contributions to NATO crisis operations, announced the withdrawal of 5,000 troops stationed in Germany, and launched a review to inform further force reductions. A RAND study provides insights into how four of the most influential and militarily capable NATO allies (France, Germany, Poland, and the United Kingdom) would likely respond to further U.S. retrenchment and identifies steps the United States can take to realize its strategic goals and mitigate risks of unintended reactions.
