Economy Explained: NBU’s Forecast (July 2026) Economy Explained: NBU’s Forecast (July 2026) 25 Aug 2026 17:40 The NBU updates its macroeconomic forecast every quarter and publishes it in its Inflation Report. The report has lots of useful information and is mainly meant for professionals to read. Here on this page, we use plain language to tell you what the NBU thinks the Ukrainian economy’s future will be like. The following is based on the July 2026 Inflation Report . Main economic trends Economy Has revived following a difficult start of the year. Inflation Has picked up but remains moderate. Wages Are growing further due to a shortage of workers. Hryvnia deposits and government bonds Are shielding savings from inflation. How the economy is holding up The beginning of 2026 was difficult for the Ukrainian economy due to significant damage to the energy infrastructure. The electricity shortage severely affected businesses. Another problem was the war in the Middle East, which caused fuel prices to rise. In addition, there were delays in international funding during the first months of the year, and the government lacked the funds to support the economy. As a result, Ukraine’s real GDP contracted in Q1 2026 for the first time in a long time. However, the economy began to recover as early as Q2. An important factor was the improvement in the energy sector, thanks to rapid repairs, warm weather, and the active operation of solar power plants. In addition, inflows from international partners increased. In early summer, Ukraine received the first tranche of funds under the Ukraine Support Loan program (a key source of financial assistance in the near future). These funds supported the economy. Overall, the NBU expects Ukraine to receive a record amount of external support this year – USD 87 billion. These funds will be allocated to various social programs (education, healthcare, etc.), the reconstruction of damaged infrastructure, and the production of weapons in Ukraine. All of this will support economic activity. According to the NBU’s forecast, real GDP will grow by approximately 1.8% by the end of 2026. Economic growth could have been higher were it not for russia’s intensified attacks on ports, warehouses, and manufacturing facilities. These attacks are causing additional losses for the economy. Businesses are adapting to the new conditions: purchasing additional power generation equipment, establishing new supply routes, and distributing product inventories across various warehouses. The government and the NBU are supporting the economy through infrastructure reconstruction and preferential lending programs. However, the impact of these attacks on the economy will be significant. If the security situation improves, Ukraine’s economic growth in the coming years could accelerate to about 3%. This will be driven, in particular, by new investments in reconstruction and the gradual return of migrants. However, the course of the war remains the main risk to this forecast. After that, GDP growth rates are forecast to increase to 2.8% in 2027 and 3.0% in 2028. How jobs and wages are doing Due to the war and migration, the labor shortage persists. Despite the increase in the number of job seekers over the past few months, businesses are facing an acute shortage of workers. Job seekers’ skills often do not meet employers’ requirements, and many vacancies remain unfilled. This is particularly acute when seeking skilled workers in blue-collar trades. More active efforts to recruit young people and older adults, as well as to reintegrate veterans, only partially address the labor shortage. As a result, competition for skilled professionals is intensifying. Businesses have to raise wages both to attract new workers and to retain existing staff. While real wages (adjusted for inflation) in Ukraine rose by an average of 7% last year, the NBU forecasts a growth of more than 12% this year. Wages are rising in both the private sector and the public sector; in particular, teachers’ salaries have increased markedly this year.
