Topline As U.S. retrenchment from Europe gets underway, allies’ reactions will hinge on the manner in which Washington pulls back. The Trump administration has taken initial steps to retrench from Europe—that is, to reduce U.S. commitments to NATO allies and U.S. military presence in Europe. These steps include modestly reducing peacetime presence and the forces the United States commits to make available to NATO in a crisis. As Washington weighs how much more and how quickly to pull back, a critical factor to consider is how key allies will respond. RAND researchers analyzed how four NATO allies—France, Germany, Poland, and the United Kingdom—are likely to react to different approaches to U.S. retrenchment. Key Takeaways The manner of U.S. retrenchment matters. A coordinated, gradual drawdown would likely lead allies to preserve security ties with Washington; a sudden, large-scale withdrawal could cause allies to accelerate efforts to reduce dependence on U.S. arms. U.S. transactionalism could divide allies. Allies’ diverse responses to U.S. use of presence and commitments to reward and punish individual states could create fissures that undermine regional cooperation. The associated U.S. policy toward Russia matters, too. U.S. pursuit of a détente with Russia that does not prioritize European security could lead allies to reduce cooperation with the United States, such as by curtailing intelligence-sharing. The four allies are unlikely to align with Russia or China. All four countries see Russia as a threat and view China's interests as misaligned with their own. No structural conditions exist for aligning with a rival power, regardless of how the United States retrenches. Recommendations To implement NATO 3.0 or a similar framework: Develop and share long-term plans with allies for reductions in U.S. peacetime presence, crisis contributions, and force structure, so allies can phase their investments more effectively. Take concrete steps to execute that plan over time as conditions allow, helping allies sustain political momentum on defense spending.
