Late last week, the Russian government released figures that underscored Russia’s continued strong economic rally this year and its ability to wage war in Ukraine through 2027. As a result of unexpectedly resilient growth in GDP and a strong boost in trade activity, Russian President Vladimir Putin remained defiant in the face of heightened efforts from the Europeans to increase pressure on Moscow.
In the process, Putin sharpened his rhetorical attacks on the European Union and signaled that he now has the additional resources necessary to continue the war indefinitely.
Already emboldened by the complete absence of a European presence at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Summit last week, Putin used his platform at APEC to praise Russia for its economic resilience. He underscored that “external pressure has been a great stimulus for Russia” and that “over the last three years, Russia’s GDP has grown by over 10% and keeps growing.”
Moreover, the Bank of Russia provided additional data last week indicating that GDP grew by 1.0% in the third quarter of 2026, exceeding the expected 0.6%. As a result, Rosstat plans to raise its annual growth figure from a range of 0 to 1% to 0 to 1.5%.
In contrast, the OECD recently reported growth for the third quarter in the Eurozone at 0.4%. The results for specific EU countries were even less impressive: France at -0.1%, Germany at 0.2%, and Italy at 0.0%.
It is noteworthy to highlight that Russia has also become the world's fourth-largest economy by Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) and the largest economy in Europe by PPP.
While speaking at APEC, Putin accused Europe of economic ineptitude. Raising the European Union’s worsening economic conditions in a November 18 speech , he exclaimed that “the EU is now losing its industrial capacity and it’s losing in the competition.”
These remarks seek to undermine the European elite during a time of political vulnerability as they continue to increase their anti-Russian rhetoric and seek to escalate support for the war in Ukraine. It is important to note that the EU, almost simultaneously during Putin’s remarks, was preparing a new packet of sanctions to include banking and cryptocurrency among other items, as well as another loan package of 90 billion euros. In addition, Europe’s “Coalition of the Willing” met in Berlin on November 19 to discuss further support for Ukraine.
Putin then criticized sanctions imposed by the West and proceeded to say that “this is what we have seen in countries that have been actively imposing anti-Russian or any other sanctions. The EU hasn’t been able to adapt to this. Hasn’t been able to adapt to the loss of cheap and reliable energy supplies from Russia.”
His comments regarding the ineffectiveness of sanctions were backed by recent indicators that a trade surge in 2026 (January-September) buoyed recent Russian economic growth. The Federal Customs Service (FCS) reported that trade turnover in 2026 grew 13.9% compared with the same period in 2025, with exports increasing by 14.1%. The overall trade balance increased from $103.2 billion to $116.8 billion.
A closer look at exports by sector supports a Chatham House report that rightly concludes “the US-Israeli war on Iran has been a welcome gift for Russia’s President Vladimir Putin.” For example, earlier referenced data from Russia’s FCS shows that mineral fuel exports (including oil) increased 14.3%, followed by metals (21.8%) and foodstuffs and agricultural materials (15.6%).
Furthermore, the latest trade statistics indicate how the Iran war has only intensified ties between Russia and Asia, particularly China . Federal Customs statistics show that Asia accounted for three-quarters of Russian trade turnover in 2026, with exports increasing 17.7% and exports increasing 17.6%. Moreover, trade with Beijing increased over 30%.
In contrast to his earlier comments regarding the EU’s economic stagnation, Putin’s remarks at APEC heavily underscored the importance of Russia’s trade policy pivot from Europe to Asia. “To Russia, APEC is an important line of trade. In 2014, the region’s share in our commodity turnover was over 25%...Today, this figure is over 50%, 53 to be precise, of Russia’s total trade turnover,” he emphasized .
In contrast, FCS notes trade with Europe grew only 5.9% in the third quarter and now represents only 12% of total Russian exports . This marks a significant shift from just 20 years ago, when Russian exports to Europe accounted for over 55% of total exports.
Increased revenues from improved Russian trade in commodities also have improved Russia’s budget position. According to Kommersant , the draft second reading of the budget reflects that while the budget for 2027 still anticipates a deficit, the deficit is predicted to narrow from 3.2% of GDP to 2.2% of GDP as a result of a 5.1% rise in income. This figure is in line with the 2.2% deficit through October 2026.
