World Oil Transit Chokepoints | U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), February 2026
International energy markets depend on reliable transport routes. The blockage of oil transit through a major chokepoint, even temporarily, can lead to substantial supply delays and higher shipping costs, resulting in higher world energy prices.
In the first half of 2025 (1H25), total world petroleum and other liquids supply was about 104.4 million barrels per day (b/d). We estimate that about 76% of that amount (79.8 million b/d) traveled via seaborne trade (Table 1).
Red Sea attacks and oil disruptions in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait that began at the end of 2023 caused these intra-Saudi Arabia volumes to drop to 18,000 b/d by 1H25. Red Sea disruptions increase oil flows around Cape of Good Hope. Red Sea attacks increase shipping times and freight rates.
In 1H25, the United States imported about 0.4 million b/d of crude oil and condensate from Persian Gulf countries through the Strait of Hormuz, accounting for about 7% of U.S. crude oil and condensate imports and 2% of U.S. petroleum liquids consumption. U.S. crude oil imports from countries in the Persian Gulf have fallen to the lowest level in 40 years as domestic production has increased.
