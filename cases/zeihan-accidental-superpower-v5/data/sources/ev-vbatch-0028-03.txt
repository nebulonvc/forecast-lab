2025 Social Security Trustees Report Explained | Bipartisan Policy Center
Social Security’s primary trust fund is projected to be depleted in 2033—no change from last year’s projection. Unless Congress acts, current and future beneficiaries alike will see their benefits cut by 23%.
The program’s long-run finances are no better, with an estimated $25 trillion shortfall over the next 75 years.
An aging population is a major contributor to the problem: In 1960, there were more than five workers paying Social Security taxes per beneficiary, but that ratio has dropped to less than three-to-one.
At the same time, the payroll taxes that fund benefits are levied on a smaller proportion of total income than in the past: just 83%, compared to 90% in 1983.
Update: In August 2025, Social Security’s chief actuary reported that the “ One Big Beautiful Bill Act ,” which was signed by the president on July 4, 2025, will advance trust fund depletion to 2032.
