The U.S. Age-Adjusted Death Rate Reached a Record Low in 2025: What Drove the Decline? Breadcrumb ASPE ASPE Reports The U.S. Age-Adjusted Death Rate Reached a Record Low in 2025: What Drove the Decline? Publication Date Oct 27, 2026 Authors Stephen Murphy, Nicholas Holtkamp, and Eric Sun KEY POINTS CDC reported a provisional 2025 age-adjusted death rate of 689.2 deaths per 100,000 population, the lowest ever reported death rate and 4.6% below the final 2024 rate of 722.1 deaths per 100,000 population. CDC notes that the published 2024 and 2025 rates use different Census population-estimate methodologies. Re-estimating both years using Vintage 2025 populations reduces the estimated decline from 4.6 percent to approximately 2 percent. A common-vintage calculation for all years covered by Vintage 2025 (2020 – 2025) confirms that 2025 still has the lowest age-adjusted death rate. Using this common-vintage comparison between 2024 and 2025, we find that declines in unintentional injury mortality made the largest contribution to the age-adjusted death rate decline (30%), followed by deaths from COVID-19 (28%), cancer (18%) and heart disease (11%). Approximately 73 percent of the decline in unintentional-injury mortality came from a decline in unintentional drug overdoses, and nearly all of the overdose decline was concentrated among deaths involving synthetic opioids other than methadone, including fentanyl. INTRODUCTION In July 2026, the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) reported that 3,094,593 deaths occurred among U.S. residents in 2025 and that the provisional age-adjusted death rate was 689.2 deaths per 100,000 population.
