Short answer Alberta is by far the largest net contributor to federal finances. The Fraser Institute estimates Alberta's net contribution net contribution , federal taxes paid minus federal money spent in the province, at $244.6 billion from 2007 to 2022, more than five times that of British Columbia or Ontario. That gap is real, but it is not simply because Albertans are permanently richer people. Alberta tends to draw younger workers from other provinces for jobs, while some retirees later collect pensions and health spending elsewhere, which keeps the workforce here relatively young and high-earning on average and raises federal tax paid per person in the province. Federal tax rates are the same nationwide; the difference is who is living and working here at a given time. For clarity, the figure is a net contribution measured across all federal taxes and programs, which is a broader measure than the equalization equalization program specifically, but the underlying claim that Albertans pay far more in than they get back is correct. What the evidence shows The non-partisan Library of Parliament finds Alberta is the largest per-capita net contributor to federal finances, with the federal government raising more revenue per person there than it spends, reflecting Alberta's high incomes and young population.
