Old-age dependency growing across EU regions. 1 October 2025. Eurostat. The EU’s old-age dependency ratio, defined as the proportion of elderly individuals (aged 65 years or over) relative to the working-age population (people aged 20 to 64 years), has increased noticeably in the last 20 years. In 2004, the EU’s old-age dependency ratio was 26.8%, meaning there were slightly fewer than 4 working-age adults per elderly person aged 65 years or over. On 1 January 2024, the ratio was 37.0%, indicating that there were fewer than 3 working-age adults for every elderly person. On January 2024, there were 139 nomenclature of territorial units for statistics (NUTS 3) regions where the old-age dependency ratio was at least 50%. This group of regions was mainly concentrated in (eastern) Germany and France. In addition, Italy, Finland, Portugal, Bulgaria, Greece and Spain also had several regions where the old-age dependency ratio was at least 50%.
