A snapshot view of business activities along the Lobito Corridor
The Lobito Corridor promises more than mineral exports—yet SMEs, weak logistics and state-dependent ventures reveal a deeper struggle between high-speed extraction and truly inclusive value addition.
The major focus of the Lobito Corridor has been on the concessionaire – a consortium comprising Mota-Engil, Trafigura and Vecturis – which operates cargo trains on the Benguela Railway line to transport hundreds of thousands of tonnes of minerals per year.
However, this narrow focus risks overlooking the Corridor’s broader developmental potential, particularly regarding small and medium enterprises (SMEs) which are constrained to using the erratic passenger train run by the Angolan rail operator Caminho de Ferro de Benguela (CFB) for their logistics.

The involvement of the Angolan state in Pensana Plc and Flying Swans adding value in the Corridor may underscore the imperative for state-led catalysation of investment and coordination of value-chain development.
The Lobito Corridor is a strategic transport route anchored by the rehabilitated Benguela Railway. It links the Atlantic port of Lobito in Angola to the Copperbelt mining regions of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and Zambia to facilitate the export of minerals and bolster the integration of regional trade.
