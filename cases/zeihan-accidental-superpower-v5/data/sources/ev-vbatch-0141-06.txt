Euro area government deficit at 3.0% and EU at 3.1% of GDP
In the euro area (EA20) the government deficit to GDP ratio remained unchanged at 3.0% in 2024 and 2025. In the EU , it also remained unchanged at 3.1%, the same level as in 2024. In the euro area the government debt to GDP ratio increased from 86.6% at the end of 2024 to 87.7% at the end of 2025, and in the EU from 80.3% to 81.5%.
In this release, Eurostat, the statistical office of the European Union , is providing government deficit and debt data for the years 2022-2025, based on figures reported by EU Member States in the second notification in 2026, for the application of the excessive deficit procedure (EDP). This notification is based on the ESA 2010 system of national accounts. This release also includes data on government expenditure and revenue.
Deficit and debt data for Euro area and EU
Government deficit (-) / surplus (+)
In 2025, all Member States, except Cyprus (+3.6%), Denmark (+2.9%), Greece (+2.0%), Ireland (+1.8%) and Portugal (+0.7%), reported a deficit. The highest deficits were recorded in Romania (‑7.8%), Poland (-7.2%), Belgium (-5.2%) and France (‑5.1%). Eleven Member States had deficits equal to or higher than 3% of GDP.
At the end of 2025, the lowest ratios of government debt to GDP were recorded in Estonia (24.0%), Luxembourg (26.0%), Denmark (27.2%), Bulgaria (29.9%), Ireland (34.8%), Sweden (35.1%) and Lithuania (39.3%). Twelve Member States had government debt ratios higher than 60% of GDP, with the highest registered in Greece (145.4%), Italy (136.7%), France (115.7%), Belgium (108.1%) and Spain (100.5%).
In 2025, the government total expenditure to GDP ratio in the euro area stood at 49.8% of GDP and the government total revenue to GDP ratio stood at 46.8%. The figures for the EU were 49.4% and 46.3%, respectively. Government revenue and expenditure ratios increased both in the euro area and the EU compared to 2024.
Reservations on reported data
Eurostat is expressing a reservation on the quality of data reported by Bulgaria for 2025. Eurostat is discussing with the Bulgarian statistical authorities the appropriate national accounts recording of some operations of the Road Infrastructure Agency (RIA) ( Агенция Пътна инфраструктура ). This will require additional data from the national authorities. Bulgaria is currently recording these amounts as impacting the 2025 deficit by 0.6% of GDP.
Amendment by Eurostat to reported data
Eurostat has made no amendments to the data reported by Member States.

In the previous provision of data for the excessive deficit procedure, the 2025 government deficit for the euro area (EA20) was 2.9% of GDP, for the EU it was 3.1% of GDP, and the government debt was 87.8% of GDP for the euro area and 81.7% for the EU. See News release of 22 April 2026.
According to Article 14 (1) of Council Regulation (EC) 479/2009, Eurostat publishes the actual government deficit and debt data for the application of the Protocol on the excessive deficit procedure, within three weeks after the reporting deadlines.
The Protocol on the excessive deficit procedure annexed to the EC Treaty, defines government deficit (surplus) as the net borrowing (net lending) of the whole general government sector (central government, state government, local government and social security funds). It is calculated according to national accounts concepts (European System of Accounts, ESA 2010). Government debt (commonly known as Maastricht or EDP debt) is the consolidated gross debt of the whole general government sector outstanding at the end of the year (at nominal value).
