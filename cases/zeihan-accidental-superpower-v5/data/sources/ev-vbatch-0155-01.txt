Population structure and ageing - Statistics Explained - Eurostat. Data extracted on 2 February 2026. Highlights: On 1 January 2025, the EU population was estimated at 450.6 million people and more than one-fifth (22.0%) of it was aged 65 years and over. On 1 January 2025, the median age of the EU's population reached 44.9 years. Between 2015 and 2025, the median age increased in all EU members, except Malta and Germany, where it decreased (both with -0.4 years). As a result of demographic change, the proportion of people of working age in the EU is shrinking while the relative number of those retired is expanding. The old-age dependency ratio for the EU was 34.5% on 1 January 2025 (Table 2), with just over 3 persons of working age for every person aged 65 years and over. The old-age dependency ratio across the EU countries ranged from lows of 22.0% in Luxembourg, 23.8% in Ireland and 26.6% in Malta, with almost 5 working age persons for every person aged 65 years and over, to highs of 39.0% in Italy, 38.7% in Bulgaria and 38.6% in Portugal, where there were fewer than 3 working age persons for every person aged 65 years and over. Between 2024 and 2025, the old-age dependency ratio increased in EU by 0.6 pp from 33.9% in 2024 to 34.5% in 2025. It increased in all EU countries.
