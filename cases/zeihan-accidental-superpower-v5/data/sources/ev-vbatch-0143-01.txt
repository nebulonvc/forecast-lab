Chandrasekhar, IDEAs Article summary The global uncertainty unleashed by the new US administration in the last six months is leading to a worsening context for the Global South, which already faces significant obstacles to economic diversification and productivity increase. The attack on the ‘free trade’ regime by its primary beneficiary, the US, threatens to displace the order which emerged following the collapse of the Bretton Woods monetary system in the 1970s. This creates a deepening crisis of legitimacy for the IMF as the gendarme of global finance, as the US forsakes the basic tenets of the neoliberalist approach the Fund and the World Bank have promoted. In this context, less developed countries are likely to be left with a legacy of debt and foreign financial dependence with dwindling foreign earnings to service those liabilities, a situation not easily resolved without hurting finance. Share Almost six months after “Liberation Day”, on 2 April 2025, when US President Donald Trump announced his decision to impose varying levels of tariffs on imports to the US, uncertainty prevails. One uncertainty arises, for example, from the sudden increases and reversals of US import tariffs imposed on different countries, which disrupts their role in global production chains.
