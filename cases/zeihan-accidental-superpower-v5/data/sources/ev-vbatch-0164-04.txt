Release date: 2025-06-18
In 2024, virtually all of Canada’s natural gas exports went to the United States (U.S.), with exports averaging 8.8 billion cubic feet per day (Bcf/d) – the highest level since 2010. This marked an 8.1% increase from the previous year. A mild winter in Canada and record natural gas production Footnote 1 in western Canada led to increased supply available for export. Additionally, relatively low gas prices in Canada made Canadian natural gas more competitive, while extremely cold weather in parts of the U.S. increased demand for heating. At the same time, the value of Canadian natural gas exports fell by one-third in 2024, due to a sharp decline in export prices.
Exports accounted for 47.8% of Canadian gas production in 2024, a 2.6% increase from the previous year and a 3.5% rise compared to 2020.

In 2024, Canada transported 99.9% of its exported natural gas via pipelines, all of which flowed to the U.S., with volumes varying across U.S. regions. Of Canada’s natural gas exports to the U.S. in 2024, 45.8% went to the Midwest, 41.2% to the West, and 13.0 % to the East. Compared to 2023, exports to the Midwest rose by 8.1%, to the West by 2.3%, and to the East by 32.6%.
