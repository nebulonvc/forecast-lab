Defense Primer: U.S. European Command (EUCOM), Updated June 30, 2026 (IF11130). U.S. European Command (USEUCOM, commonly referred to as EUCOM) is one of 11 combatant commands (CCMDs) within the Department of Defense. EUCOM was established in 1947 and is responsible for the "command and control of U.S. military forces in Europe." The Senate confirmed the current EUCOM commander, U.S. Air Force General Alexus Grynkewich, in June 2025. The EUCOM commander, therefore, has traditionally had dual-hatted responsibilities (i.e., simultaneously overseen EUCOM and NATO's military operations). All five DOD armed services, as well as U.S. Special Operations Command, provide forces to EUCOM. According to DOD, as of March 2026, there were more than 86,000 DOD personnel in Europe. According to EUCOM, the command's mission is to "deter conflict, maintain peace and security, and provide strategic depth through military operations, exercises and security cooperation with NATO allies and partners." In March 2026 testimony, General Grynkewich stated that EUCOM "has prioritized three mission areas: 1) Protect the U.S. Homeland; 2) Project U.S. Combat Power; and 3) Position the Alliance to Deter and Defend."
