Factsheet: Asia and Oceania Highlights from the International Yearbook of Industrial Statistics 2025. Asia and Oceania have firmly established themselves as the epicenter of global manufacturing. Since 2015, the region has consistently contributed more than half of the world’s manufacturing value added (MVA), reaching 57.2 per cent in 2024. Its role in trade is equally significant, with exports of manufactured goods accounting for 47.2 per cent of the global total in the same year, underscoring the region’s central position in international production networks. Manufacturing activity remains highly concentrated in Eastern Asia, home to some of the world’s largest and most competitive economies such as China, Japan, the Republic of Korea, and Taiwan Province of China. In 2023, this subregion alone generated three-quarters of Asia and Oceania’s MVA, while South-eastern and Southern Asia contributed 8.5 per cent shares each. Trade dynamics further highlight the region’s industrial prowess. A widening manufacturing trade surplus, driven by Eastern Asia and supported by Southeastern Asia, contrasts sharply with trade deficits in other subregions. Employment trends also show resilience: manufacturing jobs increased across all subregions in 2023, extending the post-pandemic recovery. Between 2010 and 2023, all subregions except Oceania also improved their rankings in the Competitive Industrial Performance (CIP) Index, with Eastern Asia leading regionally and globally.
