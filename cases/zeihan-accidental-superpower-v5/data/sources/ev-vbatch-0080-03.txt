U.S. Security Cooperation with Ukraine — Bureau of Political-Military Affairs, U.S. Department of State. Negotiations will involve complex and vigorous diplomacy, and both Russia and Ukraine will have to make tough decisions. In talks in Jeddah on March 11, 2025 the Ukrainian delegation expressed readiness to accept the U.S. proposal to enact an immediate, interim 30-day ceasefire. To date, we have provided $66.9 billion in military assistance since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine on February 24, 2022, and approximately $69.7 billion in military assistance since Russia’s initial invasion of Ukraine in 2014. We have now used the emergency Presidential Drawdown Authority on 55 occasions since August 2021 to provide Ukraine military assistance totaling approximately $31.7 billion from DoD stockpiles. Equipment provided includes three Patriot air defense batteries and munitions, 12 National Advanced Surface-to-Air Missile Systems (NASAMS) and munitions, more than 40 High Mobility Artillery Rocket Systems (HIMARS) and ammunition, more than 200 155mm Howitzers and more than 3,000,000 155mm artillery rounds, and more than 300 Bradley Infantry Fighting Vehicles.
