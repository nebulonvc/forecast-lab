Covering death registrations through September 29, 2026, the files reveal at least 490,000 officially registered Russian military deaths in the war with Ukraine. We confirmed the data is genuine by cross-checking its 11.6 million unique death records against our named list (matching 95.1% of entries through late September, including unpublished names), the national Probate Registry, other civil registry leaks, court rulings, and Rosstat’s own previously published statistics. The leak also allowed us to test our earlier Probate Registry estimates—confirming that they closely tracked regular combat losses, while systematically undercounting soldiers missing in action starting in early 2025. Because a growing share of military deaths are now registered a year or more after the fact, even Rosstat’s authentic records fall short of the full toll. Accounting for these registration lags and the missing, our updated estimate puts total Russian losses at more than 700,000—likely reaching 720,000 or more by fall 2026. From early summer and into the mid-fall season of 2022, volunteers bore the brunt of the losses, which is strikingly different from the situation in the initial stage of the war: in winter and early spring, the Airborne Forces suffered the greatest damage, followed by the Motorised Rifle troops. By the end of 2022 and the beginning of the next year, losses among prisoners recruited into the Wagner PMC increased markedly. They were formed into “assault groups” to overwhelm Ukrainian positions near Bakhmut. By March 2023, prisoners became the largest category of war losses. After the capture of Bakhmut, there have been no cases of mass use of prisoners so far. By September 2024, volunteers once again emerged as the largest category among the KIA. This shift reflects a cumulative effect: prison recruitment had significantly waned, no new mobilisation had been announced, yet the stream of volunteers continued unabated.
