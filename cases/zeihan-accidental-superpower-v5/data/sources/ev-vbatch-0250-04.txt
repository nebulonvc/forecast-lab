Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) and World Meteorological Organization (WMO), 2025: European State of the Climate 2024, released on 15 April 2025. Since the 1980s, Europe has been warming twice as fast as the global average, making it the fastest-warming continent on Earth. Globally, 2024 was the warmest year on record, following on from the remarkable warmth of 2023. The last 10 years have been the warmest ten years on record. Heatwaves are becoming more frequent and severe, and southern Europe is seeing widespread droughts. Glaciers in all European regions continue to melt. Changes in the pattern of precipitation, including an increase in the intensity of the most extreme events, have been observed. Storms were often severe and flooding widespread, claiming at least 335 lives and affecting an estimated 413 000 people. During the year, there was a striking east-west contrast in climate conditions, with extremely dry and often record-warm conditions in the east, and warm but wet conditions in the west.
