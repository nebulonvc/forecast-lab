Poverty, Prosperity, and Planet Report 2026: Jobs at the Center of Poverty Eradication (World Bank). Global progress on reducing extreme poverty has come to a standstill, and the world is at risk of falling further behind, according to the new Poverty, Prosperity and Planet Report 2026: Jobs at the Center of Poverty Eradication. In 2026, 811 million people, 1 in 10, live on less than $3.00 a day, a rate unchanged since 2019. More than half the extreme poor live in nine economies, and by 2030, 87 percent will be in Sub-Saharan Africa, in fragile and conflict-affected situations, or both. Despite the reduction seen in past decades, escaping extreme poverty has not meant reaching security. Nearly half of humanity, 3.7 billion people, still lives on less than $8.30 a day, most of them one shock away from falling back. Data for 2025 and 2026 are nowcasts (refer to annex 1A in the full report). World Bank PIP actual global aggregates (api.worldbank.org/pip/v1/pip-grp): at $3.00/day (2021 PPP), global poverty headcount fell from 14.51% (1,066.9 million) in 2014 to 10.12% (824.3 million) in 2024, while global daily mean income/consumption rose from $17.76 to $21.61; at $8.30/day, global headcount fell from 54.65% (4,019.3 million) in 2014 to 46.16% (3,758.1 million) in 2024.
