Throughput then increased by 1.8 million b/d in the third quarter, primarily as refiners in the region adjusted to new trade flows. Crude oil inputs to refineries typically increase in the second and third quarters of the year as refiners complete planned maintenance and gear up to meet peak seasonal demand for refined products, such as gasoline and jet fuel. Global crude oil inputs increased by 0.3 million b/d in the second quarter of 2025 and by another 2 million b/d in the third quarter. The countries with the largest refining capacity in the world are the United States, China, Russia, and India. Refining in countries most directly impacted by the loss of crude oil supply from conflict in the Strait of Hormuz saw the largest declines. Crude oil refinery inputs in China fell by 2.1 million b/d in 2Q26 and by an additional 0.8 million b/d in 3Q26, while crude oil inputs in India, South Korea, and Japan fell by more than 1.0 million b/d collectively in 2Q26 before rebounding by 0.8 million b/d in 3Q26. Crude oil inputs also fell in the Middle East as the closure of the strait significantly limited the export of refined products. Crude oil inputs fell by 0.7 million b/d in Kuwait, 0.7 million b/d in the United Arab Emirates, 0.3 million b/d in Saudi Arabia, and 0.2 million b/d in Iran in 2Q26. Total crude oil inputs in these four countries increased by 0.4 million b/d in 3Q26. Increased attacks on Russian oil infrastructure during the ongoing conflict between Russia and Ukraine reduced refinery crude oil inputs in Russia by 0.6 million b/d in 2Q26 and an additional 0.8 million b/d in 3Q26.
