Amid a pause in Donald J. Trump’s bombing of Iran, the U.S. president is said to be weighing diplomatic and military options in the conflict. But the conclusive victory that Trump advertised — where the regime is removed, Iran gives a binding pledge to not build nuclear weapons, and its missile production capabilities are capped — would likely now require the administration to put boots on the ground in large numbers. Critics have pointed out the obvious problems with this: Iran’s mountainous terrain makes a land invasion difficult; it is a country of 90 million people, twice the population of Iraq; the Iranians have been planning for this contingency ever since George W. Bush labeled them part of the “Axis of Evil”; and unlike Iraq and Libya, the Iranian military has shown a willingness to fight and inflict casualties. But there is another problem. America has neither enough ground troops for an invasion some estimate would require more than one million troops , nor the willingness to take sufficient casualties in such a bloody battle.
