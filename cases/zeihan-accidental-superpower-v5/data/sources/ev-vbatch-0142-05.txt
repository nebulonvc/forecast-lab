European Central Bank: Financial Stability Review, November 2026 (The Review was endorsed by the ECB’s Governing Council on 12 November 2026). The euro area economy has proved resilient so far this year, in the face of energy supply disruptions, trade frictions and extreme weather events. Sovereign vulnerabilities are also building up, and debt levels are high in many advanced economies, including some euro area countries. At the same time, sovereign funding costs have risen. Rising exposures to AI and sovereign debt warrant attention, with vulnerabilities being more pronounced in some segments of the non-bank financial sector. Even if banks are not exposed to a potential correction in AI-related assets in material terms, they could face spillovers via strong links with non-banks. Moreover, a prospective deterioration in macro-financial conditions due to the energy supply disruptions could lead to rising credit risk. Banks have weathered past shocks well overall, thanks in part to the regulatory reforms introduced since the global financial crisis. They remain strongly positioned to absorb shocks, with resilience underpinned by ample liquidity buffers, sizeable capital headroom and robust profitability. Although the euro area banking sector is healthy and credit dynamics are favourable, albeit unevenly so across countries, financial stability risks remain elevated.
