Source: Eurostat ( migr_asytpsm ), ( migr_asytpspop ), ( demo_gind) On 4 March 2022, the European Council unanimously adopted an implementing decision introducing temporary protection for people fleeing Ukraine as a consequence of the Russian war of aggression against Ukraine that began on 24 February 2022. On 15 July 2026, the Council extended temporary protection until 4 March 2028. Temporary protection is an exceptional measure to provide immediate and temporary protection to displaced people from non-EU countries and those unable to return to their country of origin. It applies when there is a risk that the standard asylum system will struggle to cope with demands stemming from a mass inflow, risking a negative impact on the processing of claims. This article presents the main developments for September 2026. Which EU countries do people fleeing Ukraine go to? At the end of September 2026, the EU countries hosting the highest number of beneficiaries of temporary protection from Ukraine were Germany (1 300 070 people; 29.7% of the EU total), Poland (858 545; 19.6%), and Czechia (391 720; 9.0%). The number of people under temporary protection relative to the EU population was 9.7 per 1 000 people at the end of September 2026. The highest ratios were observed in Czechia (35.9 per 1 000 people), ahead of Slovakia (28.0) and Cyprus (27.4) (Map 1). Compared with the end of August 2026, the number of people from Ukraine under temporary protection at the end of September 2026 decreased by 61 055 across the EU (-1.4%). However the number of people under temporary protection increased in 25 of the 27 EU countries. The largest absolute increases were observed in Germany (+5 515; +0.4%), Romania (+3 900; +1.8%) and Italy (+2 505; +4.4%). The 2 countries that registered decreases were Poland [ 1 ] (-80 850; -8.6%) and Czechia (-6 640; -1.7%).
