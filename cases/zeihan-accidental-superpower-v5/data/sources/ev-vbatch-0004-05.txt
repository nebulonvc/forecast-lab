Key statistics and trends in international trade 2025 | UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD), May 18, 2026
Global trade is expanding again, but the forces shaping it are undergoing significant change. Geopolitical tensions, evolving trade policies and rising uncertainty are altering trade patterns and restructuring global value chains. Fragmentation and uneven trajectories across regions and sectors are becoming defining characteristics of trade patterns.
Global trade in goods and services surpassed $35 trillion in 2025 – a new all-time high. Goods made up roughly three quarters of the total. Services accounted for the remainder. Goods trade grew by about 6.5% and drove most of the increase. Services grew faster, at almost 9%, continuing a long-term trend. Higher volumes rather than higher prices were behind most of the growth.
After two years of decline, globalization strengthened in 2025. At the same time, trade patterns are shifting: geopolitical tensions continue to reshape trade flows. These shifts point to a system that is less uniform and more fragmented, with a growing focus on resilience.
International integration has declined across most manufacturing sectors since 2019. Global trade reached a new peak in 2025, but the underlying structure of the system is changing.
