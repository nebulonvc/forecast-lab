IEA (2025), Global Energy Review 2025, IEA, Paris. Global coal demand grew by 1.2% in 2024 in energy terms, rising by around 67 million tonnes of coal equivalent (Mtce) (or in physical terms by 1.4% or 123 million tonnes). The electricity sector continues to drive coal demand, accounting for two-thirds of global consumption. In 2024, global coal power generation grew by nearly 1% to 10 700 TWh, a new high. A key driver was record temperatures, which pushed up electricity demand for cooling (especially with intense heatwaves in China and India). While coal remains the world’s largest source of power generation, its share in the electricity mix is falling: its current share (35%) is the lowest since the IEA was founded in 1974. The share of global coal demand consumed in Developing Asia rose again in 2024, reaching nearly four fifths, up from below two fifths in 2000. China’s influence in global coal market trends is unparalleled by any country for any type of fuel, with China’s share of global coal consumption now standing at 58%. In advanced economies, coal demand has halved from the peak seen in 2007 and 2024 saw another decline. In the United States, coal consumption fell by 4% (around 10 Mtce), though this is a significant slowdown compared with the 17% drop recorded in 2023. In the European Union, coal demand continued its decline as an increasing number of countries closed coal power plants. The United Kingdom, where the world’s first coal power plant was set up in 1882, joined the list of countries without coal power capacity in September 2024. EU coal power generation fell 15% in 2024 and total coal demand fell by over 10% or 21 Mtce.
