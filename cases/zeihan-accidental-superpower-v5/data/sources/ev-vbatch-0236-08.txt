Trump administration officials, and the president himself, have taken credit for a decline in fatal overdoses, citing a “sealed” or “secure” border and actions against cartels, including airstrikes on alleged drug boats. But overdose deaths were already falling when Trump took office, and experts told us there’s little evidence his enforcement policies have been major drivers of that trend. In some statements, the White House has also misleadingly referred to a 40% drop in overdose deaths “from the Biden-era peak” — without mentioning that more than half of the decline happened while Joe Biden was still president. The release of the latest drug-death figures from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention this month, which showed fatal overdoses continuing to decline, prompted celebratory statements from the White House. President Donald Trump’s immigration policies are “crushing the overdose crisis,” the White House said in a Nov. 16 news release , adding that “overdose deaths have now declined for 18 straight months under President Trump. After Democrats refused to secure the border and allowed cartels to flood American communities with poison, that pipeline is now being choked off.” “It’s because of President Trump’s common-sense policies, like securing our borders, dismantling and eliminating narco-terrorist organizations, and employing primary and early prevention efforts within our communities that thousands of American lives are being saved each year,” Sara Carter, director of the White House’s Office of National Drug Control Policy, said in a separate news release the same day. Those statements echo how Trump himself has talked about the fentanyl crisis in recent months. In rallies and other appearances before and after the Nov. 3 midterm elections, he’s repeatedly talked about reducing the flow of drugs over the southern border and striking alleged drug boats off the coast of Latin America.
