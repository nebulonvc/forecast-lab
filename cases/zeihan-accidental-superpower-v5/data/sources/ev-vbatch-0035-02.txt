Total natural gas industry consumptive water use in the Susquehanna River Basin between 2008 and 2018 was 26.3 billion gallons.
The natural gas industry is the third largest consumptive water user in the Susquehanna Basin following public water suppliers and electric power plants.
SRBC approved a total of 261 surface water withdrawal points for use by the natural gas industry between 2008 and 2018, the most recent analysis available.
Of those, 96 expired and were not renewed and fewer than 70 were actively used as water sources during any one year.
The natural gas industry held 37 approvals using water sources having compromised water quality, primarily the use of acid mine drainage (34) and three from wastewater plant discharges, and used less than one-third of those sources between 2008 and 2018 with a total withdrawal of about 865 million gallons.
The Commission has also approved 13 diversions of surface water for use by the natural gas industry originating in the Ohio River Basin. As of December 2018, only three of the approved diversions have been actively used.

A total of approximately 21.5 billion gallons of surface water were withdrawn by the natural gas industry and consumptively used during that period.
An additional 2.35 billion gallons of water was withdrawn from public drinking water systems for natural gas industry use.
A report on natural gas industry water use released by the Commission in June 2020 concludes, “Potential adverse impacts to water sources are focused on the quantity, timing and location of the withdrawals.”
SRBC’s report of oil and gas industry water use between 2008 and 2018 found hydraulically fractured wells in Pennsylvania used, on average about three times as much water in 2018 than they did in 2009 because the average length of laterals increased from 2,200 feet to 7,000 feet.
Water use per foot of well fractured also increased from 1,000 to 1,5000 gallons per foot to an industry average 2,200 gallons per foot.
SRBC said industry reported reusing flowback water in its operations, but that peaked in the third quarter of 2015 at 34 percent.
Overall, average water use per well for fracturing rose 600 percent between 2009 and 2018.
