FAO Cereal Supply and Demand Brief. Release date : 06/11/2026. Global cereal production remains high but trade set to contract on logistical constraints. At 2 999 million tonnes, world cereal production (including rice in milled equivalent) is forecast to retreat from the 2025 record in 2026, while remaining at the second-highest level on record. World cereal utilization in 2026/27 is forecast at 2 971 million tonnes, up 5.6 million tonnes, or 0.2 percent, from 2025/26. Food consumption accounts for most of the increase, with food use of both wheat and rice rising by just over 4.0 million tonnes, or 0.8 percent and 1.0 percent, respectively, slightly below the pace of global population growth. World cereal trade in 2026/27 is forecast to contract by 17.4 million tonnes, or 3.3 percent, to 507.7 million tonnes. An 18.1 million tonnes, or 8.6 percent, fall in wheat trade is the main driver, with shipments from all major exporters declining amid supply or logistical constraints. Barley trade is also expected to fall, while maize trade is forecast to increase by 4.5 million tonnes, or 2.2 percent, supported by large exportable supplies in Argentina. Moving in the opposite direction, rice trade is forecast to recover by 2.9 percent in 2027 to 61.6 million tonnes, underpinned by firmer import demand and increased shipments from India and Pakistan.
