Single Resolution Mechanism In March 2014, Parliament and the Council agreed on the second pillar of the Banking Union, the Single Resolution Mechanism (SRM). Its objective is to manage bank failures efficiently, with minimal costs to taxpayers and the real economy. When action is needed, a central authority – the Single Resolution Board (SRB) – decides whether or not to initiate the resolution of a bank. Operationally, the decision is implemented in cooperation with national resolution authorities. The SRB started its work as an independent EU agency in January 2015 and became fully operational in January 2016. In June 2017, the SRB adopted its first resolution decision in the case of Banco Popular. The SRB, however, decided not to take resolution action in June 2017 as regards Banca Popolare di Vicenza and Veneto Banca, in February 2018 as regards ABLV Bank AS and its subsidiary ABLV Bank Luxembourg S.A., and in August 2019 as regards AS PNB Banka. These banks became subject to national insolvency proceedings instead. The proposal builds on existing national deposit guarantee schemes and envisages the gradual introduction of a common system. The objective of EDIS is to strengthen confidence in the banking sector, reduce the risk of bank runs and boost the overall resilience of the Banking Union. The European Parliament’s rapporteur published her draft report on EDIS in November 2016. Subsequent discussions in Parliament and the Council revealed divergent positions. In order to facilitate progress, the Commission published an additional communication in October 2017, proposing some options for the design of EDIS . In December 2020, the three EU institutions agreed to include EDIS in the legislative priorities for 2021. Parliament’s Committee on Economic and Monetary Affairs (ECON Committee) adopted a proposal for the first stage of a European Deposit Insurance Scheme in April 2024. The report suggests the establishment of EDIS in three phases, and provides for the first phase only, ‘EDIS I’, with the aim of making progress towards a full insurance scheme with loss coverage at a later stage.
