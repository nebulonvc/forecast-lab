Poland and the United States have signed an agreement on defence research, development, testing and evaluation, concluding 17 years of negotiations and opening the way for deeper technological cooperation.
The agreement establishes a framework for joint defence projects, as well as the exchange of technical data and expertise, giving Poland – which is already a major importer of US arms – a formal role in the development of American defence technologies for the first time.

The agreement was signed at the Pentagon by Poland’s deputy defence minister Cezary Tomczyk and US under secretary of war for research and engineering Emil Michael.
According to the Department of War, the deal will help “turn research and development into validated military capabilities to jointly support US and Polish warfighters”.
Tomczyk said the agreement would allow Polish universities, research institutes and companies to participate in joint research and development work on defence technology with US partners and would enable such technologies to be tailored more closely to the requirements of the Polish armed forces.
During a briefing in Washington, Tomczyk admitted that the agreement had been negotiated for what he described as a “probably record-breaking” 17 years, but he noted a “significant acceleration in relations” between the US and Poland over the last six months.
General Marcin Górka, who oversees innovation in the Polish armed forces and accompanied Tomczyk to the United States, likewise welcomed the agreement, which he said would “open the doors to American military laboratories for our scientists and engineers”.
Górka also said the agreement would enable Poland and the United States to jointly develop anti-drone systems and advanced drones, with testing taking place in both countries.
Poland is a major importer of US military hardware. Between 2021 and 2025, it was the biggest overall importer of arms in NATO , with around 44% of that equipment coming from the US, including F-35 fighter jets , Abrams tanks , Patriot air defence systems and HIMARS rocket launchers .
But Poland has recently sought to move from being a client of the US defence industry to more of a partner. Earlier this month, Polish defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz and Secretary of War Pete Hegseth signed a declaration on expanding bilateral defence-industrial cooperation.
Poland and the US have signed a statement of intent on strengthening defence industry cooperation.
