World trade in goods and services - an overview (Eurostat Statistics Explained, Data extracted in January 2026). In 2024, international trade in goods and services was equivalent to 21.4% of the EU’s GDP. The EU’s share of world exports of goods and services was 17.0% in 2024 (this share relates solely to extra-EU trade, not to trade within the EU). In 2024, the EU exported more goods and services (€4.14 trillion) than any individual country and the EU recorded the second highest level of imports (€3.58 trillion), behind the United States (€3.82 trillion). Between 2014 and 2024, the ratio of international trade in goods and services relative to GDP across the EU ranged from a minimum of 20.0% (in 2020) to a maximum of 24.6% (in 2022). Between 2014 and 2016, the EU’s share of global exports increased, then fluctuated between 17.5% and 18.1% from 2016 to 2020. This share subsequently declined, falling to 17.3% in 2021 and 16.4% in 2022 – roughly the same level as a decade earlier – before recovering somewhat in 2023 and then dropping again to 17.0% in 2024. The EU’s share of world imports for goods and services was in the range of 15.2% to 15.6% between 2014 and 2018. This share subsequently increased in successive years to reach 16.2% in 2020 before fluctuating in 2021 (down to 15.8%) and 2022 (up to 16.5%). The latest information available shows that the EU’s share of world imports fell in successive years, down to 15.8% in 2023 and to 15.1% by 2024.
