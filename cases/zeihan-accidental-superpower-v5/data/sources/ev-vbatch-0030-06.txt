Among the EU countries, it ranged from 67.6% in Italy to 83.6% in Malta. In 2025, the EU employment rate for men stood at 80.9%, while it was at 71.3% for women, resulting in a gender employment gap of 9.6 percentage points. Between 2016 and 2025, the employment rate in the EU increased by 6.4 percentage points. This article presents the most recent EU annual statistics on employment based on the EU Labour force survey (EU-LFS) . It shows employment rates by sex, age and education attainment level. The article also presents information on the over-qualification rate among employed people with tertiary education. Employment in 2025 compared with the EU target In 2017, the European Commission, the European Parliament, and the Council jointly introduced the European Pillar of Social Rights action plan , aimed at promoting fair and efficient labour markets and welfare systems across the EU. To achieve this goal, the EU has set 3 targets for 2030, one of which is to attain a 78% employment rate for those aged 20-64 by 2030. In 2025, the number of employed people in that age group in the EU totalled around 198 million, accounting for 76.1% of all people in the age group. The employment rate has moved steadily closer to the EU target, increasing from 69.7% in 2016 to 76.1% in 2025.
