U.S. forces have completed their withdrawal from Erbil Air Base, formally ending the U.S. military mission in Iraq under Operation Inherent Resolve after over a decade of counter-ISIS operations, U.S. Central Command announced Wednesday. What we know: CENTCOM said the September 30 departure of personnel and equipment marks a planned shift toward a bilateral U.S.-Iraq defense relationship. The mission’s headquarters, Combined Joint Task Force-Operation Inherent Resolve, has relocated to Jordan, where U.S. forces will continue supporting counter-ISIS operations in Syria alongside coalition partners and the Syrian government. The backstory: CJTF-OIR was established in 2014 and has spent years advising, assisting and enabling Iraqi, Kurdish and Syrian partner forces in the fight against ISIS. (Photo by John Moore/Getty Images) What they're saying: "Job well done to the tens of thousands of American service members, diplomats, and civilian personnel who answered the call in Iraq," CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper said in a statement. "Because of your grit, sacrifice and professionalism, ISIS no longer poses a systemic threat to Iraq’s national security." Cooper said Iraq security forces, including the Peshmerga and other Kurdistan Region units, now have the capacity, leadership and operational independence to manage threats on their own. Big picture view: The drawdown began last October as U.S. and coalition forces started leaving federal Iraq. Erbil Air Base, located in northern Iraq, served as the primary hub for CJTF-OIR and hosted most of the roughly 1,500 American and coalition personnel supporting counter ISIS missions.
