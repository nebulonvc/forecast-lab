Congress will look fundamentally different when it convenes for a new session in January. Democratic gains in both the House and the Senate change the math on immigration, and recent comments by President Trump and Secretary Markwayne Mullin indicate a potential willingness to work on immigration reform.
Assuming immigration is a priority item in the new Congressional majority’s agenda, what kind of reforms should proposals include? I think the changes needed are in three broad categories: oversight, the economy, and security.
Accountability matters. Delayed or inaccurate release of immigration data, concerns about due process for immigrant detainees, and sharp declines in immigration application processing deserve scrutiny. Congress should undertake bipartisan oversight to restore trust in the immigration system.
Immigrants work, and America needs workers to continue to power the economy as our society ages and population growth slows. Workers who have been contributing for years need a permanent legislative solution, and Congress should keep the economy top of mind if it tackles future legal migration flows.
And finally, Congress should still consider border security improvements. Trade and travel are vital to our border regions. Irregular migration is low now, but it could return if there is an economic or security crisis in the Western Hemisphere. Congress should continue to appropriate funding to ensure our ports of entry can securely and efficiently move people and goods, while building the appropriate capacity to compassionately handle the next humanitarian crisis.
America’s immigration policy should be both lawful and welcoming. I hope the next Congress works with the Trump Administration to deliver this.
