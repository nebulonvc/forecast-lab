ILO-UNICEF report identifies key enablers of women's employment in wartime Ukraine | International Labour Organization Skip to main content ILO-Unicef ILO-UNICEF report identifies key enablers of women's employment in wartime Ukraine The ILO and UNICEF have presented the findings of a joint study demonstrating how access to early childhood education and care services, working arrangements and social norms affect women’s participation in the labour market in the context of the full-scale war. 28 May 2026 Facebook X Linkedin Panelists of an ILO-UNICEF joint report launch in Kyiv, Ukraine in May, 2026. © ILO Content also available in: Українська Kyiv (ILO News) – The study was conducted in response to changes in the labour market caused by the full-scale war and its impact on women’s participation in employment. In response to challenges in sustaining resilience and supporting the country’s economic recovery, the findings show that women’s participation in the labour market is shaped by three interrelated factors: the availability and accessibility of childcare services, flexible working arrangements, and social norms and stereotypes. Constraints in any of these areas reduce women’s ability to work, even where labour demand exists. The launch event of the report brought together representatives of the Ministry of Economy, Environment and Agriculture of Ukraine, the Ministry of Social Policy, Family and Unity of Ukraine, the Ministry of Education and Science of Ukraine, Parliament, employers’ and workers’ organisations, civil society organizations, UN agencies, development partners and other stakeholders to discuss the study’s findings in the context of ongoing labour market shifts and Ukraine’s economic recovery priorities. Expanding women’s economic opportunities is essential for Ukraine’s recovery and for addressing the labour shortages intensified by the war. Aida Lindmeier, Director of the ILO Country Office for Ukraine “Expanding women’s economic opportunities is essential for Ukraine’s recovery and for addressing the labour shortages intensified by the war. When women have access to high-quality early childhood education and care services, combined with decent work, it strengthens the economic resilience of families, communities, and the country as a whole. For the ILO, it is imperative that Ukraine’s recovery is grounded in the principles of decent work, equality, and inclusive development,” said Aida Lindmeier, Director of the ILO Country Office for Ukraine.
