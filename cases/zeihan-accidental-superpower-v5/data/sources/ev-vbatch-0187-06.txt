April 30, 2024. Challenges to Chinese blue-water operations By Mike Sweeney. Key points Despite having the world’s largest navy, important questions can be asked about China’s ability to challenge the U.S. Navy on a global scale. A number of factors—geography, logistical infrastructure, force structure, and command culture—all argue that China cannot do so at this time. In particular, China would need to significantly expand the number and caliber of its overseas bases in order to support large-scale, blue-water operations by the People’s Liberation Army Navy (PLAN). China currently has just two overseas bases—at Djibouti and Cambodia—and both are of limited capacity. While Chinese naval aviation has shown important improvements over the last year, the PLAN does not appear to have the logistical capacity to sustain high-tempo carrier operations outside the First Island Chain for an extended period of time. Introduction In terms of raw ship numbers, the People’s Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) is now the world’s largest fleet. Current estimates by the Congressional Research Service (CRS) assign the U.S. Navy a force of 291 ships while the most recent estimate from the U.S. Department of Defense (DoD) gives China a force of 370 ships.
