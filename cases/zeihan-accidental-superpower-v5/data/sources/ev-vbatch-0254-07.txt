2026-11-13. The number of irregular border crossings into the European Union continued to fall in the first ten months of 2026, with detections down by 29% compared with the same period last year. Almost 110 000 crossings were recorded, according to preliminary data collected by Frontex. The Eastern Mediterranean was the busiest route in the first ten months of the year, with around 38 200 crossings recorded, a fall of 12% compared with the same period last year. The main nationalities detected were Sudanese, Bangladeshi and Afghan. The Central Mediterranean recorded around 26 600 arrivals, 55% fewer than in the same period last year and the steepest fall of all routes. Libya remained the main departure hub, accounting for around three quarters of arrivals on the route, ahead of the Tunisian and Algerian corridors. Crossings from Algeria to Italy, while far fewer, have grown steadily and reached a monthly record in September, with unprecedented activity towards Sardinia. The main nationalities detected were Bangladeshi, Algerian and Somali. The Western Mediterranean was the only major route to register an increase, with around 19 700 detections, up by 21% year-on-year. Algeria remained the main departure country for sea crossings, with the Balearic Islands and the Spanish mainland the principal destinations. The main nationalities detected were Algerian, Moroccan and Malian.
