Key datapoints from the report include: Nuclear reactors worldwide generated 2702 TWh of electricity in 2025, up 35 TWh on 2024 and setting a new annual record for global nuclear generation. Since 1970, nuclear generation is estimated* to have avoided 43.6 billion tonnes of CO₂ compared with gas-fired generation and 97.2 billion tonnes compared with coal-fired generation. In 2025, the global nuclear fleet demonstrated its high reliability with an average capacity** factor of 83.7%, continuing the trend of high capacity factors since 2000. National nuclear targets, identified projects and continued operation of the existing fleet would deliver approximately 1457 GWe of nuclear capacity by 2050, exceeding the tripling goal by more than 200 GWe. Of the projected total global nuclear capacity, 1303 GWe is in countries where nuclear reactors already operate. New entrant countries are projected to reach a combined nuclear capacity of 154 GWe by 2050. New capacity under construction increased to 82 GWe as eleven reactors started construction in 2025. Combined planned, proposed and potential capacity increased to 416 GWe, reducing the gap between actual projects and government targets. Growth in nuclear capacity to 2030 is expected to come primarily from reactors currently under construction, adding 54 GWe by 2030. A further 28 GWe of capacity now under construction is expected to enter operation by 2035.
