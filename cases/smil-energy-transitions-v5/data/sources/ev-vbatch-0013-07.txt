This page was first published in July 2020. We revised this page in August 2026 to reflect the change in how primary energy is measured. Data from 1965 onwards comes from the latest release of Energy Institute’s Statistical Review of World Energy. Approximately one-seventh of global primary energy comes from low-carbon sources. Low-carbon sources are the sum of nuclear energy and renewables – which includes hydropower, wind, solar, bioenergy, geothermal, and wave and tidal. We continue to produce more energy from fossil fuels – particularly oil and gas – each year. Solar energy is a relatively new energy technology but is growing very quickly.
