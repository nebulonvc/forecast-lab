Energy use: how Sweden powers a low-carbon economy (sweden.se, citing Swedish Energy Agency and Statistics Sweden): Nearly two-thirds of the energy used in Sweden comes from renewables. This makes Sweden the leading user of renewable energy in the EU, according to Eurostat. Sweden’s extensive use of renewable sources such as biomass, water and wind, in combination with nuclear power, makes it possible to maintain relatively low carbon emissions – despite the fact that people in Sweden consume a lot of energy. Sweden’s energy use at a glance: Energy use per person and year: ~35 MWh (EU: ~24 MWh); Renewable energy share: 63% (2024); Fossil fuel-free electricity: 98.8% (2024). Nuclear power is the third largest contributor to Sweden’s national electricity production, representing around 30 per cent. The six nuclear reactors currently in operation are distributed between three plants and are expected to remain in operation until 2040–2045. The Swedish Energy Agency concludes that nuclear power is one of the fossil-fuel free power sources needed to achieve electrification and a fossil-fuel free energy system overall.
