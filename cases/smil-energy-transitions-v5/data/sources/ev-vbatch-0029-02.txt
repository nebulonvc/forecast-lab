Uncovering Degradation Mechanisms in Bifacial PV Modules: Insights From Five Years of Field Operation. National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR), Golden, CO (United States), DOE OSTI ID 17426227, DOI: 10.1109/JPHOTOV.2026.3732754, published 2026-10-06. At the National Laboratory of the Rockies' 75-kW bifacial single-axis-tracked field, four out of five bifacial silicon photovoltaic (PV) module technologies exhibit accelerated degradation compared to their monofacial counterparts. Most cases point to carrier lifetime degradation causing simultaneous open-circuit voltage (Voc) and short-circuit current (Isc) loss. In some cases, Isc further decreases because of optical effects from encapsulant degradation. Fill factor losses are also observed from effects, such as grid finger disconnections, delamination of bussing wires, and broad-area contact nonuniformity. These results represent a snapshot of bifacial module performance from products available in the 2018-2019 time frame when the field test was initiated. More recent bifacial modules may have different degradation characteristics.
