European Commission — Energy Efficiency Directive (EU/2023/1791). To support the EU’s 2030 target of reducing greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% -compared to 1990-, the Commission revised the Energy Efficiency Directive (EU/2023/1791) in 2023. It significantly raises the EU’s ambition on energy efficiency and sets a target of reducing the final energy consumption by at least 11.7% compared to the 2020 projections for energy use in 2030. Full implementation of the directive will also be key for the EU to comply with the commitment of the Global Pledge to double the global rate of energy efficiency improvements from about 2% to over 4% by 2030. It establishes ‘energy efficiency first’ as a fundamental principle of EU energy policy, giving it legal-standing. It means that energy efficiency must be considered by EU countries in all relevant policy and major investment decisions taken, in both the energy and non-energy sectors. The revision followed a Commission proposal for a recast directive in 2021, as part of the European Green Deal. This proposal was further enhanced in the REPowerEU plan of May 2022, aiming to decrease the EU’s dependence on Russian fossil fuel. On 10 March 2026, the Commission published practical guidance on the establishment of one-stop shops for the energy renovation of buildings, as part of a comprehensive support package for energy efficiency financing. The deadline for transposition was 11 October 2025.
