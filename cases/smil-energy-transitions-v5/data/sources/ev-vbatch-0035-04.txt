Energy efficiency statistics - Statistics Explained - Eurostat (updated 30 October 2026). In 2024, primary energy consumption in the EU hit a record low of 1 202 Mtoe, or 21.1% away from the 2030 target. Primary energy consumption in the EU dropped sharply to 1 237 million tonnes of oil equivalent (Mtoe) in 2020, outperforming the 2020 efficiency target by 5.7%. The year 2021 clearly demonstrated this challenge, as primary energy consumption increased compared with 2020 by 6.2%, reaching 1 314 Mtoe. In 2022 primary energy consumption dropped to 1 260 Mtoe, while in 2023 it further decreased to 1 212 Mtoe. This slow decreasing trend continued in 2024, with primary energy consumption at 1 202 Mtoe, or 0.8% lower than in 2023. Primary energy consumption peaked in 2006 and in 2024 it was 20.4% lower than the peak value. The first Energy Efficiency Directive was adopted in 2012. With the availability of data for year 2024, we can compare the latest data on energy consumption with those 10 years ago - two years after the Directive was adopted. When comparing 2024 with 2014, primary energy consumption decreased in the Union by 9.6%. In several EU countries primary energy consumption decreased even more than that, most notably in Estonia (-21.2%), Germany (-21.0%) and the Netherlands (-15.5%). On the other side, in Croatia and Lithuania there was a double-digit increase in primary energy consumption. Final energy consumption of the EU in 2024 was 2.1% lower than in 2014, with several countries registering more pronounced decreases.
