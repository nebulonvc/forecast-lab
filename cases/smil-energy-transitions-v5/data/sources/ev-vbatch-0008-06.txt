This increase boosts the share of renewables in final energy consumption to nearly 20% by 2030, up from 13% in 2023. Electricity generation from renewable energy sources makes up more than three-quarters of the overall rise, owing to continued policy support in more than 130 countries, declining costs and the expanding use of electricity for road transport and heat pumps. Renewable fuels, including liquid, gaseous and solid bioenergy as well as hydrogen and e-fuels, account for near 15% of the forecast growth in renewable energy demand. These fuels expand the quickest in areas not amenable to electrification (e.g. the aviation and marine sectors) and offer energy access in rural areas and in industries with readily available biomass (e.g. sugar and ethanol, and pulp and paper). Other renewable energy, such as solar thermal and geothermal, accounts for the remaining 10% of growth. Notes and sources Other includes geothermal, concentrated solar power, and tidal for electricity generation and geothermal, solar thermal, district heating (primarily bioenergy) and ambient heat for Heat.
