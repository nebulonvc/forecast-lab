The International Energy Agency has published its World Energy Outlook 2026 amid an unprecedented global energy crisis that is reshaping how countries think about energy security. The report finds that the push to reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels is accelerating electrification and the shift toward domestic energy sources, including renewables. Yet electricity demand is surging, grids are struggling to keep pace, and concerns about energy security are also giving coal a longer life in some countries. While the world's reliance on fossil fuels hasn't changed substantially, there's been a real shift in how countries think about the risks of that dependence — and the benefits of electrification. But progress is too slow. The IEA projects that under current and proposed policies, electricity will account for only 29% of final energy consumption by 2035. That's only half the increase needed to reach the COP31 target of 35%.
