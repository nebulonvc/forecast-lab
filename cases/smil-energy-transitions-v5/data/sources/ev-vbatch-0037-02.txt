U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), U.S. energy facts explained — Consumption and production. Fossil fuels have dominated the U.S. energy mix for more than 100 years, but the mix has changed over time. Petroleum's share of total U.S. energy consumption peaked in the 1970s. In 1978, total petroleum consumption was about 49%, or 37.9 quadrillion British thermal units (quads) of total U.S. energy consumption. U.S. petroleum consumption decreased in 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic and increased in subsequent years as the economy returned to pre-pandemic activity. In 2025, petroleum's share of total U.S. energy consumption was about 38% (35.9 quads). Natural gas consumption has increased both in amount and share of U.S. energy consumption. In 1950, natural gas consumption was about 18% (5.97 quads) of total U.S. primary energy consumption, and in 2025, natural gas consumption was about 36% (34.6 quads) of total U.S. primary energy consumption. Renewable energy production and consumption both reached record highs in 2025: production was about 9% (9.2 quads) of total primary energy production, and consumption was about 9% (8.8 quads) of total primary energy consumption. These increases have primarily been driven by large increases in solar and wind energy production. Coal’s share of total U.S. energy consumption declined from about 37% in 1950 to 9% in 2025, largely because the U.S. electric power sector increased its use of other energy sources in place of coal.
