Trends in Income and Wealth Inequality | Pew Research Center. Report | October 14, 2026. By Juliana Menasce Horowitz and Richard Fry. Note: Wealth inequality figures were updated on Nov. 17, 2026, to reflect the newest available data. Income and wealth have grown for each group. But the gains have been greatest for upper-income households, leading to increased economic inequality. The median income for upper-income households increased 25% between 2001 and 2024 (from $222,800 to $278,800). Incomes for middle- and lower-income households grew less over that period – 22% and 17%, respectively. In 2024, upper-income households had 7.3 times the typical income of lower-income households. The ratio was 6.8 in 2001. Wealth inequality is up sharply from 2001. Wealth is spread much less evenly across U.S. households than income, and the gap in wealth between upper- and lower-income households has grown wider between 2001 and 2025. Source: Pew Research Center analysis of the Survey of Consumer Finances. Over that period, median wealth increased for middle- and upper-income households and remained mostly flat for lower-income households. Upper-income households saw the largest gains, with wealth increasing 90% (from $809,700 to $1,537,300). Wealth increased 27% for middle-income households and only 2% for lower-income households. All told, upper-income households owned about 58 times the median wealth of lower-income households in 2025. That gap has increased from 31 times in 2001.
