Earnings of Employed and Self-employed US Health Care Professionals, 2001 to 2015 (JAMA Network Open, June 1, 2018; PMID 30646085). Over the last 15 years, the health care practitioner landscape has changed significantly. Fewer practitioners are self-employed and more are employed by for-profit or nonprofit organizations. Survey study in which data on employment type (self-employed, employed by private sector, or employed by government) and annual labor earnings for 50 states and the District of Columbia were extracted from the 2001 to 2015 American Community Survey. Our sample of 175 714 respondents included 99 077 physicians. The weighted percentage of self-employed physicians decreased from 35.2% (95% CI, 34.4%-36.1%; 6807 of 18 726 physicians) in 2001 through 2005 to 24.7% (95% CI, 24.2%-25.2%; 10 974 of 41 205 physicians) in 2011 through 2015. Among physicians, the regression-adjusted earnings gap reversed from $19 679 (95% CI, $14 431-$24 927; P < .001) during 2001 through 2005 to -$10 623 (95% CI, -$14 547 to -$6699; P < .001) during 2011 through 2015. Since 2001, the percentage of health care professionals who are self-employed declined, and the gap in earnings between self-employed and employed health care professionals narrowed.
