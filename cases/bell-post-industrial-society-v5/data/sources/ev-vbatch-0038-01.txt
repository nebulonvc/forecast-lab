U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Handbook of Labor Statistics, Bulletin 2175 (December 1983), Table 16. Employed civilians by sex, race, Hispanic origin, and occupation, 1958-82 (Numbers in thousands; annual averages, 16 years and over, adjusted to 1980 Census population controls). Total employed civilians: 1974 = 86,794; 1975 = 85,846; 1976 = 88,752; 1977 = 92,017; 1978 = 96,048; 1979 = 98,824; 1980 = 99,303; 1981 = 100,397; 1982 = 99,526. Farm workers total (Farmers and farm managers + Farm laborers and supervisors): 1974 = 3,064 (1,647 + 1,417), representing 3.5 percent of total employed; 1975 = 2,955 (3.5 percent); 1976 = 2,845 (3.2 percent); 1977 = 2,783 (3.0 percent); 1978 = 2,829 (3.0 percent); 1979 = 2,737 (2.8 percent); 1980 = 2,741 (1,493 farmers and farm managers + 1,247 farm laborers and supervisors), representing 2.8 percent of total employed (2.76 percent unrounded). Operatives total (Operatives, except transport + Transport equipment operatives): 1974 = 14,038 (10,719 + 3,319), representing 16.2 percent of total employed; 1975 = 13,003 (15.2 percent); 1976 = 13,532 (15.3 percent); 1977 = 14,034 (15.3 percent); 1978 = 14,848 (15.5 percent); 1979 = 14,782 (15.0 percent); 1980 = 14,096 (10,565 except transport + 3,531 transport equipment), representing 14.2 percent of total employed; 1981 = 14,016 (14.0 percent). Between 1974 and 1980, total civilian employment increased by 12,509 thousand (+14.41 percent), whereas total operatives employment increased by only 58 thousand (+0.41 percent). Between 1974 and 1980, total farm workers declined from 3,064 thousand (3.5 percent of total employed) to 2,741 thousand (2.8 percent of total employed, or 2.76 percent unrounded).
