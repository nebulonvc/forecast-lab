Diane N. Westcott and Robert W. Bednarzik, "Employment and unemployment: a report on 1980," Monthly Labor Review, February 1981, Bureau of Labor Statistics, pp. 4-14. Table 3. Employment status of selected worker categories, 1978-80 (Numbers in thousands). Annual averages (1978, 1979, 1980): Employed, 16 years and over: 94,373; 96,945; 97,270. White-collar workers: 47,205; 49,342; 50,809 (1980 IV seasonally adjusted quarterly average: 51,105). Professional and technical workers: 14,245; 15,050; 15,613. Managers and administrators, except farm: 10,105; 10,516; 10,919. Sales workers: 5,951; 6,163; 6,172. Clerical workers: 16,904; 17,613; 18,105. Blue-collar workers: 31,531; 32,066; 30,800 (1980 IV seasonally adjusted quarterly average: 30,481). Service workers: 12,839; 12,834; 12,958. Farm workers: 2,798; 2,703; 2,704. Occupations. The decline in employment during 1980 was concentrated in blue-collar occupations. Total blue-collar employment dropped 1.7 million between the fourth quarter of 1979 and the third quarter of 1980 and had only just begun to edge up during the final quarter as production levels increased. In contrast, white-collar jobs continued to grow steadily during 1980, increasing 2.4 percent to 51.1 million over the year.
