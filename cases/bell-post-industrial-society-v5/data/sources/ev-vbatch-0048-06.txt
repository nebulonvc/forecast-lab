Title: Private equity and the corporatization of health care: implications for pulmonary, critical care, and sleep medicine. Journal: Annals of the American Thoracic Society (epub 2026-03-24). Body Excerpt: The corporatization of health care refers to the increasing influence of financial markets, motives, and institutions on the delivery and structure of health care. Corporatization results in a system driven primarily by profit, investment returns, and economic metrics. Examples include private equity (PE) ownership of health care organizations and nonprofit hospitals serving as investment vehicles with large stock portfolios. A slew of financial actors, including for-profit and corporate investors, have consolidated health care organizations over the past several decades. Most notable are PE firms that have accelerated acquisitions in the U.S. health care system, including broad investments in PCCSM. PE currently represents just 7.5% of all nongovernmental and one-third of for-profit hospitals. Concerns have been raised about this business model because PE owners often pursue more aggressive, short-term profit strategies compared with other non-PE for-profit owners. In a study using a difference-in-differences design between PE-acquired hospitals and control hospitals, PE hospitals had increased hospital-acquired conditions (eg, falls, central-line associated infections) by approximately 25% and surgical site infections doubled compared to hospitals not acquired by PE.
