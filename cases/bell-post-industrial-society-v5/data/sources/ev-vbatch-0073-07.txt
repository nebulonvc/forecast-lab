NASA's budget peaked during the Apollo program in the 1960s. After the United States won the race to the Moon, space exploration lost political support, and NASA's budget was cut significantly. Since the 1970s, NASA has accounted for, on average, 0.71% of annual U.S. government spending. Since the 2010s, that value has been between 0.4% and 0.3%. NASA's inflation-adjusted appropriation (enacted) levels represent a diminishingly small portion of the annual expenditures by the U.S. government, dropping by a factor of four in the last 30 years. Inflation-adjustment via NASA's New Start Index (NNSI).
