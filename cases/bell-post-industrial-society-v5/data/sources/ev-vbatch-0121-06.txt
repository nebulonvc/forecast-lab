Regulators' Budget: An Analysis of the U.S. Budget for Fiscal Years 1960 to 2024. Finn Dobkin and Matias Vesperoni, GW Regulatory Studies Center, May 2026. This report tracks the spending and staffing of 77 federal departments and agencies from Fiscal Year (FY) 1960 to FY 2024. While overall regulatory spending and staffing have continued to rise in recent years, this growth has been uneven across agencies and policy areas. Social regulation and homeland security drive most of the expansion, whereas economic regulation grew at a more modest rate over the same period. Table 1 (Constant Real 2012 Dollars, Millions of Dollars in Outlays): Total Social Regulation rose from $2,666 (FY 1960) and $6,148 (FY 1970) to $16,318 (FY 1980), $18,917 (FY 1990), $27,918 (FY 2000), $47,664 (FY 2010), $58,069 (FY 2020), and $69,937 (FY 2024). Total Economic Regulation rose from $1,348 (FY 1960) and $2,728 (FY 1970) to $3,360 (FY 1980), $4,354 (FY 1990), $5,855 (FY 2000), $8,503 (FY 2010), $11,341 (FY 2020), and $13,156 (FY 2024), while Industry-Specific Regulation declined from $1,546 (FY 1970) to $1,310 (FY 1980) and $872 (FY 1990) before reaching $1,667 (FY 2024). GRAND TOTAL constant 2012 dollar outlays rose from $4,014 (FY 1960) and $8,876 (FY 1970) to $19,678 (FY 1980), $33,774 (FY 2000), $69,410 (FY 2020), and $83,093 (FY 2024; $102,264 million in nominal dollars). In FY 2024, Social Regulation constituted 84.1% of the government regulatory expenditures tracked here. Between FY 2020 and FY 2024, Economic Regulation has declined from 16.3% of all regulatory outlays down to 15.8% ($11.3 billion to $13.2 billion).
