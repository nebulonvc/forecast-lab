Instructions per second (IPS) is a measure of a computer's processor speed. The term "mega instructions per second" became useful in the late 1970s. The IBM System/370 model 158-3 and the VAX-11/780 were considered roughly equivalent at 1 MIPS. The integer-and-string-heavy Dhrystone benchmark of 1984 inherited the idea of using VAX as a MIPS reference. Its results were reported in "DMIPS", for Dhrystone MIPS. Timeline of instructions per second (selected processors): Intel 8080: 0.290 MIPS at 2.000 MHz (1974); Intel i486DX: 11.1 MIPS at 33 MHz (1991); Intel Pentium III: 2,054 MIPS at 600 MHz (1999); Intel Core 2 Extreme QX9770 (4-core): 59,455 MIPS at 3.2 GHz (2008); Intel Core i7 3770K (4-core): 106,924 MIPS at 3.9 GHz (2012); Intel Core i9-9900K (8-core): 412,090 MIPS at 4.7 GHz (2018); Texas Instruments Sitara AM64x ARM Cortex-A53 (2-core): 5,992 MIPS at 1 GHz (2021).
