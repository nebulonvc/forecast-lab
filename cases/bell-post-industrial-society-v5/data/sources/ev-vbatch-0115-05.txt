A century of extreme poverty: five facts from 1950 to 2050. Christoph Lakner, Samuel Kofi Tetteh Baah. October 26, 2026. Published on Data Blog (World Bank). The global extreme poverty rate fell from about 60% in 1950 to 10% in 2025, but progress has slowed sharply in the past decade and future trajectories remain uncertain. 1. The world has made remarkable progress in reducing extreme poverty in the past 75 years. Between 1950 and 2025, the world has experienced remarkable progress in reducing the proportion of the population living in extreme poverty from 60 percent to 10 percent. The world has made remarkable progress in reducing extreme poverty over the past 75 years, despite the global population more than tripling (from 2.5 billion to 8.3 billion). Today, more than 800 million people are living in extreme poverty; that is, they live on less than $3.00 a day (2021 PPP). 4. The historic progress in reducing extreme poverty in the world has stalled in the last decade. In the past decade, progress in reducing extreme poverty has stalled amid a polycrisis, including the COVID-19 pandemic, climate-related shocks, geopolitical tensions, resulting in rising extreme poverty in the Middle East and stagnation in Sub-Saharan Africa.
