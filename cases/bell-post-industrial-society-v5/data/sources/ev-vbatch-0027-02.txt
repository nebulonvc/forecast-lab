Title: Elites moved toward democrats more than nonelites moved away: Income, education, and occupational class in US presidential elections, 1980–2020

Abstract: SignificanceRecent work on partisan realignment has often highlighted shifts among the White working class. Examining elections from 1980 to 2020, we show that the partisan realignment of White voters has been primarily driven by higher status Whites—those with the highest decile household incomes, college degrees, and white-collar jobs—making large moves toward the Democratic Party. Working-class Whites, however, show no clear long-term trend. They have indeed become less Democratic since 2012, but these levels remain within historically observed bounds.

Like previous researchers, we find that the income gradient on voting has decreased over time such that income no longer has much if any power to predict vote choice. Meanwhile, the gradient on education has reversed and become quite steep with more educated people voting more Democratic.
