Artificial intelligence (AI) has quickly become part of Americans’ everyday lives, even as many express concerns about its broader economic consequences. According to data from PRRI’s 2026 American Values Survey , two in ten Americans report using AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Copilot at least once a day or more, although its use varies across demographic groups. [1] At the same time, most Americans believe the rapid expansion of AI will benefit big corporations while hurting American workers. While AI is increasingly used for a variety of purposes, its use in religious life remains relatively limited, with only one in ten Americans reporting they use AI for Bible study, prayer, spiritual guidance, or other religious purposes at least once a day or more. This Spotlight Analysis examines Americans’ views and use of artificial intelligence, including how frequently they use AI, who is most likely to use it, and the extent to which Americans are incorporating AI into their religious practice.
Most Americans report having used artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or Copilot (72%) over the past year, including more than four in ten who report using AI at least once a week. Among weekly AI users, two in ten use it once a day (8%) or several times a day (12%), while two in ten say they use AI once a week (8%) or multiple times a week (14%). Three in ten Americans say they have at least used AI a few times in the past year, including 10% who use it once or twice a month, 5% a few times a year, and 15% who seldom use it. About one-quarter of Americans (24%) say they have not used AI tools in the past year.
There are very few differences in AI use by party affiliation, with around two in ten Democrats (21%), independents (21%), and Republicans (20%) reporting using AI at least once a day.
Non-Christians (28%) and Black Protestants (27%) are the most likely to say they use AI at least once a day or more, compared with about two in ten members of all other religious groups. One in ten non-Christians (12%) say they never use AI, compared with one-quarter of all other religious traditions.
A slim majority of AAPI Americans (52%) say they use AI at least once a day, while 22% of Black, 18% of white, and 18% of Hispanic Americans report similar AI use frequency. By contrast, Hispanic Americans (26%), white (25%), and Black Americans (25%) are about three times as likely as AAPI Americans to report not using AI at all (8%).
Looking at Americans by age, Americans ages 65 and older are the least likely to use AI, with only 10% reporting they use it at least once a day, compared with about one in four Americans ages 18-29 (21%), ages 30-49 (26%), and ages 50-64 (23%).
