Deficits, Debt, and the Economy: An Introduction
Updated November 5, 2026
The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) projects that the federal government will record a deficit of $1.85 trillion in FY2026, equivalent to 5.8% of GDP. From FY1976 to FY2025, the average net deficit equaled 3.6% of annual GDP ($1.22 trillion in FY2026 dollars). Over the FY1976-FY2025 period, the government generated a surplus on four occasions—in each year from FY1998 through FY2001. In all other years, the federal government incurred a net deficit.
Federal debt may be divided into two major categories: (1) debt held by the public, which is the sum of accrued net deficits and outstanding money from federal credit programs; and (2) intragovernmental debt. As of August 31, 2026, the amount of federal debt outstanding was $40.2 trillion, with 80.7% of that debt held by the public and 19.3% held as intragovernmental debt.
Budget deficits are the principal contributor to debt held by the public. To finance budget deficits, Treasury sells debt instruments. The value of those debt holdings (which include interest payments) represents the vast majority of publicly held debt. From FY1976 to FY2025, annual nominal budget deficits and surpluses of the federal government summed to $27.2 trillion; over the same period, total debt held by the public increased by $29.8 trillion.

Though there have been gradual changes in the level of intragovernmental debt in recent decades, the decline in real debt following World War II and subsequent increase in debt levels beginning in the late 1970s were each caused primarily by similar changes in the stock of publicly held debt over those time periods.
From FY1976 to FY2025, net interest payments averaged 2.1% of annual GDP, equivalent to about $678 billion annually in 2026 dollars. High interest rates and increasing debt levels caused the net interest burden to peak in the 1980s and 1990s. Until recently, net interest payments had been lower than their long-term averages; however, in FY2026, net interest payments are projected to be $1.039 trillion, or 3.3% of GDP. Recent net interest payments were the product of sustained low interest rates and relatively high levels of real debt. Unless the federal debt is reduced, net interest payments will increase if the recent shift away from a low interest rate environment persists. In its most recent forecast, the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) projects that real net interest payments will increase to 4.6% of GDP by FY2036.

The primary deficit averaged 1.7% of GDP from FY1976 to FY2025, as compared to the average total budget deficit of 3.8% of GDP recorded over the same time period. While the federal government recorded a budget surplus four times from FY1976 to FY2025, in seven other years it registered a primary surplus, most recently in 2007.
