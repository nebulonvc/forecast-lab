Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies (Riordan Frost, October 8, 2026): The Outsized Role of Immigrants in the Construction Trades.
Immigrants play a disproportionately large role in the construction trades in the US. Over a third of construction trades workers were foreign-born in 2024, compared with nearly a fifth of the overall labor force. This outsized role has grown as immigrants led the recovery in the trades workforce after the Great Recession, from the post-recession trough in the workforce in 2013 through 2024. The foreign-born role has been particularly disproportionate—and has grown particularly quickly—in lower-wage trades, which aligns with the demographic characteristics of the foreign-born workers in the trades workforce. The foreign-born share of trades workers exceeds their share of the labor force in nearly every metro area, a pattern that has held over the past decade.
Net international migration fell sharply in 2025, however, and is projected to fall further in 2026 (US Census Bureau 2026). Even before the slowdown, nearly all the growth in the foreign-born trades workforce was among middle-aged and older workers.
