An evaluation of BLS' projections of 1980 industry employment. John Tschetter, Monthly Labor Review, August 1984, U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. One trend that has characterized employment over the past several decades is the movement from the goods-producing sector (agriculture, mining, construction, and manufacturing industries) to the service-producing sector (transportation, communication, public utilities, finance, trade, other services, and government industries excluding military). The projections slightly underestimated this shift. In the 1970 and 1973 projections, the service-producing sector was projected to account for 69.6 percent of all civilian jobs in 1980, and in 1976 projections, 69.9 percent. In 1980, 70.8 percent of all civilian jobs were in the service-producing sector. The difference for the most part can be attributed to the unanticipated 1980 recession. Table 2 reports actual 1980 total employment (including military) of 105,920 thousand, with agriculture at 2,860 thousand, mining at 723 thousand, construction at 5,865 thousand, durable manufacturing at 12,423 thousand, nondurable manufacturing at 8,250 thousand, and service-producing industries (transportation, communication, public utilities, trade, finance and real estate, services, and civilian government) accounting for the remaining majority of civilian jobs.
