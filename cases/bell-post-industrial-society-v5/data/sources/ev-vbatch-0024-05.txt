James Hungarian Educational Research Journal , v16 n2 p171-195 2026 Universities have long been heralded as bastions of open inquiry and democratic discourse, yet their institutional autonomy and faculty members' academic freedom are increasingly imperilled. Rising political polarisation, shifting funding landscapes, and the erosion of traditional faculty protections have reshaped the contours of institutional autonomy and academic freedom across public research-intensive universities in the United States. This study examines how institutional size, financial resources, and federal- and state-level politics may have influenced the proportion of tenure-line faculty in the third year of the Obama (2014), Trump (2018), and Biden (2023) administrations. Drawing on IPEDS data for R1 and R2 public institutions, we first chart descriptive trends in enrollment, funding, and tenure-line faculty composition. We then employ bivariate correlations to explore linear associations and multivariate OLS regressions to isolate the unique effects of (under)graduate enrollment, state appropriations, federal grants, and political context (federal party affiliation and state trifecta control). Our findings reveal that while institutional size and federal research funding remain robust predictors of tenure-line staffing, the protective power of state appropriations has diminished, and partisan configurations--especially unified Democratic control--emerge as significant factors in certain years.
