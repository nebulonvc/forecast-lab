The ‘leisure gap’ between mothers and fathers. By Wendy Wang, Pew Research Center, October 17, 2013. In America, fathers, on average, have about three hours more leisure time per week than mothers. This “leisure gap” has been consistent at least over the past decade. What are dads doing with their extra time? For the most part, they’re watching TV, according to new Pew Research Center analysis of data from the government-sponsored American Time Use Survey (ATUS). Using the narrower definition of leisure, our analysis of the 2010 ATUS data finds that fathers with children under age 18 in the household on average spend about three hours more leisure time than mothers (27.5 hours per week vs. 24.5 hours per week). Most of the gap is found in front of the television set. Fathers spend 2.8 hours more each week than mothers watching TV or using other media.
