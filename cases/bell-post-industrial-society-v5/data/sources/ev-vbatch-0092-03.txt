WASHINGTON, October 26, 2026 — Global progress on reducing extreme poverty has come to a standstill, and the world is at risk of falling further behind unless more and better jobs are created, according to the World Bank Group’s new Poverty, Prosperity, and Planet Report 2026. In 2026, about 811 million people — one in ten globally — live on less than $3.00 per person per day, a rate that has essentially not changed since the pandemic. The report notes that extreme poverty remains concentrated: More than half of the world's extremely poor live in just nine economies, and by 2030 an estimated 87% are projected to live in Sub-Saharan Africa or in fragile and conflict-affected situations. Nearly half the global population — 3.7 billion people — still lives on less than $8.30/day, a number that has remained stagnant since 1990.
