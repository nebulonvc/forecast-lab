Regulators’ Budget: An Analysis of the U.S. Budget for Fiscal Years 1960 to 2024, by Finn Dobkin and Matias Vesperoni, GW Regulatory Studies Center (Published May 19, 2026). This report presents 65 years of data on two measures of regulatory activity: the outlays associated with developing, administering, and enforcing federal rules and regulations, and the staffing associated with these activities. This report tracks the spending and staffing of 77 federal departments and agencies from Fiscal Year (FY) 1960 to FY 2024. While overall regulatory spending and staffing have continued to rise in recent years, this growth has been uneven across agencies and policy areas. Social regulation and homeland security drive most of the expansion, whereas economic regulation grew at a more modest rate over the same period. Table 1 (Spending Summary, Current Nominal Dollars, Millions): GRAND TOTAL rose from $1,584 million in FY 1970 to $7,303 million in FY 1980, $25,415 million in FY 2000, $78,225 million in FY 2020, and $102,264 million in FY 2024. Table 2 (Staffing Summary, Full-time Equivalent Employment): GRAND TOTAL rose from 90,275 FTEs in FY 1970 to 146,408 in FY 1980, 175,878 in FY 2000, 267,041 in FY 2020, and 279,138 in FY 2024, with Total Social Regulation staffing rising from 57,685 in FY 1970 to 238,456 in FY 2024.
