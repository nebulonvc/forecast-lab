A new Pew Research Center survey from the Pew-Knight Initiative finds that nearly four-in-ten U.S. adults are part of an online community – and that share rises to about half among young adults. For some, these communities are deeply valuable parts of their lives. They widely offer information, advice or experiences people say they could only get online. Still, these benefits sit alongside doubts about making genuine connections online. About half of Americans question whether online relationships can be as meaningful as those formed in person. Fewer – 34% – think they can be just as meaningful. There’s an age divide on this topic as well. Adults under 30 are far more likely than those ages 65 and older to say online relationships can be just as meaningful as in-person ones (54% vs. 14%).
