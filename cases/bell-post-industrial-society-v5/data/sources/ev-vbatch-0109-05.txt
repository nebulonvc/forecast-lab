The National Academy of Sciences at 150 (Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A, June 23, 2014; 111(Suppl 2):9327-9364, Steve Olson; PMCID: PMC4077050). In 1863, Congress created the National Academy of Sciences, a private agency with the public role of advising the government on policy-related technical issues. In the century and a half since 1863, the National Academy of Sciences (NAS) has grown from a small band of 50 charter members—each of whom was specified in the founding legislation—to an organization of more than 2,500 national members and foreign associates. In 1916, the Academy created the National Research Council, which today recruits thousands of specialists each year from the scientific and technological communities to participate in the Academy's advisory work. The Academy’s nongovernmental status made it a more acceptable mechanism to the Chinese, and the Academy supported a flurry of exchanges with China in the 1970s, helping pave the way for formal diplomatic relations.
