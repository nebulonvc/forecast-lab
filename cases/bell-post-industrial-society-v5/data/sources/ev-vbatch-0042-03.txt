U.S. Census Bureau (July 21, 2026): A 250-Year History of the Foreign-Born Population.
From 1850 to 1870, the foreign-born share of the population jumped from 9.7% to 14.4%. It continued to climb, first peaking at 14.8% in 1890 (Figure 3).
Starting in 1920, the foreign-born share began to consistently decline, and by 1970 it accounted for only 4.7% of the population.
Beginning in 1965, there was a shift to more open immigration policies, moving away from a quota-based system to one that favored family reunification, certain job skills and refugee resettlement. These changes contributed to growth in the foreign-born from countries outside Europe.
The share of the foreign-born population from Latin America increased by 10 percentage points or more each decade from 1960 to 1990, surpassing the share from European countries for the first time after 1980.
By 2024, it had climbed to its largest number ever: 50.2 million — 14.8% of the 340.1 million total U.S. population.
