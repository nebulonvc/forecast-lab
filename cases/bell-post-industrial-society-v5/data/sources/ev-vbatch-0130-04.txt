A Closer Look at the Global South | Carnegie Endowment for International Peace (May 20, 2024).
The venerable concept of the “Global South” has enjoyed a remarkable revival as a descriptor of postcolonial and developing country solidarity in world affairs. Further, the analytical purchase of the phrase is limited, as one of us has argued, because the concept purports to capture an array of nations that differ markedly in their governing systems, economic circumstances, strategic alignments, and cultural identities. Such critiques should not, however, obscure the term’s continued political salience and symbolic potency more than half a century after the phrase was coined. To some in the world, the central divide in the international sphere remains that between the dominant North and the aggrieved South, rather than that between democracies and autocracies. Meanwhile the African American intellectual Du Bois, writing in 1925, added a racial dimension to this argument, maintaining that European states used political coercion and economic compulsion to create a racialized global hierarchy that resulted in an international “color line.”
