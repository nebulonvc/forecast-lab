The labor share—the portion of an economy’s total income that is paid to workers in wages and benefits—is a key concept for understanding how economic output is distributed between workers and the owners of capital (such as shareholders and business owners). For much of the twentieth century, the labor share was remarkably stable, and this stylized fact became a defining feature of widely used macroeconomic growth models. In more recent decades, however, the labor share has declined noticeably across both developed and developing economies. 1 When the labor share falls, a smaller portion of total income goes toward worker compensation, while a larger share flows to corporate profits and capital owners. Because capital ownership is spread much more unevenly across households than wages, a shift in income toward capital can widen income inequality. Sign up for Research Department Updates. Our findings suggest that technological change plays some role in explaining the post-pandemic decline across industries, whereas evidence for a pure increase in market power is more limited. However, an increase in market power resulting from technological change could still be at work.
