Our World in Data (lastUpdated: 2025-03-07; sources: OECD 2025, OECD 1985, Lindert 2004; IMF Public Finances in Modern History). Public social spending as share of GDP (health, old age, incapacity-related benefits, family, active labor market programmes, unemployment, and housing; general government including central, state, local governments and social security funds): United States rose from 9.94% in 1973 (12.86% in 1980, 14.12% in 2000) to 19.81% in 2024; United Kingdom rose from 13.84% in 1973 to 22.99% in 2023; Germany rose from 18.30% in 1973 to 27.85% in 2024; Japan rose from 5.49% in 1973 to 24.74% in 2022; Sweden rose from 17.40% in 1973 to 26.09% in 2024; France rose from 20.33% in 1980 to 30.65% in 2024. General government total expenditure (% of GDP) in the United States rose from 31.92% in 1973 to 37.92% in 2024, and US public health expenditure rose from 2.54% of GDP in 1973 to 14.30% in 2024.
