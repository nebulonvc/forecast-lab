Does the federal government require paid family leave? No, the United States does not have a federal paid family leave program. The federal Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) guarantees eligible workers up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave, but provides no wage replacement. Paid leave benefits are currently offered in some states and through the private sector, but coverage can vary significantly depending on where you live and work. What's the difference between mandatory and voluntary paid family leave? Mandatory programs require all covered employers in a state to participate and are typically administered through a state-run social insurance fund, with premiums collected via payroll deduction. Voluntary programs allow businesses to buy a private insurance product to offer paid leave benefits to their workers, but they are not required to do so. As a result, access to benefits under voluntary programs depends on whether an employer has chosen to participate. How do I apply for paid family leave in my state? The application process varies by state. In states with mandatory social insurance programs, employees typically apply directly through the state agency administering the program.
