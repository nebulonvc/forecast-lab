U.S. Bureau of the Census, Current Population Reports P25-1130, Population Projections of the United States by Age, Sex, Race, and Hispanic Origin: 1995 to 2050 (Issued February 1996): In the middle series, the median age of the population will steadily increase from 34.3 in 1995 (the highest ever recorded) to 35.7 in 2000. The youthful dependency ratio is not projected to change much from its 1995 level (42.8) during the next 55 years. The elderly dependency ratio is projected to be at an all-time high of 20.9 in 1995 (table E). The ratio would remain near that level until 2010 (21.2), then increase rapidly to 35.7 by 2030, as the post-World War II Baby Boom joins the 65 and over age group. The elderly dependency ratio then would be almost as large as the youthful dependency ratio, while now it is half as large. Table F (Middle Series, 1995, in thousands): Total 262,820; Under 5 years 19,591; 5 to 13 years 34,378; 14 to 17 years 14,773 (total under 18: 68,742, or 26.16%); 65 years and over 33,543 (12.76%).
