U.S. Department of Energy / Savannah River National Laboratory, Update of Low-Level Radioactive Waste Disposal History in the United States, SRNL-STI-2010-00489 (September 2010):
During this time, the amount of LLW disposed at the Barnwell facility had increased from 50,000 cubic feet in 1971 to 2.2 million cubic feet in 1979. In 1981, the State of South Carolina imposed an annual volumetric limit of 1.2 million cubic feet. To correct the imbalance of the LLW disposal burden, Congress passed the Low-Level Waste Policy Act in 1980 and, in 1985, the lack of progress in disposal site development among the regional compacts, which were formed per the 1980 Act, resulted in the passage of the Low-Level Waste Policy Amendments Act. Unfortunately, this history illustrates the failure of the State Compact system established by Congress in the Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Act of 1980 and the Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Amendments Act of 1985. Those acts empowered the States to band together in regional compacts for the purpose of establishing new LLW disposal facilities.
