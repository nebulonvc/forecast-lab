Release: Personal Income by State. Updated: Sep 30, 2026. From 1982 to 2002, Sunbelt states posted much faster percentage growth and overtook Ohio and Michigan in real (inflation-adjusted) absolute income gains: GA grew +342.6% ($57.84B to $255.98B, +$79.54B in 1982 CPI dollars, #7), VA grew +265.3% ($68.81B to $251.36B, +$66.10B, #9), and NC grew +290.6% ($59.37B to $231.93B, +$65.10B, #10), pushing MI (#12, +$59.70B) and OH (#13, +$59.64B) outside the real top 10. In 1982-2025 real terms (1982 CPI dollars), NC (#5, +$169.85B), GA (#6, +$162.82B), and WA (#7, +$158.80B) further widen the gap, leaving only four of the six named states in the top 10 (NY #4, PA #8, IL #9, NJ #10) while OH ranks #14 and MI ranks #17. Moreover, among the 10 slowest-growing states by percentage growth in 1982-2002 (ND +132.8%, OK +137.7%, WY +140.6%, WV +145.0%, LA +155.0%, AK +155.8%, MT +158.9%, IA +159.7%, KS +170.5%, OH +177.4%), only OH from the named six actually fell in the bottom 10 (PA #11, MI #13, IL #14, NY #19, NJ #26).
