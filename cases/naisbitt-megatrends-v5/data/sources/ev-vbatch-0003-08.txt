Early 2025 caseload data reveal a continuing shift in the composition of state court workloads.
For the fourth consecutive year, civil filings are growing faster than criminal filings, widening a trend that first emerged as courts recovered from pandemic-era disruptions. At the same time, courts may be seeing the first signs of a slowing post-pandemic recovery, as clearance rates decline after several years of improvement.
While total filings continue to evolve, the most notable story emerging from early 2025 data is not simply that civil caseloads are increasing. Rather, the growth is concentrated within specific types of cases, particularly contract disputes, small claims matters, and debt collection-related litigation. These case categories now account for much of the growth observed across participating states.
These findings are based on preliminary data reported by about 20 states and territories submitted through the Court Statistics Project (CSP) , a collaborative initiative of NCSC and the Conference of State Court Administrators (COSCA). Complete 2025 caseload reporting is expected by September 1.
A continuing shift in court workloads
Over the past several years, state courts have experienced a gradual but significant change in the balance of incoming cases. Criminal filings, which historically represented a dominant share of court activity, have recovered more slowly following pandemic disruptions. Civil filings, meanwhile, have expanded steadily.
Early 2025 data suggest that this shift is continuing.

The gap between incoming civil and criminal cases has widened each year since 2022. Civil cases now account for an increasingly larger share of total court business compared to criminal matters.
This trend reflects more than simple changes in filing volume. It represents a fundamental shift in the nature of work entering state courts. As civil caseloads grow, courts may face increasing demands in managing large volumes of debt-collection actions and other financially driven disputes.
