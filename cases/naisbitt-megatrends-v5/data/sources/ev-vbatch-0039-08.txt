The UAW’s membership is now about one-quarter of what it was in 1979, when it stood at 1.5 million.41 The plant closures and layoffs by GM and Chrysler will further erode union membership. GM alone announced closings of 13 plants for 2009 and 2010. In spring 2009, the Center for Automotive Research (CAR) said that there were 132,600 active union members at GM, Ford and Chrysler, with another 550,000 retirees and surviving spouses.42 During the past decade, much of the growth of the U.S. motor vehicle sector has occurred at the foreign-owned plants, such as Toyota, Honda, Mercedes-Benz, and BMW. These plants, strategically located mainly in southern states with right-to-work laws, or the southern parts of Ohio and Indiana, are non-union43 and directly employed over 107,000 workers in 2009.
