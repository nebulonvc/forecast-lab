Initiative 194, also known as The Montana Plan, is an initiative in Montana that appeared on the November 3, 2026 general election ballot. The initiative would prohibit "artificial persons", such as non-profits, trusts, partnerships, corporations, and trade associations, from spending money on elections and political parties. It passed with 65% approval and will enter into effect on January 1, 2027. In 1996, Montana Initiative 125, which barred corporate spending in ballot issue campaigns, was passed; it was struck down in 2000 by the 9th Circuit Court of Appeals in Montana Chamber of Commerce v. Argenbright. The measure was approved by voters in November 2026 and will enter into effect on January 1, 2027. On November 3, 2026, several groups filed a lawsuit against the measure. As of September 2026, legislation or ballot initiatives modeled after Initiative 194 have been introduced in 32 states. In May 2026, Hawaii Act 11, modeled after Initiative 194, was signed into law. Opponents of the initiative have argued that the initiative is illegal, citing Citizens United v. FEC. Lawsuits filed against the initiative accused it of violating the right to free speech. Some critics of the measure have noted that the measure does not restrict political spending by individuals.
