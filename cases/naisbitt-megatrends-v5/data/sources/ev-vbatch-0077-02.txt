Fraudulent petition signatures are signatures in support of a ballot measure or candidate seeking to qualify for the ballot that are forged, falsified, or added by the circulator or the signer in an illegal manner. Fraudulent signatures are a subsection of the category of invalid signatures. Not all invalid signatures are fraudulent, but all fraudulent signatures are invalid.

In a 2022 report, the Michigan Bureau of Elections found that, in the 2022 election cycle, there were "36 petition circulators who submitted fraudulent petition sheets consisting entirely of invalid signatures." The bureau found "that these circulators submitted at least 68,000 invalid signatures submitted across 10 sets of nominating petitions."[1] In a subsequent report in 2024, of the 120 candidate petitions submitted during the 2024 election cycle, the bureau found "a significantly lower volume sheets showing clear indications of fraud and signatures of dubious authenticity were submitted as compared to 2022."[1]

In May 2026, a Colorado grand jury indicated two individuals for allegedly submitting petitions with forged signatures for a 2024 ballot initiative.[6]

Pay-per-signature is a method of compensating signature gatherers, also known as circulators, who collect signatures for candidates, ballot initiatives, recall petitions, and veto referendums. The method involves paying signature gatherers at a rate based on the number of signatures collected. Some states prohibit the use of pay-per-signature. As of June 2026, 10 states ban pay-per-signature for ballot initiatives.
