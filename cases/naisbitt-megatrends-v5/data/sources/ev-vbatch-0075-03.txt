State government trifecta is a term to describe single-party government, when one political party holds the governorship and majorities in both chambers of the state legislature .
As of November 25, 2026, there are 23 Republican trifectas, 16 Democratic trifectas, and 11 divided governments where neither party holds trifecta control.
HIGHLIGHTS
As a result of the 2026 elections , there will be 40 trifectas across the country. Democrats gained six trifectas in the 2026 elections and Republicans lost five. As of November 18, 2026, North Carolina's trifecta status was undetermined.
Between 1992 and 2026, the most trifectas for each party were 18 for Democrats (1993) and 26 for Republicans (2018).
As of January 2026, 39.1% of the country's population lived in a state with a Democratic trifecta, 41.5% lived in a state with a Republican trifecta, and 19.4% lived in a state with divided government.
The state with the longest running trifecta is Utah, where Republicans have controlled the state government since 1985.
