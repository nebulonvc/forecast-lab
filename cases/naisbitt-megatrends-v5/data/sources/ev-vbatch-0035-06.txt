Based on the OECD’s intercountry input-output (ICIO) tables for 1995-2022 we examine to what extent China’s manufactured exports increased competitive pressures on domestic manufacturing producers and affected their output as well as the imports from other countries. We use import penetration (IP, imports as a share of apparent consumption) as a measure of competitive pressures, and present descriptive and econometric analysis. The analysis focuses on 19 manufacturing sectors in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Mexico, and Peru (the Latin American countries and sectors included in the ICIO tables). Together the seven countries account for around 80 percent of gross national income and trade of Latin America and the Caribbean. Main findings: Competitive pressures from Chinese imports increased in all manufacturing sectors. Industries with higher Chinese IP had lower growth in real value added. The impact of Chinese IP growth on output became more pronounced over time. Growth in Chinese IP crowded out IP growth from other countries.
