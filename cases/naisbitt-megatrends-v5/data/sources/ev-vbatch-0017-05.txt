Manufacturing value-added output increased from $3.004 trillion at an annual rate in Q1 2026 to $3.168 trillion in Q2 2026. Value-added output rose in Q2 for durable goods (up from $1.636 trillion to $1.695 trillion) and nondurable goods (up from $1.368 trillion to $1.473 trillion). Manufacturing accounted for 9.7% of value-added output in the U.S. economy in Q2. At the same time, real value-added output in the manufacturing sector increased in Q2 (up from $2.450 trillion to $2.471 trillion), as expressed in chained 2017 dollars. In Q2, real value-added output rose for durable goods (up from $1.357 trillion to $1.390 trillion) but declined for nondurable goods (down from $1.095 trillion to $1.083 trillion). (Last Updated: 10/13/26; Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis )
Manufacturing employment increased in October, gaining 19,000 employees from September. After declining over the past few years, employment is back on an upward trajectory and back above the pre-pandemic average, with 12,681,000 manufacturing employees in October. The sector averaged 12,613,000 employees pre-pandemic (2017–2019).
12. Manufacturing in the U.S. would be the eighth-largest economy in the world.
Taken alone, manufacturing in the United States would be the eighth-largest economy in the world. With $2.90 trillion in value added from manufacturing in 2025, only seven other nations (including the U.S.) would rank higher in terms of their GDP. Those other nations with higher GDP in 2025 were (in order) the U.S., China, Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom, India and France. After manufacturing in the U.S., the next five economies would be the Russian Federation, Italy, Canada, Brazil and Spain, in that order. (Last Updated: 5/18/26; Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis and International Monetary Fund )
16. Manufacturers perform 50.1% of all private-sector R&D.
Manufacturers in the United States perform 50.1% of all private-sector R&D in the nation, driving more innovation than any other sector. R&D in the manufacturing sector has risen from $132.5 billion in 2000 to a record $431.3 billion in 2025. In the most recent data, pharmaceuticals accounted for 37.2% of all manufacturing R&D, spending $160.6 billion in 2025. Other computer and electronic manufacturing (14.5%), semiconductor and other electronic components (13.7%) and motor vehicles and parts (8.4%) also contributed significantly to R&D spending in 2025. (Last Updated: 10/13/26; Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis )
