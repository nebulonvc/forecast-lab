1997 Annual Report: Time Well Spent: The Declining Real Cost of Living in America (Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas, W. Michael Cox and Richard Alm). A 25-inch color television with remote control went for as little as $299 in 1997, compared with $620 in 1971 and more than $1,000 for early color sets in the mid-1950s. Videocassette recorders entered the mainstream market at $985 in 1978. Twenty years later, VCRs offering surer picture tracking, on-screen programming and other features cost less than $200. Since entering the market, portable radio prices have declined from more than 13 hours to less than 1 hour of work. In January 1998 IBM cut the price of its 233-MHz MMX Aptiva to $999, lowering the cost to under 20 minutes per MIPS.
