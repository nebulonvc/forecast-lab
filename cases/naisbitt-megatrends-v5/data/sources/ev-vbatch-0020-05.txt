What is the federal public workforce system? 
 The federal public workforce development system is authorized under the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA)5 and is funded through discretionary appropriations of around $4 billion per year.6 Through a network of nearly 2,300 American Job Centers (AJCs), also known as “one-stops,” located across the country, any job seeker or employer can access training activities and employment services such as job search assistance, supportive services, and career counseling, among others.7
In practice, AJCs serve as support hubs that determine eligibility of potential trainees, provide skills assessments, develop individual employment plans, and issue individualized training accounts if training prior to job placement career services is necessary.8 In program year 2024, the latest year for which data are available, there were 250,160 adult program participants, 187,108 adult participants in the dislocated workers program, and 125,531 participants in WIOA youth programs.9
The public system under WIOA is governed by business-majority workforce development boards (WDBs) with the remaining membership made up of representatives of workers (20 percent) and other labor market stakeholders.10 Workforce development boards are charged with establishing statewide strategic plans that align training programs with in-demand jobs.11 In addition, WIOA mandates performance goals including training completion, skills and employment gains, and increased earnings.12
