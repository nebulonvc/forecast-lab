American Economic Review 2020, 110(4): 1104–1144, Are Ideas Getting Harder to Find? By Nicholas Bloom, Charles I. Jones, John Van Reenen, and Michael Webb. We present evidence from various industries, products, and firms showing that research effort is rising substantially while research productivity is declining sharply. A good example is Moore’s Law. The number of researchers required today to achieve the famous doubling of computer chip density is more than 18 times larger than the number required in the early 1970s. We once again find that it is declining sharply: aggregate growth rates are relatively stable over time, while the number of researchers has risen enormously. We find that research productivity for the aggregate US economy has declined by a factor of 41 since the 1930s, an average decrease of more than 5 percent per year. Gordon (2016) argues that the strong US productivity growth between 1996 and 2004 was a temporary blip and that productivity growth will, at best, return to the lower growth rates of 1973–1996.
