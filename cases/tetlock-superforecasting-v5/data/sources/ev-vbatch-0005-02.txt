Exclusive: The life and death of the Intelligence Community's Prediction Market (Sasha Ingber, May 26, 2026). But in 2020, as then-21-year-old founder Shayne Coplan was building Polymarket, the intelligence community’s prediction market quietly shut down. It was called the Intelligence Community’s Prediction Market. Or the ICPM. It would then move to different parts of the ODNI, like an orphan with fickle foster parents: Into the Intelligence Advanced Research Projects Activity (IARPA) around 2010 as part of its ACE Program to improve intelligence forecasts under program manager Steve Rieber, then around 2017 to the National Intelligence Council (NIC) which provides important estimates, then back to IARPA around 2018 because the new NIC chair Amy McAuliffe was old school, and then by 2020 the prediction market had reached a rather predictable end. At its heyday, the platform had about 4,000 analysts registered, according to Jason and Adam. Only a couple hundred participated on a monthly basis, Adam said. Like Polymarket, participants could choose whether or not they wanted their real identities to be known. There was concern that people betting in the ICPM might be judged based on how well their predictions fared. “We weren’t going to put it into the President’s Daily Brief, we weren’t going put it into a [National Intelligence Estimate],” Robert Cardillo said. So in 2020, as the world was gripped by covid and grappling with a series of unprecedented questions about life, the ICPM was taken offline. And for a moment, it looked as though the Defense Intelligence Agency would become a sponsor of INFER. In the end, it did not pan out.
