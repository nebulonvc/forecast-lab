Title: Advancing AI Capabilities and Evolving Labor Outcomes. Authors: Jacob Dominski, Tyler Boes, Yong Suk Lee. Submitted on 11 Jul 2025 (v1), last revised 20 Nov 2026 (v4). Abstract: This paper examines how labor market outcomes changed across occupations as AI capabilities advanced from chatbots to reasoning models and AI agents. We construct an occupational AI exposure score for each capability stage and relate its change to the Current Population Survey through June 2026. In aggregate, occupations whose exposure rose more did not lose employment or see higher unemployment over three years. However, the number of workers under 30 in these occupations fell by about 8 percent per standard deviation of the exposure change, and the decline is concentrated among young workers without a college degree and in office and customer service occupations rather than in technical occupations. Their unemployment did not rise, consistent with less entry rather than more layoffs. College graduates aged 30 to 49 gained full-time positions in the same occupations, so the workforce in exposed occupations became older and more educated. The same comparisons for 2017 to 2022 show no such patterns.
