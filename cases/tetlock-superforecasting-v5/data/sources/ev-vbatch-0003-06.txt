Subjective-probability forecasts of existential risk: Initial results from a hybrid persuasion-forecasting tournament. Karger, Ezra; Rosenberg, Josh; Jacobs, Zachary; Hickman, Molly; Tetlock, Phillip E. International Journal of Forecasting, 2025, vol. 41(2), pages 499-516. A multi-stage persuasion-forecasting tournament asked specialists and generalists (“superforecasters”) to explain their probability judgments of short- and long-run existential threats to humanity. Specialists were more pessimistic, especially on long-run threats posed by artificial intelligence (AI). Despite incentives to share their best arguments during four months of discussion, neither side materially moved the other’s views. This would be puzzling if participants were Bayesian agents methodically sifting through elusive clues about distant futures but it is less puzzling if participants were boundedly rational agents searching for confirmatory evidence as the risks of embarrassing accuracy feedback receded. We stress the potential of these methods to inform high-stakes debates, but we acknowledge limits on what even skilled forecasters can achieve in anticipating rare or unprecedented events.
