## ℹ️ About This Indicator

▼

### Description

Gross enrollment ratio is the ratio of total enrollment, regardless of age, to the population of the age group that officially corresponds to the level of education shown. Primary education provides children with basic reading, writing, and mathematics skills along with an elementary understanding of such subjects as history, geography, natural science, social science, art, and music.

### 📏 Unit of Measurement

% of population in the 5-year age group immediately following preprimary education

## What This Indicator Measures

This indicator is drawn from the World Development Indicators (WDI), the World Bank's primary collection of development data compiled from officially recognized international sources. WDI covers over 1,400 indicators for 217 economies, with some time series extending back more than 50 years.

### How to Read This Data

Enrollment ratios measure the proportion of the eligible population participating in a given level of education. Gross enrollment can exceed 100% when it includes over-age or under-age students.

### Understanding Cross-Country Comparisons

International statistics are compiled from national sources that differ in methodology, coverage, and reporting frequency. A value reported for one country may reflect survey data while the same indicator for another country relies on administrative records or model estimates. These differences can affect comparability even when the same unit of measurement is used.

Time lags are common: many developing countries report economic data one to three years after the reference period, and survey-based indicators like poverty and health outcomes often carry even longer delays. When the latest available year differs across countries in the same chart, direct comparisons should be interpreted with caution.

Population size, geographic area, and institutional capacity all shape how indicator values should be read. A small island economy and a continental power may report similar per-capita figures but face fundamentally different structural conditions. The charts and rankings on this page provide a starting point for analysis, not a final verdict.

### Data Limitations

- Coverage varies by country and year — gaps do not necessarily mean the value is zero.
- Methodological revisions by source agencies can cause breaks in time series that appear as sudden jumps or drops.
- Aggregate figures for regions and income groups are weighted averages and may not reflect the experience of individual countries within that grouping.
- Provisional or estimated values are sometimes included in the most recent years and may be revised in future releases.

## Plain English Summary

This indicator measures the gross enrollment ratio (GER) in primary education—the total enrollment in primary school, regardless of age, expressed as a percentage of the official primary school-age population. Values can exceed 100% when over-age or under-age students are enrolled. This is a fundamental measure of educational access and system capacity.

What this indicator captures:

- School access: Overall enrollment levels
- System capacity: School places available
- Universal education: Progress toward full enrollment
- Age composition: Includes over-age/under-age students

Why primary enrollment matters:

- SDG 4: Quality education for all
- Human capital: Foundation for development
- Universal access: Basic right to education
- Literacy: Reading and numeracy foundation
- Economic development: Skills base

Gross vs. net enrollment:

- Gross: All enrolled ÷ official age population (can exceed 100%)
- Net: Only official age enrolled ÷ official age population (max 100%)
- Difference: Over-age and under-age students
- Both useful: Different perspectives on access

For example, a GER of 105% means total primary enrollment equals 105% of the official primary age population—indicating some over-age or under-age enrollment.

## How to Interpret

Gross enrollment benchmarks:

- 95-100+%: High—universal access achieved
- 85-95%: Near-universal—most children enrolled
- 70-85%: Moderate—significant gaps remain
- 50-70%: Low—major access challenges
- <50%: Very low—education crisis

Values over 100%:

- Common and expected
- Indicates over-age enrollment
- Grade repetition effects
- System catching up
- Not necessarily negative

Regional patterns:

- Developed countries: 98-103%
- Latin America: 100-110%
- East Asia: 98-105%
- South Asia: 100-115%
- Sub-Saharan Africa: 95-120%
- Conflict areas: May be much lower

Trend interpretation:

- Rising GER indicates expansion
- Very high (>120%) suggests inefficiency
- Falling toward 100% may indicate system improvement
- Compare with net for full picture

## Limitations & Caveats

### 1. Quality Not Captured

Access ≠ quality:

- Learning outcomes separate
- Attendance not measured
- Teaching quality varies
- Completion rates needed

### 2. Exceeds 100%

Interpretation care:

- Over-age enrollment included
- Grade repetition effects
- Late entry
- Not necessarily good or bad

### 3. Age Definition

Official age varies:

- Country-specific primary ages
- Duration differences
- Comparability issues
- Check definitions

### 4. Private Schools

Coverage varies:

- Private enrollment included
- Data quality differs
- Informal schools
- Reporting gaps

### 5. Attendance Hidden

Enrollment ≠ attendance:

- Ghost students possible
- Absenteeism not captured
- Effective access different
- Completion separate

### 6. Subnational Variation

National average masks:

- Urban-rural gaps
- Regional differences
- Vulnerable groups
- Local analysis needed

### 7. Conflict/Crisis

Disruption effects:

- Displacement
- School destruction
- Data gaps
- Recovery tracking
