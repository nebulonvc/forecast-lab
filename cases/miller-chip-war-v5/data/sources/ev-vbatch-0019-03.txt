Given the current level of investment in manufacturing capacity, the strategy is very unlikely to be sufficient to achieve by 2030 the very ambitious Digital Decade target of a 20 % EU share in the global market value chain by revenue. It is currently predicted that its share will be only 11.7 % by 2030. As far as the production of cutting-edge microchips is concerned, we note that in 2020 the only two companies manufacturing microchips at 5 nm were located in Taiwan and South Korea, while the EU did not have any manufacturing capabilities below 22 nm. By way of comparison, according to a JRC report the top global semiconductor manufacturers budgeted for $425 billion (€405 billion) of investment over the 2020-2023 period, 60 % of which was accounted for by TSMC, Samsung and Intel. Among these top three manufacturers, only TSMC has significant plans underway for investing in the EU. SEMI, the global industry association representing the electronics manufacturing supply chain, estimates that EU-bound capital expenditure up to 2032 would be €147 billion out of a world total of €2 162 billion (Figure 10). Although we could not obtain direct evidence of number of EUV tool shipments to EU chipmakers, in September 2023 Intel announced the first use of this technology in Ireland.
