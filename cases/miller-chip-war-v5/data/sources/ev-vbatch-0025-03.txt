"TSMC’s 2nm (N2) technology has started volume production in 4Q25 as planned," a statement at TSMC's web page dedicated to 2nm Technology reads. From an improvement standpoint, N2 is designed to deliver a 10%–15% performance gain at the same power, a 25%–30% reduction in power at the same performance, and a 15% increase in transistor density compared to N3E for mixed designs that include logic, analog, and SRAM. For logic-only designs, transistor density is up to 20% higher than N3E. TSMC's N2 is the company's first process node to adopt gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheet transistors, where the gate fully surrounds the channel formed by stacked horizontal nanosheets. This geometry improves electrostatic control, reduces leakage, and enables smaller transistors without sacrificing performance or power efficiency, ultimately increasing transistor density. Ramping up two N2-capable fabs at once is a result of strong interest for the new process technology by a variety of TSMC partners, so it needs to offer decent capacity for all of them. Furthermore, starting late 2026, both fabs will be used to build chips on N2P, a performance-enhanced version of N2, and A16, a version of N2P with the Super Power Rail backside power delivery that is aimed at complex AI and HPC processors.
