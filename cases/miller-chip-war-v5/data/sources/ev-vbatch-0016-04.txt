Following a wave of excitement, the power electronics industry has entered a consolidation phase
The power electronics market is poised for steady growth, with Yole Group’s projections estimating an increase from $23.8 billion in 2023 to $35.7 billion by 2029. Silicon carbide (SiC) technology, in particular, is expected to play a key role in this growth, especially in EV & industrial segments. However, the industry is also facing significant challenges, including recent overcapacity and price erosion in SiC wafers and devices.
Today’s chronicle is based on Yole Group’s collection of analyses focused on the power electronics industry. Including the annual report, Status of the Power Electronic Industry as well as Power Electronics for xEV, and many more, Yole Group’s Principal Analyst, Milan Rosina offer a comprehensive overview of this industry, highlighting the market evolution and business issues, all linked to the latest innovations.
Have a break and deep dive into the power electronics world.
In recent years, optimism about the power electronics market has surged. Various application segments, including electric mobility, photovoltaics, battery energy storage systems, and wind energy, saw a record year in 2023.
This period of rapid market expansion has generated excitement about future demand for power devices, especially using SiC technology, largely fueled by the electric vehicle sector. The power electronics supply chain has experienced rapid growth in manufacturing capacity, particularly for SiC wafers and both silicon and SiC devices.
A recent slowdown in demand for EV, a trend towards more affordable vehicle models, and the rush to expand device manufacturing capacity have resulted in overcapacity, particularly in the SiC sector. Moreover, political uncertainty, rising geopolitical tensions, and the worsening economic situation in Europe and the U.S. have made investors more cautious, negatively impacting the stock prices of some power semiconductor companies.
Additionally, price erosion in wafers and devices, driven by competition from Chinese players, has prompted companies to rethink their strategies and initiate cost-reduction measures. Some projects for new facilities have been delayed, and older factories using obsolete technologies are shutting down.
Market consolidation is expected to have a significant impact on the supply chain. Leading SiC wafer manufacturers are now facing intensified competition from smaller players employing aggressive pricing strategies. As China ramps up efforts to expand its domestic power device manufacturing capabilities, companies in Europe, the U.S., and Japan are contending with increasing competition. Chinese manufacturers benefit from strong technological expertise in power systems and access to a vast domestic market. Companies that once thrived by focusing on the rapidly growing EV market are now looking to diversify into other sectors.
The SiC device price reduction could accelerate SiC technology’s adoption across various industrial applications where high costs were previously a major barrier.
The development of SiC technology was initially focused on high-voltage industrial applications. Then the industry moved most of the development efforts to automotive devices within the 650V – 1,200V range. In the current climate of decelerated EV market growth and growing SiC manufacturing capacity, power electronics companies are increasing their business in non-EV applications. To better match system and device voltages, new ‘non-standard’ voltage classes have been created. Several companies, including Infineon, Toshiba, and Wolfspeed, have introduced SiC power devices with a ‘2.X kV’ voltage class, well suited for 1,500 VDC power electronic systems, such as photovoltaic inverters. Infineon has also launched a 400V SiC device for data center server power supplies and other applications.
The trend towards more affordable electric vehicles will also significantly influence the choice and demand for power devices. Electric vehicles with lower power drivetrains (around 70-100kW) will require fewer power semiconductor devices per vehicle, will make IGBTs a suitable option and discrete devices can potentially replace power modules in these cases.
This trend will lead to a consolidation of the supply chain, driving technology innovations, price reduction, and new strategies.
The market’s transitional phase presents both opportunities and obstacles
While SiC’s falling costs and advancing technology position it as a competitive alternative to traditional silicon solutions, the industry must navigate the effects of political and economic uncertainties, increased competition from smaller players, and investor caution. Companies are responding by diversifying into other applications and initiating cost-reduction strategies, leading to market consolidation.
The reduction in EV demand and shift toward more affordable electric vehicles and the subsequent changes in power device demand are likely to drive new partnerships, mergers, and acquisitions. As the industry adapts, it will not only reshape the supply chain but also accelerate innovation and drive cost reduction. The evolution of SiC technology will be particularly critical, both in terms of its impact on industrial applications and its role in the next generation of electric vehicles.
