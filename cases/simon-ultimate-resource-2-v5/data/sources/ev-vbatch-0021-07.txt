Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A (2024-10-09, PMC11515121), The Spherical Tokamak for Energy Production: theme issue introduction. Fusion energy has the potential to fill the gap in the availability of firm energy sources and to make net-zero attainable. Delivering a viable fusion power plant is one of the holy grails of science and engineering but it represents an unprecedented technical challenge. These engineering challenges are numerous, and must be met simultaneously: (i) the sustainment of a controlled plasma over long timescales with fusion-born helium dominating the plasma heating; (ii) the controlled exhaust of heat and helium 'ash' from the plasma core; (iii) the development of structural materials which have to sustain, for many years, extreme neutron irradiation.
