Originally published in September 2018; substantially updated in May 2026.
India and Bangladesh have both high population density and large households, so even areas that look like farmland from above can easily cross the town threshold. With 4–5 people per household, a landscape with just one house per hectare already has a density of 400–500 per square kilometer — well above the threshold for towns.
In 1950, as many people lived in towns as in rural areas globally. In cities, there were half as many. The story over the past 70 years, then, has been the rise of cities and the decline of rural populations. By 1980, cities had overtaken rural areas, and by the early 2000s, they overtook towns to become the most common type of settlement.
In recent decades, sub-Saharan Africa has been the world’s fastest-urbanizing region.
