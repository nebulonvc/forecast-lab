Nature Communications (2026-06-02, PMC13392370), Rethinking the economics and flexibility of U.S. nuclear power through hydrogen integration and policy support. Nuclear power supplies reliable low-carbon electricity and high-temperature heat, but existing plants face growing pressure to operate more flexibly in markets with rising renewable penetration. In today’s United States (U.S.) power markets, new large nuclear units face steep overnight capital costs ($6000-$12,000 kW−1) and long development timelines, even as electricity systems become more dynamic and price-volatile. Using an hourly optimization model linked to lifecycle techno-economic analysis, we find that retrofitting existing reactors with high-temperature electrolysis increases average net present value from about -$1.06 billion to about $240 million without policy support.
