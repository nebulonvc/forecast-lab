The National Ignition Facility (NIF) at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) in the USA remains at the global forefront of fusion ignition research, having now achieved the milestone of fusion ignition eight times, including its landmark result in December 2022. In April 2025, a NIF experiment achieved a record fusion yield of 8.6 MJ from 2.08 MJ of laser energy, representing a target gain greater than 4. SPARC and ARC are fusion projects from Commonwealth Fusion Systems (CFS), based in the USA. The SPARC tokamak, located at CFS headquarters in Devens, Massachusetts, is designed to demonstrate net fusion energy (expected in 2027), with first plasma produced by 2026. In parallel, CFS has announced plans to build its first commercial fusion power plant, ARC, in Chesterfield County, Virginia, targeting an output of 400 MW(e) and grid connection in the early 2030s. The UK’s STEP is a prototype fusion power plant targeting first operations by 2040, designed to demonstrate net electricity generation and pave the way for commercial fusion power. Fusion development still faces technical challenges in advancing superconducting magnets and achieving tritium self-sufficiency, on the one hand, and challenges in establishing a sound fusion energy industry characterized by a mature supply chain, economic viability and sustained investment, and adapting a regulatory framework commensurate to its safety requirements, on the other.
