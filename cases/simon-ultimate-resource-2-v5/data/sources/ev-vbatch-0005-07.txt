In 2026, 811 million people, 1 in 10, live on less than $3.00 a day, a rate unchanged since 2019. More than half the extreme poor live in nine economies, and by 2030, 87 percent will be in Sub-Saharan Africa, in fragile and conflict-affected situations, or both. Without stronger per capita growth, the number of people in extreme poverty is projected to be higher in 2030 than it is today.
Despite the reduction seen in past decades, escaping extreme poverty has not meant reaching security. Nearly half of humanity, 3.7 billion people, still lives on less than $8.30 a day, most of them one shock away from falling back.
