In FAO forest product statistics, the fibre used to manufacture paper and paperboard is referred to as 'fibre furnish'. This includes recovered paper (wastepaper), other fibre pulp and the wood pulp used to make paper. Global production of fibre furnish in 2018 amounted to 421 million tonnes, the same as in previous year. It shows that recovered paper and chemical wood pulp are the two main products used to manufacture paper, accounting for 54 and 35 percent of all fibre furnish consumption respectively in 2018. Mechanical and semi-chemical wood pulp is the next most important (8 percent), followed by other fibre pulp (3 percent). The trends in consumption also show that recovered paper accounts for more than half of all fibre used to make paper. In 2018, recovered paper consumption amounted to 229 million tonnes (54 percent of the total), compared to 227 million tonnes (55 percent of the total) in 2014. In contrast, consumption of other fibre pulp has declined in absolute terms. Total wood pulp consumption and its share slightly increased over the same period.
