WASHINGTON, October 28, 2026 —Commodity prices are set to rise 23% in 2026 as global shocks mount, according to the World Bank Group’s latest Commodity Markets Outlook, released today. The conflict in the Middle East has drawn down oil inventories and is set to push energy prices to a four-year high. Meanwhile, disrupted grain shipments and El Niño threaten harvests and food affordability. Energy prices are projected to rise 32% in 2026, reversing three years of declines, before moderating in 2027. Brent is forecast to average $91 per barrel in 2026, up from $69 in 2025, then ease to $79 in 2027. Metals prices are set to reach a new high in 2026, rising 25% as prices of aluminum, copper, and tin all hit record highs. These gains reflect strong demand from investments in renewable energy, electric vehicles, and data centers, together with supply disruptions this year. Prices are expected to decline by 3% in 2027. Precious metals prices are forecast to increase by 40% in 2026 as demand for safe-haven assets remains elevated amid geopolitical and policy uncertainty.
