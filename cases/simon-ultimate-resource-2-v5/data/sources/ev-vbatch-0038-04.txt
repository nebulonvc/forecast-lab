On an unsubsidized $/MWh basis, renewable energy remains the most cost-competitive form of generation. As such, renewable energy will continue to play a key role in the buildout of new power generation in the U.S. This is particularly true in the current high power demand environment, where renewables stand out as both the lowest-cost and quickest-to-deploy generation resource. Levelized Cost of Energy Comparison—Version 18.0: Solar PV—Utility $38 to $78/MWh; Solar PV + Storage—Utility $50 to $131/MWh; Wind—Onshore $37 to $86/MWh; U.S. Nuclear $141 to $220/MWh (based on Vogtle nuclear plant units 3 and 4). The results of this year’s firming analysis show that as the penetration of lowcost intermittent generation increases, the value of firm capacity rises. Both CAISO and PJM have reduced capacity accreditation values for highly correlated resources (e.g., solar and shorter-duration storage). Despite the sustained unsubsidized cost competitiveness of renewable energy, resource planning metrics indicate diverse generation fleets will be required over the long term to meet power needs, likely bolstered by now-emerging technologies such as long duration energy storage, geothermal, nuclear small modular reactors, pumped storage hydropower and carbon capture and storage.
