There are now more than 8,000,000,000 people on planet Earth.

The UN’s World Population Prospects 2024 estimates a global population of 8.2 billion in 2024. Future growth follows different possible paths, depending on the assumptions used in each projection.

Reproductive rights and individual choice come first. The UN’s 2024 high and low fertility scenarios use alternative birth-rate assumptions. From 2034, they assume 0.5 births per woman above or below the medium scenario, following smaller differences in the initial years. These are modelling assumptions, not recommendations for anyone’s reproductive choices.

(Source: UN DESA Population Division , 2024)

These figures regarding populations of different continents and countries include assumptions about future migration, but are necessarily very speculative. Climate change , poverty and population pressures themselves will lead to a highly mobile global population, with Africa likely to be the largest source of emigrants.

In other countries, net migration is outward, meaning their populations are decreasing, or growing at a smaller rate than they otherwise would. When the people leaving are of reproductive age (which is usually the case), emigration causes the fertility rate to fall even further. In some cases, these countries also have low fertility rates already (such as Bulgaria and Romania).

Biodiversity loss, climate change, pollution, deforestation, water and food shortages, these are all made worse by our huge and ever-increasing numbers. Our impact on the environment is a product of our consumption and how many of us there are. We can and must ensure a healthier, fair and sustainable future for people and the planet.
