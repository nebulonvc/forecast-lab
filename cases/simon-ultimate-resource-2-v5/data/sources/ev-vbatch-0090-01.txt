World Bank World Development Indicators (lastupdated: 2026-11-11): Combined official series for Total Population (SP.POP.TOTL, 1960-2025) and GDP per person employed (SL.GDP.PCAP.EM.KD, constant 2021 PPP $, 1995-2025) across 22 developed economies (USA, JPN, DEU, FRA, GBR, ITA, CAN, AUS, NZL, CHE, NLD, SWE, NOR, DNK, FIN, AUT, BEL, IRL, ESP, PRT, ISR, KOR). In support of faster population growth coinciding with higher per-worker income, the United States (population growth 1.11%/yr in 1960-1995 and 0.84%/yr in 1995-2025) raised GDP per person employed from $97,559 in 1995 to $156,983 in 2025 (+1.60%/yr), outpacing slower-growing Japan ($73,602 to $87,888, +0.59%/yr, pop growth -0.06%/yr in 1995-2025), Italy ($128,417 to $131,481, +0.08%/yr, pop growth 0.12%/yr), Germany ($101,656 to $125,086, +0.69%/yr, pop growth 0.07%/yr), and France ($103,861 to $128,587, +0.71%/yr). However, directly contradicting a universal advantage under every set of conditions, faster-growing developed economies including Israel (pop growth 2.79%/yr in 1960-1995; 2025 GDP/worker $104,695), Australia (1.62%/yr; $114,582), Canada (1.42%/yr; $113,563), and New Zealand (1.26%/yr; $87,948) all recorded lower 2025 GDP per person employed than slower-growing European peers such as Belgium (0.29%/yr; $147,482), Denmark (0.38%/yr; $140,397), Austria (0.34%/yr; $131,259), Italy (0.36%/yr; $131,481), Sweden (0.47%/yr; $128,980), France (0.65%/yr; $128,587), and Germany (0.33%/yr; $125,086). Across the 22 developed economies, Pearson correlation between 1960-1995 population growth and 2025 GDP per person employed is -0.244, whereas correlation with 1995-2025 GDP-per-worker annual growth rate is +0.219 (+0.321 for 1995-2025 population growth).
