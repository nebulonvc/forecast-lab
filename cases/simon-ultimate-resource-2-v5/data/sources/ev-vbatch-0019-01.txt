This mission marked the first launch of the rocket using new exploration ground systems and evaluated Orion’s systems without astronauts or the critical life support systems planned for future crewed missions. Artemis II In April 2026, Artemis II launched on a nearly 10-day voyage around the Moon, marking the first crewed flight of NASA’s Orion spacecraft aboard the SLS rocket. NASA astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, and Christina Koch, along with CSA (Canadian Space Agency) astronaut Jeremy Hansen, traveled 252,756 miles from Earth, setting a record for the greatest distance humans have traveled in space and observing parts of the lunar surface that had never been seen before with human eyes. Artemis III Launching in 2027, Artemis III will send NASA astronauts Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio, Andre Douglas, and ESA (European Space Agency) astronaut Luca Parmitano on a demonstration mission in low Earth orbit to test critical rendezvous and docking operations between Orion and test versions of commercial human landing systems developed by Blue Origin and SpaceX. Artemis IV NASA’s first crewed Moon landing under the Artemis program will take place in 2028. After launching aboard the SLS rocket, the crew will transfer from the Orion spacecraft to a commercial human landing system that will transport the astronauts to the lunar surface. Artemis V The journey does not end with a return to the Moon. As NASA's second lunar landing mission in 2028, Artemis V will build on earlier exploration efforts and help establish a steady yearly cadence of lunar expeditions that transform the Moon from a destination into a place where humans live, work, and explore. Our Artemis III Crew Meet the astronauts who will carry out a series of objectives to demonstrate critical systems needed for a future lunar landing, helping NASA and its partners reduce risk before Americans return to the Moon on Artemis IV.
