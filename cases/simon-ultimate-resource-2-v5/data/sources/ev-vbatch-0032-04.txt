World Bank (2025), Fresh Perspectives: Emerging Issues and Opportunities for Desalination in the Middle East and North Africa (International Bank for Reconstruction and Development / The World Bank; GWI DesalData consulted April 11, 2025):
Significant advancements in technology and innovation have reduced the cost and increased the accessibility of desalination to enable wider adoption across the Middle East and North Africa region and globally. Many of these innovations have been driven by countries across the region, notably among those in the GCC, and progressively decreased CAPEX and OPEX (operating expenses), reduced energy intensity, and improved water quality parameters. Desalination costs have dropped from US$2.5–US$5.0 per m3 in the 1980s to an average of less than US$1.0 per m3, with some projects in the late 2010s and early 2020s realizing a cost of US$0.4–US$0.5 m3.
According to GWI DesalData 2025, over 5,100 desalination plants are online in 2025 in the Middle East and North Africa, among which 332 are MED-based (8 percent of capacity), 214 are MSF-based (27 percent of capacity), and 4,474 are RO-based (63 percent of capacity).
