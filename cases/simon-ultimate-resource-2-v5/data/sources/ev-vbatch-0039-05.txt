Proved reserves of crude oil and natural gas decreased 1% and 3%, respectively, compared with 2023. Oil highlights 2024 U.S. crude oil and lease condensate proved reserves decreased 1% from 46.4 billion barrels to 46.0 billion barrels at year-end 2024. U.S. crude oil and lease condensate production increased 2% in 2024. Texas crude oil and lease condensate reserves decreased 3% in 2024, the largest annual net decline (529 million barrels) reported among all states. The second-largest net decline of oil reserves occurred in North Dakota (11%, or 487 million barrels). The price of West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil at Cushing, Oklahoma, decreased 1%, from $77.58 per barrel (b) in 2023 to $76.63/b in 2024. New Mexico crude oil and lease condensate proved reserves increased 8%, the largest net increase (496 million barrels) in 2024. Proved reserves from shale plays grew to 60% of U.S. crude oil and lease condensate proved reserves, after increasing 5% from 26.2 billion barrels at year-end 2023 to 27.5 billion barrels at year-end 2024. Natural gas highlights 2024 Proved reserves of U.S. natural gas decreased 3%, from 603.6 trillion cubic feet (Tcf) at year-end 2023 to 583.9 Tcf at year-end 2024. U.S. natural gas production increased 1% in 2024. Texas natural gas proved reserves decreased 7% in 2024, the largest annual net decline (10.2 Tcf) among all states in 2024. Louisiana had the second-largest net decline in proved reserves of natural gas in 2024 (26%, or 8.4 Tcf). The price of natural gas at the Louisiana Henry Hub decreased 13% in 2024, from $2.53 per million British thermal units (MMBtu) in 2023 to $2.19/MMBtu in 2024, the lowest annual average price since 2020 Alaska reported the largest annual net increase in proved reserves of natural gas in 2024 (7%, or 6.4 Tcf).
