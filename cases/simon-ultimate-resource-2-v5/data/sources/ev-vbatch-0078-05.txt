After millennia of agricultural expansion, the world has passed ‘peak agricultural land’ — Hannah Ritchie, Our World in Data (published 2022-05-30). The world produces more food than ever, but the amount of land we use is now falling. This means we can feed more people while restoring wild habitat. This expansion of agricultural land has now come to an end. After millennia, we have passed the peak, and in recent years global agricultural land use has declined. Despite this reduction in agricultural land, the world has continued to produce more food. This is true of both crops and livestock. We see this decoupling in the chart that presents the UN FAO’s data. It shows that global agricultural land – the green line – has peaked while agricultural production – the brown line – has continued to increase strongly, even after this peak. Global agricultural land has peaked, but cropland has not. Agricultural land use is still increasing in many countries – often at the expense of carbon-rich habitats.
