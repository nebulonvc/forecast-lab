Significant advancements in technology and innovation have reduced the cost and increased the accessibility of desalination to enable wider adoption across the Middle East and North Africa region and globally. Many of these innovations have been driven by countries across the region, notably among those in the GCC, and progressively decreased CAPEX and OPEX (operating expenses), reduced energy intensity, and improved water quality parameters. Desalination costs have dropped from US$2.5–US$5.0 per m3 in the 1980s to an average of less than US$1.0 per m3, with some projects in the late 2010s and early 2020s realizing a cost of US$0.4–US$0.5 m3 (World Bank 2019). While there is a general trend toward lower-cost solutions, the cost of water from desalination continues to vary widely depending on various factors, such as feedwater quality and salt concentration, technology used, energy costs, and other institutional factors. Energy consumption for seawater desalination in 2010–2020 was estimated to have been between 2.5–5.5 kWh per m3 for RO, 5.5–12.0 kWh per m3 for MED, and 9.0–16.0 kWh per m3 for MSF.
