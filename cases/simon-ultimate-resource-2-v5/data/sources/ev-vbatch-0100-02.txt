11 October 2026 Nature World News (United States) Solar Outproduced Nuclear Power Worldwide in 2025 for the First Time, Independent Report Finds Solar panels produced more electricity worldwide than nuclear reactors across all of 2025, the first full year in which that has happened, according to the latest World Nuclear Industry Status Report . Global solar generation jumped about 30% last year, while output from the world’s operating reactors rose just 1.1%, pv magazine reported on September 29 . Nuclear plants generated 2,703 terawatt-hours (TWh) in 2025, the report found, and nuclear’s share of global electricity slipped to 8.8%, its lowest level in about four and a half decades and roughly half its 1996 peak of 17.5%. The finding should be read with its source in mind. The report is an independent annual assessment overseen by French energy consultant Mycle Schneider, and its analyses have often questioned the nuclear industry’s prospects. A separate global dataset from the energy think tank Ember, published earlier this year, described solar as nearly level with nuclear in 2025 rather than ahead. Both agree on the direction of travel. Growth Rates Behind the Crossover The gap in growth is the core of the story. According to the report, global solar capacity rose by 510 gigawatts ( GW ) in 2025 to 2,383 GW , a 27% increase, while wind added a record 159 GW to reach 1,291 GW . Installed nuclear capacity rose just 1.5 GW to 399.5 GW , and operating nuclear capacity slipped by 0.5 GW to 368.6 GW . Those solar figures may be conservative. Newer data from the International Energy Agency’s photovoltaic program suggest global additions reached roughly 690 GW in 2025, about 180 GW more than the report used, pv magazine noted. Nuclear’s modest growth came largely from China, where generation rose 7.6%. Outside China, nuclear output fell 0.2% and remained well below its 2006 level. Schneider told pv magazine the state of the industry is “ quite sobering for those who expected to find a booming sector. ” A Monthly Streak That Began in Spring 2025 The annual result followed a string of monthly firsts. Wind generation alone surpassed nuclear in March 2025, and solar followed in April, the first month in which global solar output exceeded nuclear. From March 2025 through June 2026, either solar or wind individually out-generated nuclear every month, and in March, April and May 2026 both did. Batteries are part of the explanation. Grid-scale storage expanded sharply in 2025, with 104 GW and 257 gigawatt-hours connected during the year, according to the report, which identifies falling battery costs as a potential enabler of continued solar growth.
