Land statistics 2001–2024. Global, regional and country trends
FAO data reveal shifting global land use patterns and intensification trends between 2001 and 2024.
FAOSTAT data reveal a shift in global land use patterns, with cropland expanding while total agricultural land declines. Between 2001 and 2024, cropland increased significantly, driven by regional expansion and changes in cropping systems, while permanent meadows and pastures declined. At the same time, irrigation and land productivity rose markedly, pointing to increasing agricultural intensification, alongside continued pressures on forests and land resources.
Global agricultural land reached 4.6 billion ha in 2024, more than one-third of total land area.
Cropland expanded by 92 million ha since 2001, while permanent meadows and pastures declined by 238 million ha.
Africa is the main driver of cropland expansion, followed by South America and South‑eastern Asia.
Land equipped for irrigation reached 351 million ha, about 22% of cropland, accounting for a large share of cropland growth.
Area under organic agriculture reached 121 million ha in 2024, more than five times its 2004 level.
Cropland per capita declined by about 20%, while land productivity increased by nearly 68%.
Cropland expansion accounted for about 72% of forest land loss, confirming agriculture as a major driver of deforestation.
