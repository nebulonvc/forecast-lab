NETL has been recently assessing and developing CTL technology through process feasibility, cost determinations, and lifecycle emissions studies, plus a notable collaboration with the Department of Defense (DOD)/U.S. Air Force on a set of projects in 2014 –2017 aimed at technology development for reducing costs and emissions associated with synthetic jet fuel production from coal. This work has shown that carbon capture and storage and substitution of some biomass with coal in the feedstock to liquid fuels synthesis processes can reduce the lifecycle CO2 emissions of CTL to below the baseline levels characteristic of conventional petroleum-derived fuels. However, the future of CTL use in the United States is challenged by its relatively high cost compared to conventional petroleum-derived fuels, with cost competitiveness generally realized only with crude oil prices over $70 per barrel. As such, domestic CTL processes may have better near-term deployability in smaller modular-scale plants located strategically (e.g., at military bases) or according to special economic and environmental opportunities or needs.
