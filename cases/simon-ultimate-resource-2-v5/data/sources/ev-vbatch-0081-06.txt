The most widely used and comprehensive data on food supply and consumption is published by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). This data is annually available and is updated by the FAO, extending back to 1961. Over the decades since 1961, there has been a consistent global uptrend in the per capita calorie supply, reflecting changes and advancements in food production, distribution, and consumption patterns worldwide. There has been a substantial increase in caloric supply in Asia and Africa in recent decades. The steeper rise across poorer regions of the world meant that over the last few decades, global trends in caloric supply have been converging. Note that supply figures do not include consumption-level waste (i.e. food that is wasted at retail, restaurant, and household levels), and therefore represent food available for consumption at the retail level, rather than actual food intake.
