Synthesis:
From 1789 to 1820, European colonial holdings near the United States were generally weakened by war, revolution, and imperial decline—especially Spain’s inability to sustain control in Latin America and France’s loss of Saint-Domingue. Yet Britain remained a major naval and imperial power, so it could still threaten U.S. shipping and border interests. The principal danger was not weak colonies themselves but renewed European intervention or recolonization after the Napoleonic era, which prompted the Monroe Doctrine in 1823.
