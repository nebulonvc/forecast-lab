Synthesis:
Spanish Florida became a dangerous annoyance to the United States because it sheltered runaway enslaved people alongside Seminole and Creek peoples. As American settlement expanded, these Black Seminole communities threatened slaveholders and prompted U.S. demands for their return; Washington and Jefferson took notice in the 1790s. The refuge also enabled Seminole resistance, culminating in U.S. raids and the First Seminole War of 1816–1818.
