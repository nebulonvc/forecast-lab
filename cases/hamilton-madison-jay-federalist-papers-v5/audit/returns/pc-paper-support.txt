No. Paper-money and debt-relief measures were state-specific, not a nationwide policy. In the 1780s, several states adopted them, but Rhode Island’s radical debt-cancellation proposals remained confined to that state. After 1819, stay laws, minimum-appraisal laws, and related relief appeared in numerous states, while others rejected them. The Constitution barred states from issuing paper money, but states continued using other relief mechanisms.
