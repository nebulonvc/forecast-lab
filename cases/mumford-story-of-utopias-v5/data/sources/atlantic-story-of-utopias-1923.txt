THE unfortunate mental squint into which the word Utopia throws most of us is corrected pretty fully after donning the spectacles of Mr. Mumford’s first chapter. We find as we take the journey through the broad territories of Utopia, which extend from Plato to H. G. Wells and after, that, with Mr. Mumford as guide, such traveling is as important educationally as a trip to the Orient; and so the book grows into a peculiarly practical treatise. ’In the midst of the tepid and half-hearted discussions that continue to arise out of prohibition laws and strikes and “peace" conferences, let us break in with the injunction to talk about fundamentals—consider Utopia!’ We consider it through all of the classic treatises on this highly abused country, from Plato to H. C. Wells. Some of these are amusing, some fascinating, some dull, and there are times, when one wonders if the travel over this part of Utopia is worth the discomfort of the journey. But most of the chapters are written with grace and easy diction, and the large feats of summary and judgment performed are apt to pass unrecognized.
