World War I, once called “the war to end all wars,” killed roughly 20 million soldiers and civilians. But the conflict that followed just two decades later was far deadlier. World War II’s total death toll is generally estimated at 60 million to 80 million, including those who died from combat, genocide, famine, disease and other war-related causes.
“The Great War industrialized killing on the frontlines; World War II industrialized total destruction everywhere,” says MacGregor.
