For most of the last century, the preeminent role of the U.S. dollar in the global economy has been supported by the size and strength of the U.S. economy, its stability and openness to trade and capital flows, and strong property rights and the rule of law. As a result, the depth and liquidity of U.S. financial markets is unmatched, and there is a large supply of extremely safe dollar-denominated assets. The role of the U.S. dollar has received renewed attention this year. A sharp rise in policy uncertainty has led some to question the strength and stability of the U.S. economy. In particular, increased attention has been paid to the U.S. fiscal outlook, as noted in the recent downgrade by Moody's of the credit rating on U.S. government debt. According to our index combining various factors, the dollar's international usage is little changed over the past 5 years and far exceeds the U.S. share of global GDP and trade (see Figure 1).
