Section 7. State-owned Enterprises (SOEs) Under General Secretary Xi Jinping, SOEs are playing an increasingly prominent role in China’s economy, particularly in strategic sectors such as energy, infrastructure, and technology. In 2024, including at the Third Plenum, Chinese leadership repeatedly pledged to make state-owned enterprises “bigger, better, and stronger,” signaling continued state support for and expansion of SOEs. China’s state-owned sector comprises SOEs controlled by the central government as well as provincial, municipal, and other subnational government-controlled SOEs. According to the Stanford Center on China’s Economy and Institutions, of the nearly 40 million corporations in China, 363,000 firms are 100-percent state-owned, 629,000 are 30-percent state-owned, and 867,000 have at least some state ownership. SOEs and mixed SOE-private companies account for nearly 60 percent of total stock market capitalization. SOEs comprise 30 to 40 percent of GDP and 20 percent of urban employment, spanning all sectors from tourism to heavy industries. Funds controlled by national or subnational governments may hold majority or controlling shares in ostensibly private enterprises. Leading SOEs benefit from preferential government policies to develop “national champions;” 16 national SOE entities are “Chain Masters” overseeing industrial chains in important sectors, giving SOEs control over private enterprise access to business lines. SOEs enjoy preferential access to essential economic inputs, such as: Land Hydrocarbons Finance Telecommunications Agriculture Electricity and Human resource recruitment SOEs also exercise considerable power in markets such as metals and minerals, gaining preferential access to credit and public equity, and debt markets. The State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission (SASAC) controls China’s 96 national-level SOEs . Provincial and local governments also control significant numbers of SOEs which may also hold significant stakes in private firms.
