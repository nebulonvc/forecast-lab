Manufacturing value added in the U.S. in 2025 was $2.4 trillion in chained 2017 dollars or 10.0 % of GDP (Bureau of Economic Analysis 2026a). This amounts to $2.9 Trillion in current dollars. As seen in Figure 2.3, among the 10 largest manufacturing nations, U.S. manufacturing value added, as measured in constant 2020 dollars, is the second largest national manufacturing economy (United Nations Statistics Division 2026). In current dollars, the U.S. produced $2.9 trillion in manufacturing valued added while China produced $4.7 trillion in 2024. In terms of subsectors of manufacturing, China produces more than the U.S. in 11 of the 14 subsectors shown in Figure 2.8. When aggregated together, U.S. and European manufacturing value added exceeds that of Eastern and South-eastern Asia (excluding Japan) for 7 of the 14 subsectors. Computer, electronic, and optical products is among those that Asia produces more value added.
An economic input-output analysis using OECD inter-country input-output data, shown in Table 3.5 shows that U.S. manufacturing products have an estimated 12.9 % of their intermediate goods and services being from foreign origin . When focusing only on the manufactured goods imported, the U.S. manufacturing industry imports 7.9 % of its intermediate manufactured goods. For food, discrete, process, and discrete tech manufacturing, the imported portion of intermediate goods/services is 10.8%, 14.7%, 7.7%, and 14.7% respectively.
