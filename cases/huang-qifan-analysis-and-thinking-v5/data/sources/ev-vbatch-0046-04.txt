Hottman Share X LinkedIn Facebook Bluesky Threads Email Link Working Paper 34901 DOI 10.3386/w34901 Issue Date February 2026 Revision Date November 2026 The 2018–2019 U.S. tariffs more than doubled customs duties collected, and the prices paid by U.S. importers rose one-for-one with them. This does not mean that U.S. buyers bore all the incidence of the tariffs. For a buyer who purchases along a price schedule, where unit prices fall with order size, an envelope argument shows that her first-order welfare loss depends on the shift in the price schedule she faces, not on the total realized price change. Using administrative transaction-level data on the universe of U.S. imports, we document that such schedules are pervasive. The price schedule U.S. importers faced shifted up by 60 percent of the tariff, not 100 percent. U.S. buyer surplus fell by about $19 billion per year against about $29 billion per year in incremental tariff revenue: domestic buyers bore about 64 cents of each tariff dollar collected, and foreign exporters bore the rest.
