The US Dollar has served as the world's primary reserve currency since the Bretton Woods agreement in 1944, which established the framework that underpinned the post-World War II international monetary system. As of Q2 2026, the USD holds 56.70% of allocated global foreign exchange reserves, totaling approximately $7.5 trillion. While this represents a significant decline from about 71% in 2000, the dollar remains by far the most held reserve currency globally, with no immediate challenger.
