New trade-restrictive measures surge but WTO members step up trade facilitation efforts
The pace of newly-introduced tariffs and other non-facilitating measures rose sharply in the first half of 2026, while the stockpile of such measures reached a record level, according to the WTO Director-General's latest annual overview of developments in the international trading environment. At the same time, WTO members introduced a significant number of trade-facilitating measures, helping to sustain global trade flows despite heightened geopolitical tensions and economic uncertainty.

New trade-facilitating measures accelerated sharply in the first half of 2026. WTO members and observers introduced 466 such measures, exceeding the 391 recorded during all of 2025.

The report covers trade and trade-related measures up to 30 June 2026, presenting annual data for 2025 and semi-annual data for the period January to June 2026, while also tracking the accumulation of measures introduced since 2009 and still in force.
As of 30 June 2026, the stockpile
of import-related restrictive and other non-facilitating measures introduced since 2009 and still in force, covered USD 5.4 trillion of trade, equivalent to 21.3 per cent of world imports. The stockpile of such export-related measures reached USD 1.5 trillion, covering 6.3 per cent of world exports. Both figures represent the highest levels recorded since the WTO trade monitoring began.
Highlighting two contrasting trends, WTO Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala said: "On the one hand, import tariffs and other non-facilitating measures affect significant shares of global trade, and the stockpile of trade-restrictive measures continues to grow, reaching its highest level since WTO trade monitoring began in 2009 and affecting an increasing share of global trade. On the other hand, WTO members have stepped up their efforts to facilitate trade, introducing measures that have helped businesses navigate recent disruptions and maintain the flow of goods across borders."
"In most sectors examined, new import-facilitating measures covered a larger share of imports than new restrictive and non-facilitating measures. The only exceptions were electric vehicles and photovoltaic cells, where major economies
heavily competing for market share, which has sparked lots of trade policy debates recently", DG Okonjo-Iweala noted.
She added: "I welcome these trade-facilitating actions and encourage WTO members to build on this positive momentum. At a time of a rapidly changing global trading environment, restraint in the use of new trade restrictions and a continued commitment to open, predictable and transparent trade will be essential for supporting economic growth, strengthening resilience and preserving the benefits of the multilateral trading system."

Global trade proved more resilient than expected in the first half of 2026 despite the disruption caused by the Middle East conflict, driven in large part by surging demand for AI-enabling goods, according to the latest "
Global Trade Outlook and Statistics
" released on 8 October 2026. The WTO has revised its forecast for world merchandise trade volume growth in 2026 upward to 3.9 per cent, from the 1.9 per cent baseline forecast issued in March. In value terms, merchandise trade increased by around 15 per cent year-on-year during the first half of 2026.
Commercial services trade growth is now forecast at 3.3 per cent in 2026, down from the March forecast of 4.8 per cent owing largely to the impact of the Middle East conflict on transport and travel services. Nevertheless, commercial services trade in value terms increased by around 14 per cent year-on-year in the first half of 2026.
Trade remedy actions continued to be widely used, although the trade affected by these measures remained limited. In 2025, WTO members initiated 391 investigations, averaging 32.6 initiations per month. During the first half of 2026, 218 new investigations were initiated, averaging 36.3 per month, while only 31 trade remedy actions were terminated. Anti-dumping measures remained the most frequently used trade remedy instrument.
WTO members and observers introduced 130 new measures affecting trade in services in 2025 and a further 52 measures during the first six months of 2026. Most were aimed at facilitating trade or strengthening regulatory frameworks, while many applied across multiple sectors and modes of supply.
WTO members and observers continued to make extensive use of general economic support measures, with industrial and agricultural support remaining at historically high levels and increasingly focused on competitiveness, resilience, security of supply and climate objectives.
