The Zimbabwe Gold ( ZiG ; code : ZWG ) [ 3 ] is the official currency of Zimbabwe since 8 April 2024, [ 2 ] backed by US$1.6 billion worth of hard assets: foreign currencies, gold, and other precious metals. [ 4 ] [ 5 ] [ 6 ] [ 7 ] [ 8 ] It replaced the 2019–2024 Zimbabwean dollar , which had suffered from rapid depreciation , with the official exchange rate surpassing 30,000 Zimbabwean dollars per US dollar on 5 April 2024, whilst the parallel market rate reached 40,000 per US dollar. [ 9 ] Prior to the introduction of the ZiG, annual inflation in Zimbabwe hit 55.3% in March 2024. [ 10 ]

Though, in the first six months after its introduction the ZiG lost half of its value, later the exchange rate of the ZiG stabilized and since November 2024 the rate has been around 25 to 27 ZiG per US Dollar. [ 11 ]

The ZiG currency was announced on 5 April 2024, taking the name from ZiG digital tokens, which are now available as GBDT . [ 14 ] The ZiG is Zimbabwe's sixth attempt since 2008 at creating a new currency that will make it independent of the US dollar. [ 15 ]

Since the currency crisis of 2008–2009, Zimbabwe has a multi-currency system. It was introduced in 2009 after the hyperinflation of the fourth Zimbabwean dollar (ZWL). For ten years there was no Zimbabwean currency. However, in February 2019 the government introduced the Zimdollar (ZWL) on par with the US dollar, but it was quickly devalued to $2.50 Zimdollars per US-dollar. [ 16 ] Briefly, from June 2019 to March 2020, the government required all transactions to be done in Zimdollars, but that quickly proved impractical. [ 17 ] Legal use of foreign currencies was restored and they remain legal tender. [ 18 ]
