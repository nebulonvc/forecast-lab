Established in 1310, following a failed attempt to overthrow Doge Piero Gradenigo, the Council of Ten was the exclusive committee responsible for the security of the Venetian state. The Council was made up of seventeen men, including ten ordinary members who served annual tenures, the Doge’s six ducal councillors, who did not have any voting rights during the Council’s assemblies, and the Doge as the ceremonial figurehead.
