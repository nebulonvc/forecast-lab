Whenwespeak of depopulation caused by enclosures, itmust, ofcourse, be understood that this refers to rural depopulation and not to a general depopulation ofthe wholecountry. On thecontrary, during the period under review, the population ofthecountry asa wholeincreased rapidly.[44] About 7,000,000 in the middle oftheeighteenth century, it had risen to 8,892,536 in 1801, to 12,000,236 in 1821,and by 1861 had reached 20,066,224. This wasan increasealmostsolely in urban districts,and we havealready seen how, in addition to the naturalincreasein the population, thetowns werefed fromthecountry-side.
