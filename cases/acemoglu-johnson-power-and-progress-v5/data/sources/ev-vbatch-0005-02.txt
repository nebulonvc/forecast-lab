For New Grads Looking for Work, the Struggle Is Real – But Not for All
More new graduates are creating profiles on Indeed, and it’s very likely many of them are there because their job searches have gotten harder.
The indexed share of new graduates with Indeed profiles that were created or modified during their year of graduation increased dramatically between 2023 and 2025 – growing by 67% among bachelor’s degree recipients and 61% among master’s degree recipients.
The acceleration coincides with a significantly harder job market for new graduates. The unemployment rate for recent grads hit 5.7% in Q4 2025, a three-year high, while the share of unemployed Americans who are new workforce entrants reached a 37-year peak.
The surge is not uniform across fields of study. Fields where graduates are typically placed through more-established routes, including clinical rotations, student teaching, and/or apprenticeships, show stable Indeed engagement regardless of broader market conditions. Fields with open, competitive job markets show the sharpest increases in profile creation.
For years, the share of college graduates who created profiles on Indeed by the end of their graduation year grew slowly and steadily. Then, starting in 2024, it surged.
Among bachelor’s degree recipients, the indexed share of US graduates who created or modified their Indeed profile during their year of graduation rose from 100 in 2018 to 134 in 2023 – a gradual 34% climb over five years. It then jumped to 177 in 2024 and 223 in 2025. Put another way, profile creation rose by 34% over the five years from 2018 to 2023, then shot up another 67% in just the two years between 2023 and 2025. Master’s degree recipients followed the same arc: a slow rise from 81 to 105 between 2018 and 2023 (+29% in five years), then a sharp acceleration to 169 by 2025 (+61% in two years).
