Since its launch, Tech Policy Press has covered the findings of the annual Freedom on the Net report from Freedom House, a nonprofit organization founded in 1941 to advance democracy and human rights. As we noted last year , it is typically grim reading, a chronicle of governments suppressing dissent, people arrested for nonviolent expression, tallies of those attacked or killed for their online activities, and other threats to internet freedom. It should come as no surprise to our readers that the topline results in this year’s report correspond to more of the same, as authoritarianism continues its advance across the planet. Freedom House finds that “Global internet freedom declined for the 16th consecutive year.” Among the most significant developments was Iran, which, amid demonstrations that rocked the country earlier this year and the subsequent United States and Israeli military strikes on the country, “imposed the longest nationwide internet shutdown Freedom on the Net has ever recorded.” In Russia, the Putin regime “consolidated control over internet service providers (ISPs),” further restricting internet access and introducing new censorship measures. But the problem goes far beyond these worst offenders: only 9.1% of the world’s internet users live in countries that Freedom on the Net considers “free.” The US remains in that increasingly lonely category, but it has seen its standings decline substantially over the years. Pointing to measures across multiple countries targeting the use of AI for online harassment and impersonation, as well as mitigation measures such as labeling AI-generated content, Freedom on the Net says some “raised concerns about overreach.” India, for example, introduced rules requiring prominent labels on AI-generated content while shortening the period in which platforms must remove content flagged by authorities from 36 hours to three hours. Civil society groups warned that the shorter deadline could encourage platforms to remove legitimate journalism and satire rather than risk penalties.
