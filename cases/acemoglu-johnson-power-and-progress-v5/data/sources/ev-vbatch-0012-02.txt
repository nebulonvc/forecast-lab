Connecting the world and beyond
6.3 billion people now online, yet digital divides persist
ITU’s Facts and Figures 2026 shows growth in global Internet use, while 2 billion remain unconnected
ICT for Sustainable Development
​​​​An estimated 6.3 billion people, or 76 per cent of the world's population, are using the Internet in 2026, according to
released today by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU).
The figure is up from a revised estimate of 6.1 billion people, or 74 per cent, in 2025. Despite the continued growth in connectivity, 2 billion people remain offline.
“In the age of AI, being offline means being shut out of countless opportunities, from education to employment," said Doreen Bogdan-Martin, ITU Secretary-General. "Now more than ever, we need to expand connectivity and make it meaningful for everyone, everywhere."
With Internet use now estimated to extend to more than three-quarters of global population,
shows continued progress toward universal connectivity.
At the same time, the data reveal persistent disparities in who is connected. As example, Internet use stands at 93 per cent in high income countries, compared with just 32 per cent in low-income countries.
Closing gaps in the affordability, availability, quality and use of digital technologies and services is essential to achieving universal and meaningful connectivity, when everyone can use the Internet under optimal conditions, at an affordable cost, wherever and whenever they need.
“The continued expansion of global connectivity creates opportunities for socio-economic development and supports wider access to emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence," said Cosmas Luckyson Zavazava, Director of ITU's Telecommunication Development Bureau. “Facts and Figures 2026 shows us the significant progress achieved so far and the work that lies ahead of us to translate access to digital technologies into meaningful connections that can improve people's lives. For this to happen, we should also prioritize the delivery of digital skills. Without digital skills and meaningful connectivity, the benefits of technologies risk remaining out of reach for many, reinforcing existing digital divides."
Digital divides are narrowing, but remain significant
The latest edition of ITU's annual connectivity assessment provides global, regional and income group estimates for indicators related to Internet use, subscriptions and traffic as well as affordability, digital skills, mobile network coverage and mobile phone ownership.
Globally, 79 per cent of men and 74 per cent of women use the Internet. The ratio of women to men using the Internet has increased to 0.94.
Young people remain more connected than the rest of the population, with 86 per cent of people aged 15 to 24 using the Internet globally, compared with 74 per cent of the rest of the population.
The urban-rural divide has narrowed, although clear disparities persist. Globally, 82 per cent of urban residents have access to the Internet, compared with 63 per cent in rural areas.
Globally, 84 per cent of the population aged 10 and over own a mobile phone. Ownership exceeds 95 per cent in high-income countries, compared with 57 per cent in low-income countries.
An estimated 97 per cent of the global population lives within reach of at least a 3G network. However, coverage by more advanced technologies remains highly unequal. While 5G reaches 59 per cent of the global population, coverage ranges from 86 per cent in high-income countries to just 4 per cent in low-income countries.

Making connectivity work for everyone in the AI era
According to the report, both landlocked developing countries (LLDCs) and small island developing states (SIDS) met, for the first time in 2026, a widely recognized affordability target of 2 per cent of GNI per capita for the mobile broadband basket.
In least developed countries (LDCs), however, the median price remains at 6.1 per cent of GNI per capita. Globally, three in ten economies meet neither the fixed nor the mobile broadband affordability target.
As AI reshapes economies and societies, the report notes that connectivity, including its affordability, and digital skills will determine who benefits from advancing technologies, and that closing the global digital divide is essential so that no one is left behind in the digital future.

The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) is the United Nations agency for digital technologies, driving innovation for people and the planet with 194 Member States and a membership of over 1,000 companies, universities, civil society, and international and regional organizations. Established in 1865, ITU coordinates the global use of the radio spectrum and satellite orbits, establishes international technology standards, drives universal connectivity and digital services, and is helping to make sure everyone benefits from sustainable digital transformation, including the most remote communities.
