The OECD Employment Outlook 2025 highlights both resilience and looming structural challenges in labour markets. The findings underscore the urgent need to adapt workforce strategies to address demographic shifts, productivity concerns, and evolving employee expectations. Main Findings Strong labour market, but slowing growth Employment and participation are at record highs across the OECD, with unemployment at just under 5%. However, job creation is decelerating, and labour market tightness is easing. Demographic headwinds Ageing populations and declining fertility are increasing the old-age dependency ratio. Without policy and business adaptation, GDP per capita growth is expected to decrease by almost half in the coming decades. Untapped labour potential Greater participation by women, older workers, and migrants is seen as crucial to sustain growth and offset shortages. Productivity slowdown Productivity growth remains weak, partly because older workers tend to change jobs less frequently. Wage growth lags, particularly for low-wage workers, which raises concerns about competitiveness. Older workers’ employability While people are living longer, adaptation to technological and organisational change is more complex for some. Cognitive decline and limited re-skilling opportunities may reduce productivity if not addressed. Implications for Employers 1. Workforce planning Businesses must prepare for a tighter labour supply. Strategic workforce planning, including retention, succession, and redesign of retirement policies, will be key to securing talent. 2. Leveraging older workers Companies should implement measures such as flexible schedules, ergonomic redesign, phased retirement, and targeted training. These practices help sustain productivity and transfer knowledge across generations. 3. Supporting mobility and skills The slowdown in job reallocation necessitates that employers promote internal mobility, upskilling, and career progression. Active management of skills ensures both firm competitiveness and employee engagement. 4. Expanding talent pools Employers can benefit significantly from better integrating women returning to the workforce and migrants. Inclusive hiring, return-to-work schemes, and the removal of workplace barriers not only support growth but also address labour gaps, paving the way for a more diverse and productive workforce. 5.
