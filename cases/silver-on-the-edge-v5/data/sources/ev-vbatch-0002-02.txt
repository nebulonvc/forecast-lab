Revolution at WSOP 2026: ESPN Deploys AI to Spot Bluffs. The prestigious World Series of Poker (WSOP) series has made a comeback on ESPN with technology sparking both awe and ethical debates. Under the guidance of production company Omaha Productions, owned by NFL legend Peyton Manning, this year's live broadcasts of the Main Event feature artificial intelligence capable of detecting player bluffs. The tool, developed by independent AI engineer and avid poker player Luke Geel, is more than just a simple algorithm. This system operates based on computer vision, analyzing subtle details that are nearly imperceptible to the human eye: Smile symmetry – according to Geel, a symmetric smile is usually more genuine. To avoid accusations of influencing the game, AI analysis will only appear in broadcasts after a player has been eliminated from the tournament. Interestingly, the system's creator himself doubts its effectiveness against the absolute world's elite. "If I deployed it on a top professional like Daniel Negreanu, the system would probably find no signals. I assume he's worked hard to eliminate them," Geel remarked. Not everyone in the poker community welcomes the innovation with enthusiasm, with some calling it a "solution to a non-existent problem." Critics argue that strategy based on mathematics and board composition is far more crucial than eye movements, which can often be misleading.
