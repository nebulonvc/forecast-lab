Title: Market Attention and Information Quality in On-Chain Prediction Markets: Evidence from Polymarket (arXiv:2610.37669, Submitted on 4 Aug 2026). Abstract: This paper asks whether the leading on-chain prediction market, Polymarket, behaves more as an information market, in which monetary stakes compress dispersed beliefs into a price that tracks the truth, or more as an attention market, in which capital flows toward the most visible contracts rather than the most informative ones. Using trade-level data from Polymarket's central-limit-order-book (CLOB) markets from January 2024 through March 2026, and a one-sided exchange-contract convention that avoids double-counting maker and taker fills, we study the top 1,000 markets by taker-side dollar volume. For each, we build an attention score from the within-sample percentile ranks of volume, transaction count, and active trader wallets, and an information-quality score on resolved Yes/No markets equal to one minus the absolute distance between the YES-token price seven days before resolution and the realised outcome. Placing markets on this attention/information plane answers the question in favour of the information-market reading: 68.2% of analysis-sample volume sits in well-calibrated markets (average 7-day error of 0.03, falling to 0.02 one day before resolution), spread across 515 of 679 contracts. A narrower, identifiable cluster of 19 high-attention/low-information contracts captures 22.1% of analysis-sample volume, but 79% of that mass is the 2024 US Presidential Election, the single closest macro-political event of the window. Polymarket is therefore primarily an information aggregator; the attention component is real but small and concentrated in a handful of headline contracts whose outcomes are hardest to forecast ex ante.
