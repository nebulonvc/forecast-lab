In 1867, Prussia and France were on the brink of war over Luxembourg. This conference resulted in the signing of the Treaty of London on 11 May 1867, which stipulated that Luxembourg would retain its independence. At the same time, the country was declared demilitarised and perpetually neutral.
The First World War began with the German invasion of Luxembourg and Belgium on 2 and 4 August 1914. While Luxembourg protested the violation of its neutrality, it also sought to reach a modus vivendi with the German Empire, which occupied the country until the war ended in 1918.
It was only after a second invasion on 10 May 1940 that Luxembourg altered its foreign policy stance: the government-in-exile aligned itself with the Allied forces and introduced compulsory military service for male citizens in 1944.
