In June 1959 President Soekarno, with the support of the army, centralized political power in his hands by dismissing the elected representatives. The parliamentary seats were reassigned to representatives of regional interests and of “functional” groups. Examples of the latter are youth and women’s groups, and religious, labor, and peasant organizations. This form of “guided democracy” lasted until October 1965 when the armed forces took over after suppressing a communist coup.
