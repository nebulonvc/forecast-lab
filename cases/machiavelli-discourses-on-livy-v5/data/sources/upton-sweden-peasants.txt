Sweden was a 
peasant society to a degree that was unusual even in early modern Europe. In 
France, peasant agriculture supported some 70% of the population; in Sweden it 
was 95%. Sweden had a small population spread thinly over a large geographical 
area. A typical settlement was of three to six farmsteads with a fringe population of 
cottagers, labourers and farm servants attached. The Swedish peasant was always a 
tenant, but there were three main types of tenure: tax-peasants, crown peasants and 
nobles' peasants.
