Many Americans use social media to connect with one another, engage with news content, share information and entertain themselves. Explore the patterns and trends shaping the social media landscape. About this research This fact sheet is the latest in our long-running series on technology adoption in the United States. For more than a decade, we’ve surveyed Americans to understand which social media platforms they use. Why did we do this? Pew Research Center does research to inform the public, journalists and decision-makers. This research builds on our past work studying social media platforms Americans use . Learn more about Pew Research Center . How did we do this? This analysis is based on findings from the National Public Opinion Reference Survey, a Center survey of 5,511 U.S. adults. We allowed people to respond online, by mail or by phone. The survey represents the views of all U.S. adults. The survey was conducted Jan. 30-June 18, 2026. Its overall margin of error is plus or minus 1.8 percentage points. This analysis also includes past data. The ways we’ve allowed people to respond to these surveys have evolved over the years, including in 2023 and again in 2024. For more on these shifts and how we approached them, read our mobile and broadband fact sheets and our Q&A . Here are the questions used for this analysis , the topline and the survey methodology . Which social media platforms are most common? YouTube and Facebook are the most widely used social media platforms in the United States. About half of U.S. adults say they use Instagram, and smaller shares use sites or apps such as TikTok, Reddit, Snapchat and X (formerly Twitter).
