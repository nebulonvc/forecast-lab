Global number of Internet users increases, but disparities deepen key digital divides. ITU’s Facts and Figures 2025 shows steady progress in connectivity, while highlighting gaps in quality and affordability. The world's online population grew by more than 240 million people in 2025, according to Facts and Figures 2025 released today by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU). Globally, an estimated 6 billion people – about three-quarters of the world's population – are using the Internet in 2025, up from a revised estimate of 5.8 billion in 2024. However, 2.2 billion people remain offline, down from a revised estimate of 2.3 billion in 2024. For the first time, Facts and Figures estimates the total number of 5G subscriptions, which now account for about one-third – or around 3 billion – of all mobile broadband subscriptions worldwide. In 2025, 5G networks are estimated to cover 55 per cent of the world's population, reflecting strong momentum in advanced mobile technologies. Coverage, however, remains uneven, with 84 per cent of people in high-income countries having access to 5G, compared with only 4 per cent in low-income countries. A typical user in a high-income country now generates nearly eight times more mobile data than one in a low-income country.
