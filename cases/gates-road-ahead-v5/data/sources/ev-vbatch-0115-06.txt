How we did this For this analysis, we surveyed 5,166 U.S. adults from Aug. 24 to 30, 2026. Everyone who took part in these surveys is a member of the Center’s American Trends Panel . The surveys represent the views of the full U.S. adult population.
A large majority of U.S. adults (88%) say they at least sometimes get news from digital devices like a smartphone, computer or tablet. This includes 59% who say they do so often – up slightly from 2025, when 56% of Americans said they often get news from digital devices.
Digital devices are by far the most common way Americans get news, although a majority (63%) also get news from television at least sometimes. This includes 31% who say they often get news from TV, a share that has stayed fairly steady over the last few years.
Americans turn to radio and print publications for news less frequently. As of 2026, 12% of U.S. adults say they often get news from radio, and 6% say the same about printed newspapers or magazines. Both of these shares are roughly the same as last year.
About a third of Americans say they often or sometimes get news from email newsletters (34%) or podcasts (32%).
