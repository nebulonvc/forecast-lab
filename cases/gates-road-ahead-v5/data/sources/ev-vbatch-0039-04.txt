Americans’ Use of Mobile Devices in 2026

Large shares of Americans are connected to the world of digital information while on the go via smartphones and other mobile devices. Explore the patterns and trends that have shaped the mobile revolution below.
This fact sheet is the latest in our long-running series on technology adoption in the United States. For over a decade, we’ve surveyed Americans on subjects like who has a smartphone and whether they rely on smartphones for internet.
Pew Research Center does research to inform the public, journalists and decision-makers. This research builds on our past work studying Americans’ smartphone use and the digital divide.
Learn more about Pew Research Center.
This analysis is based on findings from the National Public Opinion Reference Survey, a Center survey of 5,511 U.S. adults. We allowed people to respond online, by mail or by phone. The survey represents the views of all U.S. adults.
The survey was conducted Jan. 30-June 18, 2026. Its overall margin of error is plus or minus 1.8 percentage points.
This analysis also includes past data. The ways we’ve allowed people to respond to these surveys have evolved over the years, including in 2023 and again in 2024. For more on these shifts and how we approached them, read our Q&A.
Here are the questions used for this analysis, the topline and the survey methodology.
The vast majority of U.S. adults (98%) own a cellphone of some kind. About nine-in-ten (91%) own a smartphone, up from just 35% in the Center’s first survey of smartphone ownership conducted in 2011.

Who owns cellphones and smartphones?
Substantial majorities of Americans across a wide range of demographic groups are cellphone owners. The same is true for smartphone ownership – though some differences do emerge, particularly by age, household income and education.
