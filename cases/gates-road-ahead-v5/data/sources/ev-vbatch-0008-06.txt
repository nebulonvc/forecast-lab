GTFS-RT in practice: what operators get wrong about real-time feeds | Pysae. GTFS-RT has become the de facto standard for real-time public transport data. Passenger apps, national open data portals, journey planners, and MaaS platforms all consume it. For operators, publishing a GTFS-RT feed has moved from an optional extra to a contractual obligation in most new DSP agreements. But adoption and quality are two different things. A large number of operators publish GTFS-RT feeds that are technically valid but operationally unreliable. Ghost buses in passenger apps often come from GTFS-RT trip IDs or stops that no longer match the static GTFS, which is why both feeds should be generated from one source. A reliable GTFS-RT implementation meets the contractual refresh rate, stays synchronised with static GTFS, is actively monitored for anomalies and handles GPS dropouts with a defined fallback strategy.
