Federal Communications Commission DA 17-71, Annual Assessment of the Status of Competition in the Market for the Delivery of Video Programming, Eighteenth Report, Adopted and Released January 17, 2017. Para. 2: At the end of 2015, cable MVPDs accounted for 53.1 percent of all MVPD subscribers, down from 53.4 percent at the end of 2014. Direct broadcast satellite (DBS) MVPDs accounted for 33.2 percent of MVPD subscribers at the end of 2015, down slightly from 33.3 percent at the end of 2014. Telephone company MVPDs accounted for 13.4 percent of MVPD subscribers at the end of 2015, up from 12.9 percent at the end of 2014. Para. 35: At the end of 2015, telephone company MVPDs offered video services to over 52.2 million households, up from 51.1 million in 2014. Table III.A.1 estimates housing units passed at year-end 2015: AT&T U-verse 28.0 million, Verizon FiOS 20.0 million, CenturyLink 3.2 million, Consolidated Comm. 0.6 million, Cincinnati Bell 0.4 million. Para. 62 Digital Technology: Wireline MVPDs continue to upgrade their systems by transitioning their analog channels to digital, which frees up bandwidth for additional services (e.g., more digital channels, more HD channels, more VOD programming, and faster Internet speeds). The transition from analog to digital generally requires deployment of additional set-top boxes and digital terminal adapters to access the MVPDs' video programming. According to SNL Kagan, most major cable MVPDs had completed, or nearly completed, their all-digital transition at the end of 2015. Para. 72: Although video margins are declining, most MVPDs also offer Internet services with margins near 60 percent and voice services with margins near 20 percent.
