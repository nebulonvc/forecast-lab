INTRODUCTION Global cross-border flows of goods, services and capital have slowed down markedly since the global financial crisis (Figure 1), reversing a multi-decade expansion dating to the middle of the 20th century. The reversal has occurred against the backdrop of increasing trade tensions between the US and China, and more generally a rise in populism and greater scepticism about the benefits of globalisation (Ottaviano et al. 2021).
