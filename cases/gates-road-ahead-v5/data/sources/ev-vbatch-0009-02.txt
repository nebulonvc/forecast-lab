Americans’ Use of Internet, Broadband in 2026
The internet represents a fundamental shift in how Americans connect with one another, gather information and conduct their day-to-day lives. For more than 25 years, Pew Research Center has documented the internet’s growth and distribution in the United States. Explore the patterns of internet and home broadband adoption below.
This analysis is based on findings from the National Public Opinion Reference Survey, a Center survey of 5,511 U.S. adults. We allowed people to respond online, by mail or by phone. The survey represents the views of all U.S. adults.
The survey was conducted Jan. 30-June 18, 2026. Its overall margin of error is plus or minus 1.8 percentage points.
When the Center began asking Americans about their internet usage in early 2000, about half of all adults were already online. Today, 97% of U.S. adults say they use the internet.
For some demographic groups – such as younger adults, those with at least some college education and those from high-income households – internet usage is near universal.
Home broadband use over time
The share of U.S. adults with high-speed broadband service at home increased rapidly between 2000 and 2010. And that growth continues today: 78% of U.S. adults now say they subscribe to a broadband internet service at home.
As is true of internet adoption more broadly, home broadband adoption varies across demographic groups. For instance, those with lower income levels and formal education are less likely to say they subscribe to a broadband service at home.
Smartphone dependency over time
Today, 16% of U.S. adults are “smartphone-only” internet users, meaning they own a smartphone but do not subscribe to a home broadband service.
Reliance on smartphones for online access is especially common among Americans with lower household incomes and those with less formal education.
