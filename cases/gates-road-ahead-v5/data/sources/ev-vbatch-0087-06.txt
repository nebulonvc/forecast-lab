Increased network reliability and beefier speeds and feeds are both core to DOCSIS 4.0, but overall costs remain a critical decision point as cable operators explore which path to take with their HFC networks.
There are still decisions to be made on HFC. Alongside some fiber-to-the-premises (FTTP) overbuilds (such as what Optimum Communications is doing in the northeastern US), operators are likewise mulling whether to upgrade that existing plant to DOCSIS 4.0 or to squeeze more out of DOCSIS 3.1, including the relatively new downstream-boosting DOCSIS 3.1+ variant .
"The key parameters are cost of labor of upgrading networks," Paul Broadhurst, CEO of Technetix, explained last week on a DOCSIS 4.0/PON panel at the Cable Next-Gen Technologies & Strategies event in Denver.
That parameter is geo-specific. In North America, where D4.0 network upgrades are in the range of $100 to $200 per home passed (depending on the operator), that could be the right move. In Latin America, where labor costs are lower, FTTP could be the better solution.
To wit, Liberty Latin America CEO Balan Nair said here last week that his company will not be upgrading to DOCSIS 4.0. In North America, the scene is different among a few tier ones. Comcast, currently the D4.0 deployment leader, has deployed the platform to "millions" of homes . Charter Communications will deploy D4.0 to about 35% of its HFC footprint (not counting the footprint coming way of the FCC-approved Cox Communications merger ), Mediacom Communications has begun D4.0 deployments , and Rogers Communications also intends to upgrade to D4.0 in portions of its HFC footprint.
There's still a case to be made for PON. While DOCSIS has delivered incremental upgrades over the years, going with PON is the future-proof path, said Doug Blue, broadband business development leader at Nokia, which is squarely in the PON camp. He wondered, for example, how cable operators would respond if, say, an AT&T or Verizon launched a 5-Gig fiber service for $70 per month.
Jay Lee, chief technology officer and general manager, access networking at ATX Networks, agreed that HFC enhancements still make the most sense in brownfields, but PON is a "no-brainer in new areas."
