For this analysis, we surveyed 5,166 U.S. adults from Aug. 24 to 30, 2026. Everyone who took part in these surveys is a member of the Center’s American Trends Panel. The surveys represent the views of the full U.S. adult population.
A large majority of U.S. adults (88%) say they at least sometimes get news from digital devices like a smartphone, computer or tablet. This includes 59% who say they do so often – up slightly from 2025, when 56% of Americans said they often get news from digital devices.
Americans turn to radio and print publications for news less frequently. As of 2026, 12% of U.S. adults say they often get news from radio, and 6% say the same about printed newspapers or magazines. Both of these shares are roughly the same as last year.
There are several different pathways Americans use to get news on their digital devices. News websites or apps, social media, and search engines are the most common: More than half of U.S. adults say they at least sometimes get news in each of these ways. The shares of Americans who get news from social media and search have both increased since 2025.
Artificial intelligence chatbots such as ChatGPT or Google Gemini have become a source of news for more Americans in the past year. As of summer 2026, 15% say they get news from AI chatbots at least sometimes, up from 9% in 2025.
