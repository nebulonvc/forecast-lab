Freelancers, independent contractors, and other forms of external talent now play a central role in how many organizations operate. Businesses increasingly rely on project-based specialists to access skills quickly, scale teams without increasing permanent headcount, and deliver work more efficiently.
What used to be occasional freelance hiring is now embedded in workforce strategy.
As reliance on contractors and external specialists grows, so does operational complexity. Independent contractors may be engaged across departments, business units, and countries. Worker classification rules differ by jurisdiction. Contracts vary by engagement. Payments must move across currencies. Finance teams need visibility into contractor spend, while legal teams require documentation that stands up to regulatory scrutiny.
This is why freelancer management has become a critical capability for modern businesses.
Freelancer management refers to the processes, tools, and practices used to manage freelancers, contractors, consultants, and other external talent throughout the entire engagement lifecycle. This includes sourcing talent, onboarding contractors, managing projects, maintaining compliance, and ensuring accurate and timely payments.
When these processes are structured correctly, organizations can scale their contingent workforce while maintaining governance and strong relationships with external specialists.
If you want a deeper look at the systems that support these processes, you can also read our guide explaining what a freelancer management system is.
Freelancer management is the process of coordinating and overseeing work delivered by freelancers, independent contractors, consultants, and other non-employee workers.
Unlike employees, freelancers typically operate as self-employed professionals. They often work with multiple clients at once, set their own schedules, and may be located in different countries or time zones.

Common challenges when managing freelancers
Freelancers offer flexibility and specialized expertise, but working with external talent introduces challenges that organizations must address.
Below are some of the most common issues businesses encounter when managing freelancers and contractors.
Freelancers often work remotely, sometimes across multiple time zones. Without clear communication practices, misunderstandings can occur more easily than in traditional office environments.
Using reliable tools such as Slack, Microsoft Teams, or Zoom helps teams stay connected.
However, communication practices matter just as much as the tools themselves.
Regular check-ins, clearly defined expectations, and structured feedback loops help ensure freelancers understand what is required and can deliver work efficiently.
