As Federal Priorities Shift, States Boost Broadband Affordability. Article October 13, 2026. The Pew Charitable Trusts. With uses from smart appliances to precision farming, the internet is increasingly woven into daily life—yet millions of Americans still lack access to high-speed service. While creating more network connections is key to expanding broadband availability, cost also remains a significant barrier: Pew Research Center has consistently found that households with lower incomes subscribe to home broadband service at lower rates than households with higher incomes do. While federal broadband programs have sought to address both infrastructure and affordability, shifting federal priorities have resulted in fewer resources to help bring service within economic reach. In recent years, two key federal programs aimed at improving broadband availability and affordability have either expired or been dramatically altered, including the Affordable Connectivity Program (ACP) and the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD) program.
