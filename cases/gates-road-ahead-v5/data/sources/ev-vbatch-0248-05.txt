Benchmarking the most promising EdTech startups from 2025. Each regional list is focused on identifying young, fast growing, and innovative learning and upskilling start-ups. To be eligible, startups are generally less than 10 years old (though there are some exceptions), are either headquartered in the region, or predominately focused on the market (e.g. > 80% revenue/customers), are pre exit (not acquired or listed) and not a subsidiary of a larger company or controlled by an investor group. The 2025 Global EdTech 1000 highlights a market still young, with K–12 continuing to play a large role and workforce learning accelerating. K–12 represents nearly 40% of this year’s cohort, driven by demand for tutoring, content, school support, and classroom infrastructure. Workforce training and development accounts for more than one-third, consistent with the global shift toward job-relevant, short-cycle learning. Post-secondary represents about 20%, and Early Childhood Education remains a small but steady segment. Subsector activity illustrates where innovation is concentrating. Early Childhood innovation remains centered on learning content, foundational skills, and parent-focused offerings.
