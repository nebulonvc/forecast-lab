In December 2004, Lenovo agreed to acquire IBM's personal-computer business in a transaction valued at US$1.75 billion, comprising US$1.25 billion in cash and shares and the assumption of US$500 million in debt. The acquisition included the ThinkPad business and transferred approximately 10,000 IBM employees to Lenovo. It also gave Lenovo access to IBM's worldwide PC sales operations and research operations in the United States and Japan, alongside its existing operations in China.The transaction received clearance from the U.S. Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) in March 2005 and was completed later that year. The acquisition made Lenovo the third-largest computer manufacturer worldwide by unit shipments.
Following completion of the IBM PC acquisition, former IBM executive Stephen Ward became Lenovo's chief executive, while Yang Yuanqing succeeded Liu as chairman. In December 2005, former Dell executive William Amelio replaced Ward as chief executive. Amelio remained in the role until February 2009, when Yang returned as chief executive and Liu returned as chairman.
Commenting on the acquisition in 2012, Liu said: "We benefited in three ways from the IBM acquisition. We got the ThinkPad brand, IBM's more advanced PC manufacturing technology and the company's international resources, such as its global sales channels and operation teams. These three elements have shored up our sales revenue in the past several years."
