The amount generated for good causes is influenced by the mix of types of Lottery products sold (“channel mix”) with a greater proportion of Draw Based Game (e.g. Lotto and EuroMillions) sales going to good causes than instant products (Scratchcards and instant Win Games) sales. Over 2025-26 there was a strong performance in EuroMillions and continued expansion of digital channels, which now account for over half of total lottery sales. EuroMillions delivered record UK sales during the year, supported by a higher number of rollovers and larger jackpots, and generated significant returns to good causes. Instant Win Games also achieved record sales, reflecting increased player engagement and the continued shift from retail to digital play. These increases were partially offset by a continued decline in Scratchcards sales, reflecting changes in retail footfall and consumer purchasing behaviour. Lotto sales were broadly stable year on year. Overall returns to the National Lottery Distribution Fund benefited not only from a small increase in sales, but also from a favourable sales mix, as EuroMillions delivers a higher return per pound to good causes and digital sales do not incur retailer commission. Interest earned on funds held within player trust and unclaimed prizes also continued to contribute to amounts payable to the NLDF.
