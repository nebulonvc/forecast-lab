WASHINGTON (February 19, 2025) — New data from the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES) show the extent to which public schools have implemented policies to limit cell phone use. According to the latest findings from the School Pulse Panel, 77 percent of public schools prohibit students from using cell phones during any class, with a higher percentage of elementary schools (86 percent) implementing such a policy compared to high/secondary schools (55 percent). With 53 percent of school leaders reporting negative impacts of cell phone use on academic performance, and even more citing negative impacts on students’ mental health and attention spans, schools are facing a critical issue. More than three-quarters of public schools (77 percent) reported that high-speed internet is available to students across all school grounds and buildings. The SPP data, collected December 6–20, 2024, came from 1,490 participating public K-12 schools from every state and the District of Columbia.
