REPORT: ‘2018 International Report on Snow & Mountain Tourism’ Blames Major Corporations and Higher Prices for Falling Skier Visits in the US Steven Agar | April 30, 2018 | Industry News
Alpine Meadows, CA. Credit: Squaw Alpine
The 10th annual 2018 International Report on Snow & Mountain Tourism, written by Laurent Vanat , is now available to download . The report is a world-renowned reference for data about ski resorts and tourism, covering 67 countries across the globe.
The latest edition paints a slightly more optimistic view of our global industry than we’ve seen in the last few years:
“It is a pleasure to notice that after three years of stagnation or decrease, the total figure of skier visits worldwide is now showing a trend upwards again,” Laurent Vanat says.
Although, before we get too far ahead of ourselves, the reason for the upward trend is the growing interest in developing markets, such as China . The western market continues to be flat.
In North America, for example, the trend for bringing individual resorts under the umbrella of major corporations with high financial expectations has, Vanat says, resulted in “ongoing rising prices, with a negative effect on the number of practicing skiers”.
The biggest problem for the US ski industry is the increasing prices, paid by a declining number of customers. The window price of daily lift ticket rose from an average of $59 a decade ago to $105 during the 2015/16 season. Average daily rates in accommodations in the western resorts also rose from an average 30% since 2009/10.
Just one North American resort in global top ten, by skier visits. Credit: Vanat
This tends to make ski less affordable, and especially for the beginners, who usually purchase daily passes, the less discounted tickets. Overall, the business model of the large US resorts summarises in getting always more money from always fewer customers. The question is to know if it will still be sustainable for a long time.
Some fascinating facts and figures from the full report :
There are an estimated 130 million skiers worldwide
67 countries have equipped outdoor snow-covered ski areas
More than a third of the 2,000+ ski resorts are in the Alps
14 indoor snow centers opened in China between 2015 and 2017
Liechtenstein, Switzerland, and Austria have the highest participation rates (35%+ of the population ski)
The Pacific Northwest had their best season on record
The Rocky Mountains had their second best season on record
Midwest had worst season since records began in 1978/79
