What is a mainframe? IBM (By Stephanie Susnjara, Michael Goodwin, Ian Smalley. Published 08 April 2024, Updated 03 September 2026). A mainframe is a large, high-performance computer designed for continuous operation and high-volume transaction processing. They remain the transactional core of industries like banking, insurance and government: according to an IBM Institute for Business Value report, 43 of the world’s top 50 banks and 8 of the top 10 payment companies rely on the mainframe as their core platform. Today, mainframes handle over 70% of transactional workloads, while accounting for less than 10% of the total IT spend.
