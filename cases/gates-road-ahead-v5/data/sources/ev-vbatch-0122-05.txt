The 2026 Common Sense Media Census: Tech and Media Use by Tweens and Teens includes updated age ranges and, for the first time, measures AI use as part of young people's overall digital media environment. We surveyed a nationally representative group of 1,211 U.S. tweens and teens (age 9 to 17) to understand how they spend their free time across today's media landscape, including time with television, online videos, short-form content, gaming, social media, and generative AI tools such as chatbots and companions. Personal smartphone ownership nearly doubles from age 9 to 10, and crosses the halfway point at age 11, a milestone that may line up with the transition to middle school. By age 17, ownership is nearly universal. TV and video, including short-form content, is the single biggest chunk of that time for both age groups, with short-form video like TikTok and YouTube Shorts now a daily habit for tweens and teens, the highest daily participation rate of any activity measured. Digital divides persist: Phone ownership is fairly even across income levels, but computer access is not, which could impact access to learning resources and opportunities.
