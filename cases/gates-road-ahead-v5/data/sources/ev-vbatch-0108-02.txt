In November 2025, US e-book sales were up 2.1% to $86.9 million, while digital audiobook revenues rose 15.8% to $107.6 million. Physical Audio revenues were down 41.9% to $500 thousand. On a year-to-date basis, e-book revenues were flat compared to the first eleven months of 2024, totaling $963.7 million. Digital audiobooks rose 2.4% to $995.3 million in revenue. Physical Audio revenues were down 33.7% to $5.5 million.

In terms of physical paper format revenues during the month of November, in the Trade (Consumer Books) category, Hardback revenues were up 7.3%, coming in at $422.7 million; Paperbacks were down 1.9%, with $294.5 million in revenue; Mass Market was down 66.5% to $3.9 million; and Special Bindings were up 9%, with $27.7 million in revenue.

Year-to-date for the first eleven months of the year. Hardback revenues were up 1.5% on a year-over-year basis, coming in at $3.4 billion; Paperbacks were down 5.6%, with $2.9 billion in revenue; Mass Market was down 26.2% to $81 million; and Special Bindings were down 0.9%, with $210.6 million in revenue.

This data comes from the American Publishers Association. AAP StatShot reports the monthly and yearly net revenue of publishing houses from U.S. sales to bookstores, wholesalers, direct to consumers, online retailers, and other channels. StatShot draws revenue data from approximately 1,300 publishers, although participation may fluctuate slightly from report to report.
