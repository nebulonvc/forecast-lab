Telecom competition roadmap: At a glance Telecom competition roadmap: At a glance Telecom competition roadmap: At a glance ( PDF - 632 KB ) Overview Affordable Internet and mobile services are essential for daily life. To improve competition, affordability, and transparency for consumers, the Government of Canada is launching a Telecom Competition Roadmap that outlines a coordinated plan of actions for 2026–2027. This comprehensive set of actions aims to expand access to competitive choices, empower consumers to get better prices, and enable investment in high-quality networks. For the first time, Canada is taking a comprehensive, national approach to telecom competition. The goal is simple: more choice, better prices, and stronger networks for Canadians. Expanding access to competitive choices Canada's telecom networks are expensive to build and maintain, and rely on access to limited infrastructure like poles, towers, and fibre lines. Because of this, a few large companies hold significant market power and it can be challenging for competitors to expand their services and offer Canadians more choice. The Government will make it easier for competitors to expand by improving access to existing infrastructure and reducing barriers to building new networks. Improving Internet competition The CRTC is requiring large players to give access to their fibre‑to‑the‑home networks to competitors, and is ensuring all players can use this access to compete. Final rates for competitor access will help competitors enter new markets quickly and offer more affordable Internet options. Better access to wireless networks Competitors rely on rules set by the CRTC to roam on incumbent networks and offer affordable cell plans. Recent improvements have reduced access costs for competitors by 75%. The CRTC will build on this progress by publishing profitability benchmarks to support fairer roaming rates. "Dig‑once" policies to reduce construction costs Coordinating fibre installation with other major projects (roads, pipelines, railways) can cut costs dramatically for companies. The Government will champion "dig‑once" practices to help companies build faster and more efficiently. Faster wireless tower approvals ISED is consulting on changes to its tower‑siting framework to speed up deployment while still ensuring community consultation. This includes a proposal to reduce review timelines from 120 days to 45 days and move to an online approval system. Accelerating CRTC decisions The CRTC will streamline its processes to respond more quickly to market changes. The Government will consider updating its policy direction or broader frameworks if needed. Faster dispute resolution The CRTC will speed up how it handles disputes between providers, using tools like mediation and administrative penalties to ensure incumbents meet their obligations.
