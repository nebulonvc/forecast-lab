Visualization Analysis & Design — Rules of Thumb (Ch 6), Tamara Munzner, Department of Computer Science, University of British Columbia. Unjustified 3D all too common, in the news and elsewhere. Depth vs power of the plane: high-ranked spatial position channels: planar spatial position – not depth! Occlusion hides information: interaction can resolve, but at cost of time and cognitive load. Perspective distortion loses information: perspective distortion interferes with all size channel encodings; power of the plane is lost! 3D vs 2D bar charts: 3D bars: very difficult to justify! – perspective distortion – occlusion; faceting into 2D almost always better choice. No unjustified 3D example: Time-series data – extruded curves: detailed comparisons impossible. Justified 3D: shape perception – benefits outweigh costs when task is shape perception for 3D spatial data – interactive navigation supports synthesis across many viewpoints.
