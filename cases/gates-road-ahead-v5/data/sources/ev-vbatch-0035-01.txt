This nationwide effort is often called the tech transition or network modernization. As part of the transition, telephone and Internet providers will retire aging copper‑line networks used to provide voice services in areas where modern fiber, mobile and fixed wireless, and satellite services are available as a replacement for both Internet and voice services. These newer technologies better meet consumers’ needs, are easier to maintain, and provide a number of benefits, including: Enhanced robocall protections Next generation 911 (NG911) capabilities Improved reliability Access to telehealth and other digital tools The vast majority of American households have already upgraded their services and are wireless-only or wireless combined with an over-the-top Internet-based home phone, commonly referred to as Voice-over-Internet Protocol, or VoIP, service. VoIP services work with just about any Internet connection. Regardless of the technology you use for phone service, FCC rules ensure you will have access to 911 and accessible relay communications services. Household Connections: By the Numbers 76% of adults live in wireless-only households. 79% of all fixed home voice connections are VoIP. 5% of households in the United States have legacy copper telephone service, and many of these also have active mobile service.
