In a fragmented media environment with seemingly endless sources of information to choose from, Americans’ news habits have changed dramatically in the 21st century. Today, an overwhelming majority of U.S. adults at least sometimes get news from digital devices. This fact sheet looks at the platforms they turn to for news, including patterns and trends over time.
This Pew Research Center fact sheet looks at trends in how people get news from different traditional and digital platforms.
We conducted this research to understand where Americans get their news, which sources they prefer and how these habits vary across demographic groups and over time.
For this analysis, we surveyed 5,166 U.S. adults from Aug. 24 to 30, 2026. Everyone who took part in these surveys is a member of the Center’s American Trends Panel . The surveys represent the views of the full U.S. adult population.
Here are the questions used for this analysis , the topline and the survey methodology .
News consumption across all platforms
A large majority of U.S. adults (88%) say they at least sometimes get news from digital devices like a smartphone, computer or tablet. This includes 59% who say they do so often – up slightly from 2025, when 56% of Americans said they often get news from digital devices.
Digital devices are by far the most common way Americans get news, although a majority (63%) also get news from television at least sometimes. This includes 31% who say they often get news from TV, a share that has stayed fairly steady over the last few years.
Americans turn to radio and print publications for news less frequently. As of 2026, 12% of U.S. adults say they often get news from radio, and 6% say the same about printed newspapers or magazines. Both of these shares are roughly the same as last year.

News consumption across digital platforms
There are several different pathways Americans use to get news on their digital devices. News websites or apps, social media , and search engines are the most common: More than half of U.S. adults say they at least sometimes get news in each of these ways. The shares of Americans who get news from social media and search have both increased since 2025.
About a third of Americans say they often or sometimes get news from email newsletters (34%) or podcasts (32%).
