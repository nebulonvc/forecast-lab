Homebound: The Long-Term Rise in Time Spent at Home Among U.S. Adults. Patrick Sharkey, Princeton School of Public and International Affairs. Citation: Sharkey, Patrick. 2024. Sociological Science 11: 553-578. Published: August 2, 2024. This article analyzes data from the American Time Use Survey over a timeframe spanning nineteen years and argues that the changes in Americans’ daily routines induced by the COVID era should be seen as an acceleration of a longer-term trend: the rise of time spent at home. Results show that from 2003 to 2022, average time spent at home among American adults has risen by one hour and 39 minutes in a typical day. Time at home has risen for every subset of the population and for virtually all activities. Young adults age 15-24 spent 57 minutes more time at home per day in 2019 as compared with 2003, and by 2022 this group spent 124 more minutes at home relative to 2003. All age groups, however, spent more than an hour longer at home in 2022 than in 2003. The figure shows that U.S. adults spent less time shopping for consumer items, socializing, volunteering, and traveling (including commuting and all transportation) in 2022 when compared with 2003. All of these activities are more likely to take part outside the home, and thus the decline in time spent in each activity results in an overall drop in time spent outside the home.
