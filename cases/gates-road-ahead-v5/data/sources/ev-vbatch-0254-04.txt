By embracing a phased rollout, strong faculty partnerships, and strategic funding, Carroll Community College created an accessible virtual reality lab that enhances teaching and learning. Carroll Community College’s Virtual Reality (VR) Lab began with a straightforward goal to use immersive technology to make learning more engaging, experiential and accessible. Through a thoughtful, multi-year approach, that goal has evolved into a robust VR lab that supports instruction across disciplines while demonstrating that community colleges can successfully launch and scale VR without significant upfront investment. This article was originally published in Community College Journal, published by the American Association of Community Colleges. VR enables students to practice, explore and learn in ways that are difficult or impossible in traditional classrooms, all within a safe, controlled environment. At Carroll, medical and industry simulations allow students to rehearse procedures and technical skills without real-world risk. Soft-skill scenarios, such as public speaking and conflict resolution, help students build confidence through realistic practice. In STEM fields, VR supports learning with interactive 3D models and virtual labs that deepen understanding in engineering and science. The humanities also benefit from immersive learning. Students can experience history and art in new ways, such as virtually witnessing the 1943 Berlin Blitz or stepping inside Vincent van Gogh’s “Starry Night.” Rather than making a large initial investment, Carroll started small. In late 2020, the college piloted VR with just two headsets to assess interest and effectiveness. Positive feedback from students and faculty guided gradual expansion. A key catalyst was the use of federal Higher Education Emergency Relief Fund (HEERF) dollars during Covid, which enabled the purchase of additional hardware and VR ready computers.
