Many public school teachers in the United States are dissatisfied with their pay . That may help explain why a sizable share of them have a second job – and not just in the summertime. For the 2020-21 school year – the most recent year with available data – 16% of full-time public elementary and secondary school teachers in the U.S. worked a nonschool job over the summer. A similar share (17%) did so during the school year , according to data from the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES). These shares are much higher than the 4.6% of all U.S. workers ages 20 and older who worked multiple jobs in 2020 and 2021, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) averages. How we did this With school out for the summer, Pew Research Center conducted this analysis to explore how many teachers supplement their income with nonschool jobs during the summer, as well as during the school year. The data for this analysis primarily comes from the National Center for Education Statistics . It includes regular full-time elementary and secondary school teachers at public schools only. It excludes other staff – such as long-term substitutes, itinerant teachers, administrators and support staff – even if they have full-time teaching duties. This analysis also excludes teachers at private schools. This analysis also includes data on the share of all workers ages 20 and older who have multiple jobs from the Bureau of Labor Statistics .
