Beyond Anthropomorphism: a Spectrum of Interface Metaphors for LLMs. Anthropomorphizing conversational technology is a natural human tendency. Today, the anthropomorphic metaphor is overly reinforced across intelligent tools. Large Language Models (LLMs) are particularly anthropomorphized through interface design. While metaphors are inherently partial, anthropomorphic interfaces highlight similarities between LLMs and humans, but mask crucial differences. As a result, the metaphor is often taken literally; users treat LLMs as if they are truly human. With few safeguards in place, this extreme anthropomorphism drives users to delusion and harm. Users also experience dissonance between the ethics of using LLMs, their growing ubiquity, and limited interface alternatives. We introduce a spectrum of metaphors from transparency-driven ''anti-anthropomorphism'' to uncanny ''hyper-anthropomorphism''.
