Ada Karina Izaguirre (aizaguirre@worldbank.org) is an infrastructure specialist with the World Bank’s Infrastructure Economics and Finance Department. Private activity in telecommunications in lowand middle-income countries remained subdued in 2003.1 Investment flows to projects with private participation declined for the third consecutive year, amounting to US$28.5 billion (table 1).2 Nevertheless, 22 new projects with fixed or mobile operators reached financial closure in 16 developing countries. Most of the decline in investment occurred in lower-middle-income countries, where investment flows dropped from US$11.3 billion to US$8.8 billion. Low-income countries also contributed to the slowdown, with investment flows falling from US$6.8 billion to US$5.4 billion. Flows were nevertheless strong, the fourth highest for this group in 1990–2003. Investment flows to upper-middleincome countries grew by 9 percent to US$14.2 billion, yet were still close to the level of 1996. Regional trends Investment flows grew in the Middle East and North Africa and in Europe and Central Asia while declining in the other developing regions (see table 1). The Middle East and North Africa saw the region’s highest investment flows in 1990–2003, more than US$5 billion, driven mainly by the partial divestiture of Saudi Telecom.
