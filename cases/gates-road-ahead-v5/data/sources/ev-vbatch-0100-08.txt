a PDF of the paper titled Synthetic Doubt and the Integrity of Digital Proof before the International Criminal Court, by Seyed Mohammad Sadegh Dashti View PDF Abstract: Generative artificial intelligence complicates evidentiary assessment at the International Criminal Court by enabling fabricated audiovisual material and making authentic digital evidence easier to dismiss as fake. This article calls the latter problem synthetic doubt: an objection alleging that a digital item may be AI-generated or AI-manipulated. Existing scholarship addresses digital evidence, open-source investigations and deepfakes in the ICC context. Yet the question of when synthetic doubt should trigger enhanced reliability review under the Rome Statute, without displacing the prosecution's burden under article 66 or compromising article 67 fair-trial rights, remains underdeveloped. The article proposes a three-stage framework: a baseline authenticity and provenance showing by the proponent; a case-specific articulation threshold for the objector; and proportionate reliability review by the Chamber under articles 64 and 69(4). The framework treats AI-related provenance information and synthetic-media detector outputs as contextual rather than dispositive.
