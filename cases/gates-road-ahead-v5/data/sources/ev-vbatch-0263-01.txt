Steam has once again broken its all-time record for concurrent users, reaching an utterly extraordinary 43,505,724 people logged in at the same time. As Eurogamer reports, picking up the numbers from SteamDB, this marks the third time this year that the top figure has been smashed, hitting over 42 million back in January, adding another half-million in March, and now breaking through the 43 million barrier for the first time in the service’s 23 years. It’s important to note that this isn’t the number of people playing games on Steam simultaneously, but rather the numbers logged in at once. However, SteamDB records that 14.5 million actually were in-game when the record was broken. That too is a record, by an extraordinary half a million more people. Clearly the release of Gears of War: E-Day will have helped, with around 170,000 sales on Steam, while free-to-play MMO Aion 2 has seen almost 400,000 players showing up all week. They join the usual suspects like Dota 2 and Counter-Strike 2, which routinely see player counts of over a million, and PUGB with its regular 700,000. Of course, it’s important to add some perspective. Roblox reported 123 million daily active users across all its many games in the second quarter of 2026 (which is in fact significantly down from Q3 2025’s 152 million), still demolishing Steam’s record by almost triple the number of people. And for a single game, Fortnite alone matched Steam’s all-time highest number of in-game players across the tens of thousands of games, with an all-time peak of 14.3 million players in 2024.
