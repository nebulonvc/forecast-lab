February 11, 2026 DEG: The Digital Entertainment Group today released its Year-End 2025 Digital Media Entertainment Report compiled by DEG members, tracking sources and retail/platform input.1 U.S. Consumer Spending on Digital Entertainment Consumed at Home and on the Go Grew by Almost $10 Billion in 2025, Surpassing $62 Billion Subscription streaming grew near 20% for the year, while smaller fourth quarter releases made a tough comparison for digital transactions Premium 4K UHD buoyed physical formats Overall U.S. consumer spending across digital and physical home entertainment formats in 2025 was $62.2 billion, a 17.4 percent increase from the almost $53 billion that consumers spent in 2024, driven by continued strong gains in subscription streaming with an assist from digital transactions2 early in the year. In the year’s final quarter, overall consumer spending rose almost 19 percent, representing $16.7 billion. Consumers spent more than $61.3 billion on digital entertainment purchases (EST2), rentals (VOD2) and subscriptions for the full year, a jump of 17.9 percent over full year 2024; and almost $16.5 billion for the fourth quarter, up 19.5 percent. Among the highlights for the Fourth Quarter and Full Year 2025: • Spending on subscription streaming in the aggregate rose 19.8 percent for full year 2025, reaching $57.5 billion, and accounting for more than 92 percent of all U.S. consumer spending on home entertainment. The highest growth rate came from consumer spending on hybrid tiers3 (subscription fees plus ads), which grew 60.9 percent, to $10.3 billion for the full year, while the much larger pure subscription (SVOD4) segment (with no ads) grew more slowly, at 13.4 percent, to $47.2 billion. In the fourth quarter, subscription streaming in the aggregate increased 22.8%, to $15.6 billion, with spending on hybrid tiers up 41.5 percent to $2.9 billion, and pure subscription spending up 19.2 percent to $12.7 billion. • For the year, total spending on digital transactions (including all VOD and EST, but excluding subscription streaming) showed resilience, falling about 4 percent for the full year, to almost $3.9 billion. In the fourth quarter, spending on digital transactions fell 20.2 percent, to $843 million, over-indexing on a 45 percent drop in box-office spending5 on titles released to the home. • Theatrical new releases are historically a key driver of transactional home entertainment spending and softness in this area contributed to a fourth quarter decline in EST spending of 24.3 percent. Consumer spending on purchases of theatrical and TV titles declined more than 25 percent in the fourth quarter due to tough comparisons with titles released in the year-earlier period, which included two $600 million-plus theatrical hits: Disney’s Deadpool and Wolverine and Inside Out 2, and the TV hit Yellowstone Season 5 Part 2, from Paramount. This dragged consumer spending on EST to a decline of 3.3 percent for the full year, despite robust growth of almost 17 percent in the first quarter and positive third quarter growth. • For the full year, spending on VOD fell a more moderate 4.7 percent, to $1.6 billion, with a lesser 2.4 percent drop in spending on theatrical titles as consumers continued to embrace feature-length theatricals released for home viewing in the premium window. In the fourth quarter, spending on digital rentals fell 14.5 percent, also as a result of the weaker release slate. • Among the fourth quarter’s best-performing titles released by DEG member companies across all transactional formats, were Black Phone 2 (Universal); Caught Stealing (Sony); The Conjuring: Last Rites (WBD); The Fantastic Four: First Steps (Disney); Good Fortune (Lionsgate); Jurassic World: Rebirth (Universal); The Long Walk (Lionsgate); Mission Impossible: The Final Reckoning (Paramount); Nuremberg (Sony, 2025); One Battle After Another (WBD); Roofman (Paramount); and Tron: Ares (Disney). • Spending on all physical formats6, including DVD, Blu-ray and 4K UHD showed some resilience, dropping less than 10 percent for full year 2025 and the fourth quarter.
