Cisco's workhorse cable modem termination system, the cBR-8, will be put out to pasture in the coming years, a move that will cause some cable ops to shift to a virtual platform or another alternative.
The future of the cable access network is trending away from legacy, chassis-based cable modem termination systems (CMTSs) to power- and space-saving virtualized platforms, but operators are embracing those platforms at different paces.
Comcast, for example, is aggressively migrating to a vCMTS, while others such as Spectrum (the newly combined Cox Communications and Charter Communications), Videotron and Mediacom Communications have made vendor selections and are in or nearing the deployment process.
Meanwhile, a subset of cable operators globally that still rely on Cisco's flagship integrated CMTS, the cBR-8, are facing some important questions as Cisco begins to phase that product out: Should they migrate to a virtual CMTS, try to squeeze more out of their cBR-8s, or even make a leap to passive optical networking (PON)?
Cisco, which effectively backed away from selling nodes, amps and other cable network hardware years ago, is in the process of shutting down support for the cBR-8, the workhorse, chassis-based CMTS that originally carried the code name "Battlestar." Cisco debuted the cBR-8 more than a decade ago as cable operators started to push toward multi-gigabit speeds over the DOCSIS network. As a converged cable access platform, or CCAP, the cBR-8 also supports cable video services.
Cisco disclosed in April 2025 that it had begun the end-of-life (EOL) process for the cBR-8 , which succeeded the uBR10012, a CMTS that reached obsolescence in May 2017. Operators could no longer purchase the cBR-8 directly from Cisco as of April 11, 2026, with July 11, 2026, designated as the last ship date for the product. But the cBR-8 can still be purchased (new or used/refurbished) on secondary markets. For instance, there's one for sale on eBay for $47,900 (or best offer), and CompuDevices lists one for $27,724.81.
In its heyday, Cisco commanded a sizable piece of the CMTS market. Some operators such as Cox Communications were almost exclusively on the Cisco platform for both integrated CMTS and distributed access architecture (DAA) upgrades that implemented remote PHY devices (RPDs). Cisco was once deep into development of a virtual CMTS but eventually mothballed the project amid its general exit from the cable tech business.
