In 2024 – the latest year for which complete data are available, owing to the delay between application and publication – computer technology remained the most frequently featured technology in published patent applications worldwide, accounting for 13.6% of the world total (table A17). It was followed by electrical machinery (7.5%), measurement (6.5%), digital communication (5.6%) and medical technology (4.6%). These five fields have consistently ranked among the top five for over a decade, though their relative positions have shifted over time. Together, their share of total filings has risen from 29.8% in 2014 to 37.7% in 2024, driven primarily by a rapid growth in computer-related technologies. Computer technology alone recorded an average annual growth rate of 9.5% between 2014 and 2024 – significantly outpacing the other four fields. It is notable that, unlike previously, no field of technology recorded double-digit average annual growth over this period. Computer technology (+9.5%) and IT methods for management (+9.0%) grew fastest, followed by chemical engineering (+7.0%) and measurement (+6.9%). At the other end of the spectrum, filings declined in several fields, most sharply in food chemistry (–4.2%) and engines, pumps and turbines (–2.2%). Overall, European countries tend to specialize more in transport-related technologies, whereas Asian countries and the US show stronger specialization in computer technology and digital communication. The number of published patent applications related to green technologies has increased steadily, from around 244,200 in 2010 to approximately 612,600 in 2024 (figure A19), representing an increase of 8.8% on the previous year.
