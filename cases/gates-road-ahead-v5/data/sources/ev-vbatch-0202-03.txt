Despite the rise of AI chatbots and the growing amount of real estate information available online, home buyers and sellers are still turning to real estate agents for guidance when it’s time to make a move—even more than they used to.

A record high—92%—of home sellers worked with a real estate agent while 90% of home buyers also purchased their home through a real estate agent or broker over the past year, according to the National Association of REALTORS®’ newly released 2026 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers , based on responses of buyers and sellers who completed transactions between July 2025 and June 2026.

“More buyers are using agents than ever before,” says Jessica Lautz, NAR’s deputy chief economist. “This might seem counterintuitive given consumers have access to more information than ever before. However, all that information can be misleading without proper context. When executing one of the largest financial transactions of most people’s lives, consumers need localized expertise from agents to give buyers and sellers personalized knowledge.”

The number of homeowners trying to sell their home themselves—known as For Sale By Owners—matched last year’s all-time low of just 5%, NAR’s research shows.

The FSBO market share has been gradually shrinking over the last four decades, even as technological advances have entered more aspects of a real estate transaction. In 1985, FSBO sales peaked, comprising 21% of home sales. The FSBO share has stayed mostly in the single digits since 2010.

Thirty-seven percent of FSBO sellers said the main reason why they sold on their own was because they didn’t want to pay an agent commission or fee. Yet, FSBO transactions tended to fall short on the final sales price compared to sellers who work with a real estate agent, data shows.

Over the past year, the median price for a FSBO sale was $368,500 compared with $455,000 for agent-assisted sales—a nearly 24% difference in favor of agent-assisted homes. The lower price point for FSBO sales also may reflect that they tend to be lower-cost mobile or manufactured homes or homes located in rural areas.

Navigating the sales process on a seller’s own can prove challenging, the survey finds. FSBO sellers most often said they struggled with preparing the home for sale, selling within their desired timeframe, pricing the home and understanding the paperwork, according to NAR’s survey. Notably, 41% of FSBO sellers did not actively market their home.
