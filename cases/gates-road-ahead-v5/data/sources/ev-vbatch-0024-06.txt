Pros & Cons Netflix Watch Now Pros & Cons Netflix Watch Now Specs Final Thoughts Click here to visit Netflix Watch Now It took a while—longer than expected, in fact—but Netflix has finally done it: The company that revolutionized movie rentals eight years ago has finally taken the next logical step, attacking the video-on-demand (VoD) market with its new Watch Now service. With iTunes, Starz's Vongo, and Amazon's Unbox having already staked their claims, it's about time. We're all familiar with Netflix, and, given its subscriber base of some 6.3 million, there's a decent chance many of you regularly receive the now-familiar red envelopes in the mail. Launched in 1998, the service has done for movie rentals what Amazon and Apple did for online booksellers and music stores, respectively. Netflix's straightforward business model of DVD rentals by mail caught the industry, and specifically mega-chain Blockbuster, off-guard. Blockbuster launched a competing service called Online Total Access, but has managed to secure only a third of Netflix's user base. Netflix, meanwhile, isn't the first to offer VoD, but it has managed to beat its main competitor to the punch again.— next: The Feature Presentation > The Feature Presentation Netflix is rolling out Watch Now with little fanfare. The company is offering the feature for free to its current subscriber base. The number of hours you can watch a month is equal to the cost of your subscription level. Pay $17.95 a month for the three-at-a-time plan (Netflix's most popular, incidentally) and you're allotted 18 hours of viewing time a month, which translates to roughly nine titles. Watch Now is also pretty forgiving with billing. Instead of charging you for a full movie every time you begin watching a new title, the service charges you by the minute. If you decide you don't like a movie a few minutes in, just hop to another, and only the minutes you actually spent watching the stinker will be subtracted from your account. Netflix compares the experience to channel surfing, which seems pretty apt. It also seems like one of the more sound VoD models for today's world. Customers who've got the audience ADD that comes from endless flipping through 500 cable TV channels will most likely appreciate the flexibility. Service installation requires the download of a simple Windows Media–based plug-in. Mac, Linux, Firefox, and Opera fans are all out of luck: You can watch Watch Now only if you're using Internet Explorer on Windows XP or Vista. Netflix has announced plans to increase the number of available platforms (including those mentioned above and some more surprising ones, such as cell phones), but isn't talking dates yet. Also, thanks to rights restrictions, you may be required to sign in to the service multiple times. The player's interface is fairly slick, with all the necessary controls: play/pause, volume, an adjustable scroll bar for fast-forward/rewinding, and a button that toggles to and from full-screen to an in-browser view. The program automatically adjusts picture quality based on your connection speed. The three speeds are Basic, Good, and High. Basic speed starts at 500 Kbps, connections between 1.0 and 1.6 Mbps are rated as Good, and anything above that qualifies as High. The emphasis is on consistent streaming, though you'll pay in picture quality at low speeds. I was able to start watching movies in under 30 seconds in a variety of settings, including a high-speed LAN connection and the far-less-than-optimal Wi-Fi network I'm on at home. The picture quality at a lower speed was watchable but far from ideal. At the higher speed, it was surprisingly clear, though not up to DVD quality. Unfortunately, despite trying the service on multiple systems with highspeed connections, we were unable to achieve the high quality setting. Watch Now buffers content as you watch. In some cases, I was able to jump to any point in the film almost immediately. At other times, it took close to 20 seconds to resume viewing, if the video stream hadn't yet buffered to that point in the film. Even with a less-than-optimal connection, the experience beats the prospect of downloading a whole movie at a time—and paying for it, even if you don't finish it. Watch Now is currently undergoing an incremental rollout. According to a Netflix representative, the company is introducing it slowly, in order to eliminate potential strain on its servers. The feature will be introduced to existing customers at a rate of around a million users a month. With Netflix's current subscriber base, the rollout should be completed around June. Watch Now is being introduced into accounts at random, regardless of members' subscription plans or the amount of time that they've been with the service. When the feature is added to their account, users receive an e-mail notification, and a small Watch Now tab is added to the top of their account page.— next: What to Watch Now? > What to Watch Now?
