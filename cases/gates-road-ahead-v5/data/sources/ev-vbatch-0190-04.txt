Local news deserts skyrocket as newspaper closures escalate, even as startups emerge, Medill study shows (2026). EVANSTON, ILL. -- The number of local news desert counties in the U.S. shot up by 26 in the last year, the largest one-year rise in at least five years, according to the Medill State of Local News Report 2026. There are now 238 news deserts in the U.S., as the pace of newspaper closures hit an average of about three per week. Exacerbating the challenge is a precipitous 50% drop in digital traffic to local newspapers’ websites, coinciding with growing consumer use of AI platforms, the study shows. Despite that, the report tracked the creation of 75 digital-only and print local news startups in the last year. Key findings from the Medill study: The number of news desert counties, those with no locally based professional news source, jumped by 26 in the last year to 238. Twenty years ago, about 150 of the nation’s 3,144 counties were news deserts. Another nearly 1,500 counties have just one source of local news remaining, leaving 47 million Americans with limited to no access to local news. In all, 170 newspapers shuttered or were merged, most of them in rural areas.
