What does the SITA 2026 Passenger IT Insights report reveal about air travel stress?
Air travel stress is suppressing demand. SITA found that 63% of passengers feel anxious during their journey and 42% have avoided booking a trip because they expected it to be too stressful. This rises to 56% among travelers under 30 and 61% among frequent flyers.
Can airport technology reduce passenger anxiety?
Greater technology use is consistently associated with lower passenger anxiety. Booking is the most digitized and calmest stage: 91% use technology and only 12% report negative emotions. Border control is less digitized, at 59%, and the most stressful, with 37% reporting negative emotions. This is correlation, not proof of causation, because crowding, waiting and other factors also affect stress.
How does passenger stress affect airline bookings and airport spending?
Passenger stress costs airlines bookings and limits airport retail revenue. Overall, 42% of passengers have avoided a trip because of anticipated stress. Meanwhile, 37% say a less stressful airport journey would encourage them to spend more in shops and restaurants, rising to 45% among frequent flyers.
Which air travel problems should airlines and airports fix first?
SITA’s research points to delays and cancellations, long waiting times, and baggage handling as the leading priorities. These are among passengers’ biggest frustrations, and 65% of those affected believe technology could reduce long waits and baggage mishandling. Key solutions include faster rebooking, timely disruption updates, queue management and real-time baggage tracking.
Do passengers trust AI in air travel?
Passengers trust AI more as an assistant than as a decision-maker. AI ranks first for potential but last for trust among the five technologies assessed. Travelers are most comfortable using it to find and book flights, provide real-time updates and answer pre-trip questions. Only 27% would let AI handle a complaint or compensation claim, although just 14% reject every proposed AI use.
Which travel technologies do passengers trust most?
Digital identity and biometrics earn the most passenger trust. Digital identity ranks first, with 50% preferring their national government to issue and manage it. Biometrics ranks second, with 65–67% comfortable using it at major checkpoints.
