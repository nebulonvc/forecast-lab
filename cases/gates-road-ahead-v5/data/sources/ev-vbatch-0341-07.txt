## Nearly 4 in 10 U.S. adults – including about half of those 18 to 29 – are part of an online community

[![An illustration of icons related to online communities connected in a network](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/10/PI_2026.10.14_online-communities_Feature_image.png?w=640)](https://www.pewresearch.org/?attachment_id=694959)

**About this research**

This report from the [Pew-Knight Initiative](https://www.pewresearch.org/collections/pew-knight-initiative/) focuses on online community and connection today. It looks at who’s part of online communities, where they form, what they’re built around, why people go to them – and why some people leave. It also examines what people think the internet is doing to connection overall.

#### Why we did this

The Center conducts research to inform the public, journalists and decision-makers. We’ve long studied [what online connection looks like for Americans](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2001/10/31/online-communities/). At a time when fewer than half of Americans are highly satisfied with their social life – or with the quality of life in their local community – it’s important to understand the role online communities play, good and bad.

Where online communities form and what they’re about

[![A bar chart showing Social media top the list of where people find community online](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/10/PI_2026.10.14_online-communities_0-02.png?w=310){.alignright width=280}](https://www.pewresearch.org/?attachment_id=692000)

More than two decades after the Center [studied online communities](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2001/10/31/online-communities/) in 2001, these spaces still vary in where they form, what they offer and who turns to them.

**It’s most common to find online community on social media.** About a quarter of Americans are part of an online community on social media sites or apps.

Discussion forums come next – 15% are part of an online community in places like Reddit, Nextdoor or Discord.

Fewer are part of an online community on video sites like YouTube and Twitch, in video games online, or on messaging apps.

And 5% are part of one on sites or apps for people who share a hobby – like Goodreads for reading, Strava for running, Ravelry for knitting or Letterboxd for movies.
