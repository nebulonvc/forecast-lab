The internet represents a fundamental shift in how Americans connect with one another, gather information and conduct their day-to-day lives. For more than 25 years, Pew Research Center has documented the internet’s growth and distribution in the United States. Explore the patterns of internet and home broadband adoption below. 
About this research 
This fact sheet is the latest in our long-running series on technology adoption in the United States. For two decades, we’ve surveyed Americans on subjects like who subscribes to broadband – also known as high-speed internet – at home, who has a smartphone, how often people go online, and whether they rely on smartphones for internet. 
Why did we do this? Pew Research Center does research to inform the public, journalists and decision-makers. This research builds on our past work studying technology adoption and the digital divide in the United States. 
Learn more about Pew Research Center . 
How did we do this? This analysis is based on findings from the National Public Opinion Reference Survey, a Center survey of 5,511 U.S. adults. We allowed people to respond online, by mail or by phone. The survey represents the views of all U.S. adults. 
The survey was conducted Jan. 30-June 18, 2026. Its overall margin of error is plus or minus 1.8 percentage points. 
This analysis also includes past data. The ways we’ve allowed people to respond to these surveys have evolved over the years, including in 2023 and again in 2024. For more on these shifts and how we approached them, read our mobile and social media fact sheets and our Q&A . 
Here are the questions used for this analysis , the topline and the survey methodology . 
Internet use over time 
When the Center began asking Americans about their internet usage in early 2000, about half of all adults were already online. Today, 97% of U.S. adults say they use the internet.

Home broadband use over time 
The share of U.S. adults with high-speed broadband service at home increased rapidly between 2000 and 2010. And that growth continues today: 78% of U.S. adults now say they subscribe to a broadband internet service at home.
