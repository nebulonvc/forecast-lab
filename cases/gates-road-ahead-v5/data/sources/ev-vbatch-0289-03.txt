Large shares of Americans are connected to the world of digital information while on the go via smartphones and other mobile devices. This analysis is based on findings from the National Public Opinion Reference Survey, a Center survey of 5,511 U.S. adults. The survey was conducted Jan. 30-June 18, 2026. The vast majority of U.S. adults (98%) own a cellphone of some kind. About nine-in-ten (91%) own a smartphone, up from just 35% in the Center’s first survey of smartphone ownership conducted in 2011. Today, 16% of U.S. adults are “smartphone-only” internet users, meaning they own a smartphone but do not subscribe to a home broadband service. Reliance on smartphones for online access is especially common among Americans with lower household incomes and those with less formal education.
